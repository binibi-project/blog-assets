Where to Eat in Jeonju Hanok Village: 3 Real Picks

Quick answer
  • Three sit-down or line-up stops, not the nearest cart: Waengijip for kongnamul gukbap, Hankookjip for bibimbap, PNB for the choco pie people queue for.
  • Prices run ₩2,300-17,000, about $1.70-$12.70 at ~₩1,336/$1, and every one of the three has been doing this one thing for years, not months.
  • Before you go: skip Hankookjip on a Friday and go to Waengijip before noon if you want a table without a wait.

Most visitors to Jeonju Hanok Village eat standing up, at whichever cart has the longest line and a sign in English, and call that Jeonju food. It isn't, not really. The dishes this city is actually known for are eaten sitting down, off tableware that's been at the same address for decades, and none of them need a translated menu to be good.

By mid-morning the alleys off Eojin-gil already smell like broth and toasted sesame oil before the pastry shops even open their doors. The three stops below are a short walk from each other and from Gyeonggijeon, the royal portrait hall that most people use as their mental map of the village. None of them are hidden. They're just easy to walk past if you're only looking for the shortest line.

What is Jeonju Hanok Village?

A cluster of roughly 700 traditional hanok houses in Jeonju's old town, the largest concentration of urban hanok in Korea, which began taking its current shape around 1910. Gyeonggijeon, the shrine holding a portrait of the Joseon dynasty's founder, sits at its center and gives most visitors their starting point.

🍲 Waengijip: the soup that's kept one line moving for decades

Order the ₩9,000 bowl and nothing else; this shop has done one dish long enough to earn a "century-old store" listing. Waengijip sits at 전북 전주시 완산구 동문길 88, near the Dongmun intersection a short walk from the hanok village core, and the official tourism listing for it opens with one line: it built its name over decades on bean-sprout soup alone, nothing else on the menu carrying the shop.

It's open 07:00 to 20:30 every day with no afternoon break, which is rare for a Jeonju kitchen this busy. The bowl arrives still boiling, anchovy broth over rice with a raw egg cracked in, crunchy pesticide-free bean sprouts, and aged kimchi and seafood folded into the stock. A squid add-on is ₩3,000, a kids' bowl is ₩7,000, and moju, the mild spiced rice wine locals drink alongside it, runs ₩2,000-10,000 by size. Order it with "콩나물국밥 하나 주세요" (kong-na-mul guk-bap hana ju-se-yo, "one bean-sprout soup, please").

What does "백년가게" (century-old store) mean?

A government designation for small businesses that have run the same trade for decades and passed a screening on food safety and record-keeping. It isn't a guarantee of taste, but it does mean the shop isn't going anywhere next year.

Jeonju has no subway, so there's no station or exit to give here. From Gyeonggijeon's main gate, Waengijip is roughly a 10-minute walk east toward Dongmun. Whether an English menu exists is not confirmed; screenshot the Korean name before you leave your hotel.

🍚 Is Hankookjip's bibimbap worth the sit-down price?

Yes, if you want the version served on rice that's already been warmed by a hot stone bowl, not the one from a cart. Hankookjip is at 전북 전주시 완산구 어진길 119, close enough to Gyeonggijeon that you can see the hanok rooflines from its door, open 09:50 to 20:50 with last order at 20:10, and closed on Fridays.

The signature 전주비빔밥 (Jeonju bibimbap) is ₩15,000, the same price as the 돌솥비빔밥 (stone-pot version), and the beef-tartare 육회비빔밥 runs ₩17,000. That's a sit-down price, closer to a Seoul lunch set than a street snack, and it buys a full table setting with a dozen small side dishes, not a paper cup. Order with "전주비빔밥 하나 주세요" (Jeon-ju bi-bim-bap hana ju-se-yo).

On paper Hankookjip looks like the expensive stop of the three, but it's the only one with a full sit-down meal and a dozen banchan included in that price.

🍫 PNB: the choco pie queue at the edge of the village

A single original choco pie is ₩2,300, and the shop selling it, PNB's Hanok Village 1st branch, is a different business from the mass-market boxes sold at any convenience store abroad. The branch is at 전주시 완산구 은행로 61 (교동), open 08:30 to 21:30 daily, and PNB's own store list confirms it as the chain's third hanok-village location, separate from its 1951-era original bakery elsewhere in the city.

The math checks out from PNB's own online shop too: a ten-piece box sells for ₩23,000, exactly ₩2,300 a piece. Ask for one with "오리지널 초코파이 하나 주세요" (o-ri-ji-neol cho-ko-pa-i hana ju-se-yo). The shop bakes the marshmallow-and-chocolate pastry fresh through the day rather than shipping it in, which is the entire reason people wait in line for something they could buy in a supermarket at home.

How is this different from the Orion choco pie sold abroad?

Orion's boxed choco pie, sold worldwide, is a shelf-stable snack cake. Jeonju's bakery version is made fresh in-house the same day, larger, and sold as a treat rather than a packaged snack, which is why locals treat the two as barely related.

Whether the Hanok Village branch carries an English menu is not confirmed. The register staff are used to tourists pointing at the case, so it rarely matters.

My take I've been to Jeonju in the last two years, on a trip built around a different city's food entirely. I eat out a lot in Gangnam and Samseong, including places with a Michelin sticker on the door, and a ₩15,000 sit-down bibimbap in Jeonju still reads as a bargain against that. If I only had one stop to make on a short visit, I'd sit down for the bibimbap before I stood in the choco pie line. A full meal beats a pastry when you've only got one slot in the day.

Check before you go
  • Skip Hankookjip on Friday: it's the one day of the week the doors stay shut.
  • Go to Waengijip before noon: it has no break time, but the line still builds fast around lunch.
  • Screenshot the Korean names: none of the three has a confirmed English menu.
FAQ
Is Waengijip only good for breakfast?
No. It's open 07:00-20:30 every day with no break, so it works for lunch or an early dinner as well as a hangover breakfast.
Do I need a reservation for Hankookjip?
Not usually, but it closes Fridays and takes its last order at 20:10, so a late dinner plan can miss it entirely.

None of these three needed a blog post to stay busy; locals never stopped going. Start with whichever is closest to wherever your day already has you standing, and save the choco pie for last since it travels well in a paper bag back to your hotel.
