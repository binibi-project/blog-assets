You land, the seatbelt sign clicks off, and forty other phones light up around you before yours even finds a signal. Somewhere behind customs a message from home is waiting, a rideshare app needs a working number, and the map to your hotel is sitting in a phone that currently talks to nobody. That gap between wheels-down and actually being reachable is smaller than it used to be, and almost entirely a matter of which small plastic card, or which invisible one, you picked before you got here.

What is an eSIM?

A SIM built into the phone itself instead of a physical card you insert. You activate it by scanning a QR code the carrier sends you, so there's no card to lose and, on plans built for visitors, often no number to memorize either; some are pure data.

🎫 What does a Korean SIM or eSIM actually cost?

Two different products answer two different needs, and the price gap between them is the point. KT sells a short-stay data pass called the Korea Tour Pack, built for a single trip: a data-only plan for ₩6,600 covering one day (3GB at full speed, then unlimited at a throttled rate), or ₩27,500 for five days, available as either a physical SIM or an eSIM. Stay five days or longer on this plan and KT throws in a free T-money transit card.

If you actually want a Korean phone number, that's a separate product: KT's Welcome Prepaid, a registered 30-day SIM with 200 minutes of calls and 200 texts included, priced by how much data comes with it. It runs ₩35,000 for 1GB, ₩45,000 for 3GB, or ₩55,000 for 5GB, each throttled rather than cut off once the data runs out. It's sold at more than 3,000 KT shops and Plazas nationwide, or online at kt.com.

Think about whether anyone needs to call or text you at a Korean number before you decide. If the answer is no, the data-only pass is cheaper and simpler. If a delivery app, a reservation desk, or a friend needs to reach you, Welcome Prepaid earns its higher price.

✈️ Is the airport counter more expensive than buying online first?

Yes. KT's own reservation site prices the same tourist pack lower than paying at the counter after you land. KT's announcement of the Korea Tour Pack says explicitly that "additional discounts will be offered when purchased on KT's official website," on top of the airport-counter price. Reserve online, and you still pick the SIM up (or the eSIM activates) once you're through the gate; you just aren't paying the walk-up rate to do it.

What is Incheon's roaming counter?

A carrier-staffed desk at the airport where you can buy, activate or troubleshoot a tourist SIM or eSIM in person. All three Korean carriers run them, and a same-day walk-up is always an option if you land without a reservation.

You don't have to guess where these counters are. Incheon Airport's own facility directory lists a 24-hour counter for SK Telecom, KT and LG U+ near Arrival Hall F, on the west side of Terminal 1's 1st floor: the counters closest to where you'll actually walk out after baggage claim. All three also keep daytime counters (06:00-22:00) near Departure Hall 3 or 4 on the 3rd floor and near Arrival Hall A on the 1st floor, in case the 24-hour one is out of your way.

The Tour Pack bundle also knocks Incheon's airport rail fare down: a one-way AREX ticket normally costs ₩11,000, but the bundle brings it to ₩9,500. That's a small saving on its own, but it stacks with the free T-money card if your trip is five days or longer.

📱 What if I'm staying long enough to want a real Korean number, twice?

SK Telecom's prepaid line skips the fixed bundle entirely and charges for exactly what you use. Its PPS (Pre-Paid Service) is aimed squarely at "international short-term visitors to Korea," and works differently from KT's tourist packs: you load cash onto a line, pick a plan tier, and pay a small daily fee (or none at all, on the no-frills "General" tier) plus per-use rates for calls and data.

What is PPS (Pre-Paid Service)?

SK Telecom's pay-as-you-go plan: no monthly contract, a loaded balance instead of a bill, and data sold in fixed packages that stay valid for a year after you buy them.

Data comes in blocks rather than a daily allowance (₩2,000 for 100MB up to ₩33,000 for 5GB), and that balance doesn't expire until a year after you top up. That detail matters more than it looks: someone who visits Korea twice in a year, on the same number, can buy data once and use the leftover balance on the second trip instead of starting over.

It's a worse fit for a one-week trip, since the daily line fee and per-second call rates add up faster than a flat tourist pack, but it's built to be reused rather than thrown away in the arrivals hall.

My take If you've ever landed somewhere with a dead phone and a hotel address you can't quite remember, you already know which five minutes actually matter. I'd buy the data-only eSIM for a trip under five days: no counter, no card to lose, and it's the cheaper of the two by design. Past a week, or if someone back home needs an actual number to reach you, Welcome Prepaid is worth the extra won. Either way, reserve it online before you fly; the counter price is there for people who didn't have the chance to.

FAQ
What if I'm staying past the 30-day mark?
A separate KT line, unrelated to the tourist packs above, runs 60/180/365 days for ₩10,000/₩30,000/₩50,000, billed per call and per packet at roughly 73% off standard rates instead of a fixed bundle.
Do I need an Alien Registration Card to get any of these, or is a passport enough?
Just the passport. SK Telecom's own PPS page lists an unexpired passport as sufficient for a foreigner to activate the line, with a further three months added on once identity checks clear. None of these tourist plans assume you hold residency paperwork.

None of this is complicated once you've picked a lane: a short trip wants the data-only pass, a longer one wants a number, and either way the carrier's own website beats the counter on price. Reserve before you fly, pick it up on the way out of the arrival hall, and the rest of the trip just works.
