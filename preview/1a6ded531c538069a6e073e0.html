Korean Folk Village: Tickets, Buses and This Fall's Ghosts

Yongin's open-air history park runs ₩37,000-39,000 for a full day, about an hour from Gangnam by bus, with a ghost festival through Nov. 15.

Quick answer
  • A full day costs ₩37,000 on a weekday or ₩39,000 on a weekend for an adult, with a lower child rate both days.
  • Bus 5001-1 runs straight from Gangnam to the gate: no transfer, roughly an hour, though subway-plus-local-bus is cheaper if you don't mind the swap.
  • Go before Nov. 15, 2026 if the fall ghost festival is the draw: book your ticket for a weekend evening, since that's when the haunted walk-throughs run.

The first thing that gets you isn't a single building, it's the quiet. Cross the entrance bridge and the parking-lot noise falls away fast, replaced by the crunch of gravel underfoot and, somewhere past the pines, the low rhythm of a drum warming up for the next show. Families in Yangban-style head wraps pose by a well that still has a bucket on a rope, and a weaver somewhere inside a log house is drawing a backstrap loom taut without looking up. It's part living museum, part open-air stage, and it rewards a reader who plans the day's shows and buses before walking in more than one who wanders and hopes.

What makes Yongin's Korean Folk Village different from a palace in Seoul is that nothing here is roped off from a hundred feet away. You can stand inside a kitchen rebuilt to look like the late Joseon period the whole village recreates, watch a farmer's dance close enough to feel the drumbeats, and still make the last bus back to Gangnam if you've timed it right.

🎫 How much does a day at the Korean Folk Village actually cost?

A full-day ticket runs ₩37,000 (about $27.74) for an adult on a weekday, rising to ₩39,000 (about $29.24) on a weekend, per the Korean Folk Village's own admission fees page. Child admission, which covers ages 36 months through elementary school, is ₩30,000 on weekdays and ₩32,000 on weekends, and anyone middle-school age or older pays the adult rate.

If you're a foreign resident rather than a short-term visitor, the site lists a ₩22,000 discounted rate valid through the end of 2026, though it doesn't spell out exactly which ID counts as proof, so bring your Alien Registration Card and a passport just in case.

What counts as "Child" admission (소인)?

Ages 36 months through elementary school. Toddlers under 36 months get in free with proof of age, and anyone middle-school age or older pays the full adult price.

Arriving in the afternoon is cheaper. From April through November, a weekend 2 p.m. entry ticket is ₩35,000 for an adult and ₩28,000 for a child, and a 4 p.m. entry drops to ₩31,000 and ₩25,000. Those afternoon tickets only exist on weekends, so a weekday visit still means paying the full-day rate no matter what time you walk in.

🚌 How do you get there, and which bus do you take?

Bus 5001-1 is the simplest way in: it runs from Sinnonhyeon Station through Gangnam and Yangjae straight to the Korean Folk Village stop, no transfer. One independent guide clocks the ride from Gangnam Station at about an hour, with the express bus 1560, also departing Gangnam, running closer to 40 minutes. A third option, bus 4101, runs from Seoul Station past Namdaemun and Yangjae IC to the same stop. Koreacharts' route data lists 5001-1 as a direct-seat (직행좌석) bus, and that category's cash fare rose to ₩3,300 after Gyeonggi's across-the-board bus fare increase on October 25, 2025.

What's the subway-and-transfer option?

Ride the Suin-Bundang Line to Sanggal Station, leave by Exit 3, and catch local bus 37 or 10-5 (10-5 stops right at the village). Jukjeon Station and Suwon Station also connect by local bus. It costs a regular city fare rather than an express one, but you're changing vehicles partway.

That transfer route is the cheaper one. A standard Gyeonggi city bus costs ₩1,650 by card or ₩1,700 in cash since the same October 2025 fare change, so riding the subway to Sanggal and switching to bus 10-5 can come in at about half of what the direct express bus's cash fare charges. Neither the park nor the bus operators publish an exact first or last departure for any of these routes, so pull up a map app the morning you go rather than build your day around a printed schedule.

If you're driving, parking runs ₩3,000 for a large vehicle and ₩2,000 for a small one, with a 50% discount for registered disabled-vehicle parking. The address to punch into your navigation app is 경기도 용인시 기흥구 민속촌로 90.

Think of the express bus as a taxi you share with forty strangers: faster and simpler, and you pay for exactly that. On paper it looks like the obvious choice since it skips a transfer, but its ₩3,300 cash fare runs nearly double the regular city bus's ₩1,700. That's ₩1,600 more each way per person, so two people riding round trip pay about ₩6,400 extra just in bus fare, roughly two coffees at Korea's average price. If your legs are tired by the time you're heading home, the direct bus can still be worth that difference.

🎃 Is this fall's ghost festival worth planning around?

Yes, if you want more than static exhibits: "2026 귀신사바 귀신놀이" ("Ghost World, Ghost Play") runs September 12 through November 15, 2026, alongside several haunted walk-through attractions on the same schedule. The park's own site lists companion sets with names like 살귀옥 ("A Haunted Prison"), 혈안식귀 ("A Haunted Den") and 조선살인수사 ("Joseon Murder Investigation"), several of which have been running since April 11 and close on the same November 15 date, plus 심야귀행 ("The Hidden Joseon: Ghost Pursuit"), which opened September 18.

What is 귀신사바 (gwisin-saba)?

Roughly "ghost play" or "ghost world": a seasonal costumed-ghost program layered on top of the village's regular daytime exhibits. It's the park's answer to a Halloween event, built around Joseon-era folklore rather than Western costumes.

The park's live operating-hours listing showed 10:00-22:00 for Saturday, October 17, 2026, which lines up with a later weekend close that gives the evening festival content room to run; hours can shift by date and season, so check the live calendar for the day you're actually going rather than assuming every day matches. If the ghost festival is why you're going, book a weekend evening rather than a weekday afternoon, since that's the slot the haunted walk-throughs are actually built around.

My take A reconstructed village can feel like a theme park wearing history as a costume, and that skepticism makes sense before you've even bought a ticket. What tips it is that the craft demonstrations and the two outdoor shows aren't static: a loom only has thread moving through it when someone's actually sitting at it, and a farmer's dance only lands if you're close enough to feel the drum. If I were choosing between a rushed half-day and a full one, I'd pay the extra two or three thousand won for the weekend rate and build the afternoon around the show times instead of trying to see everything on a tight clock.

FAQ
What if I can only get there around 3 or 4 on a Saturday? Do I lose half my money?
Oddly, no: showing up late is the one scenario that rewards you. Walking in from four o'clock trims ₩8,000 off what an adult would otherwise owe, about the price of two coffees at Korea's typical cafe rate. That particular saving disappears Monday through Friday, since the discount only exists on Saturdays and Sundays.
Will the ghost-festival sets cost extra on top of what I already paid at the gate?
The event pages don't say either way. Treat the haunted sets as possibly separate rather than automatically included, confirm it at the main counter the moment you arrive, and keep a little extra cash on hand just in case.

A village built around demonstrations and timed shows rewards planning more than wandering. Take bus 5001-1 if you want the simplest ride in, or save on fare by transferring at Sanggal Station, and buy the weekend ticket if this fall's ghost festival is the reason you're going.
