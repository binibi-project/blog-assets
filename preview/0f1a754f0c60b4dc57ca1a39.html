N Seoul Tower: The Cable Car, the Bus, and the Real Cost

What N Seoul Tower admission, the Namsan cable car and the circular shuttle bus actually cost, with hours, fares and the fastest way up Namsan.

Quick answer
  • The ₩1,500 shuttle bus is the cheapest way up: it's a city bus that drives straight to the tower, no mountain required on foot.
  • The cable car costs ten times that for three minutes in the air: you're paying for the view on the way up, not the speed.
  • Check the tower's closing time before you go up: last entry is 30 minutes before close, and that line moves earlier on weeknights.

By early evening, the queue at the Namsan cable car's lower station starts filling with couples angling their phones at the gondola overhead, and somewhere above them Seoul is turning on its lights one district at a time. The tower itself is the easy part to find; the climb up to it is where a visitor's plan actually gets made or unmade, because there are three different ways up Namsan and they cost nothing alike.

What is the Namsan Circular Shuttle Bus?

A city bus route, numbered 01A and 01B, that loops around Namsan on the usual T-money fare instead of a special tourist price. It's the version of "taking the bus" that just happens to end at a tower instead of an office building.

🚌 Up for ₩1,500, or free on foot

Catch the 01A or 01B shuttle from Chungmuro or Dongguk University station and you're at the tower gate for a single city-bus fare. N Seoul Tower's own transit page lists both lines running daily from 06:30 to 23:00 on a flat ₩1,500 fare, paid by transportation card only. Bus 01A loops roughly every 9 minutes between the Namsan Yejang bus transfer lot, Chungmuro Station, Dongguk University Station and the tower; 01B runs about every 14 minutes and adds the cable car station and the walking-trail entrance to its stops. Neither operator posts the ride's actual length, so budget it loosely rather than timing a connection around it.

General vehicle access to Namsan has been restricted since May 1, 2005, and the Namsan Circular Road itself has been closed to private cars since August 2021, so the shuttle isn't a workaround for traffic. It's the only way most vehicles reach the top at all. That's also why a taxi can't just drop you at the tower door.

For a free option, Namsan Ormi, a sloped outdoor elevator near Myeongdong Station Exit 4, carries walkers partway up with no ticket booth involved. From the exit, go straight, turn left at the Hoehyeon intersection, and look for it near the Namsan 3rd Tunnel completion monument. From there, a walking trail continues up past the cable car's lower station toward the summit, the same trail bus 01B lists as a stop. Neither operator posts how long the walk itself takes, so that part isn't confirmed; treat it as a real hike, not a stroll, and wear shoes for it.

What is Namsan Ormi (남산오르미)?

A sloped, outdoor elevator built into the hillside near Myeongdong, meant to carry people partway up Namsan without stairs. "Ormi" comes from the Korean verb "to climb" (오르다); it's a lift, not a scenic ride, and it drops you at the start of the walking trail rather than at the tower itself.

🚠 The cable car: three minutes, and ten times the bus fare

A round-trip adult cable car ticket costs ₩15,000, and the ride itself takes about three minutes each way. One-way tickets run ₩12,000 for an adult; children age 36 months to 12 years pay ₩11,500 round trip or ₩9,000 one-way. Residents of Jung-gu or Yongsan-gu get a discounted ₩7,500 round trip with ID, and groups of 30 or more pay a reduced adult rate of ₩13,500 round trip. The cable car runs 10:00 to 23:00, 365 days a year, though the operator warns that strong wind or bad weather can pause service without much notice.

Think of the fare difference this way: the shuttle bus gets you to the same hilltop for ₩1,500, so the rest of a ₩15,000 cable car round trip is really the price of the view out the window on the way up, not the climb itself. If the sky is hazy, that trade stops making much sense.

A free shuttle also connects Myeongdong Station Exit 1 to the cable car's lower station, departing roughly every 30 minutes, with the first bus at 12:00 p.m. and the last at 8:30 p.m., timed from the cable car parking lot. It skips a few slots for driver breaks: no service at 14:30 or 17:30 on weekdays and Sundays, and an extra gap at 17:00 on Saturdays. Walking instead takes about 7 to 8 minutes from Myeongdong Station Exit 3, through the alley by the 7-Eleven, between the Pacific Hotel and a GS25.

Namsan's own Love Locks plaza sits near the summit, where railings have filled with padlocks that visitors write on and leave behind as a kind of promise. It costs nothing to see regardless of which way up you choose, since it sits on the open hillside rather than past any ticket gate.

💸 What a full visit up Namsan actually costs

Pick one way up, add the observatory ticket if you want the indoor view, and the whole outing still comes in under what a single Busan X the Sky ticket costs on its own. The observation deck itself runs 10:00 to 22:30 on weekdays and 10:00 to 23:00 on weekends and holidays, with the last entry 30 minutes before whichever closing time applies that night. An adult ticket (age 13 and up) costs ₩29,000 at the booth; children age 3 to 12 and seniors 65 and up pay ₩23,000.

Skip the cable car and take the shuttle bus instead, and the whole day drops by ₩13,500 without losing the observatory itself. That's the one real lever here: the tower ticket is fixed either way, so the cable car is the optional add-on, not a required part of getting to the top.

My take A ₩13,500 gap between the bus and the cable car is easy to wave off until it's actually the money on the line. In your shoes, on a clear evening with a tight budget, I'd take the shuttle bus up and spend the savings on the observatory ticket instead, since the indoor view is the part a bus window can't give you. If it's a date, or a one-night stop in Seoul, I'd pay for the cable car's three minutes once and not think twice about it. Either way, I'd pick one and skip the other, not stack both into the same afternoon.

FAQ
Can I buy the cable car ticket online before I go?
No. Pay cash or card at the booth that same morning; nothing here offers a mobile voucher or lets anyone reserve ahead.
Is there a combined pass for the tower and the ride up?
No listed bundle exists. Each attraction publishes its own fee page with no mention of the other, so seeing both means two separate purchases, not one discounted combo.

The honest version of this guide is that Namsan doesn't have one right answer, just a cheap way up, a scenic way up, and a free way up, and the choice mostly comes down to what you're actually there for that evening. Go for sunset if you can time it, since that's the stretch the cable car's own team recommends, and the light is doing half the work for you either way.
