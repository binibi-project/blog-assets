Ganghwa Island Day Trip: Dolmens, a Temple and One Bus

A UNESCO dolmen site that costs nothing, Korea's oldest temple inside a 2.3km fortress, and the one ₩3,000 bus that gets you there from Sinchon.

Quick answer
  • Take bus 3000 from Sinchon: it's the only direct public bus linking Seoul to Ganghwa Terminal, and the card fare has held at ₩3,000 since August 2025.
  • The dolmen site itself is free: Bugeun-ri's UNESCO-listed stones are open around the clock at no charge, while the museum next door is a separate ₩3,000.
  • Budget the whole day: catch the earliest bus you can manage, since the ride alone runs close to two hours each way before you've seen a single stone.

By the time the bus crosses the last bridge onto Ganghwa, Seoul's apartment towers have already given way to rice paddies and low, round hills. Somewhere past the toll gate, the driver's radio switches from traffic updates to not much of anything, and that's usually the first sign you've left the city's gravity behind. What's waiting on the other side is a working farm island that happens to hold a cemetery older than written history and a temple that's been standing, in one form or another, since before Seoul was Seoul. It rewards a full day more than a rushed afternoon.

🚌 How do you actually get to Ganghwa Island from Seoul?

Bus 3000 is the move: it runs straight from Sinchon Station to Ganghwa Terminal, past Hapjeong and Magok, with no transfer required. It's described as the only direct public bus connecting Ganghwa Terminal to those Seoul stops, and Incheon put two more buses on the route in 2025, bringing the fleet to 12. The card fare has sat at ₩3,000 since the city's express-seat pricing took effect on August 1, 2025.

Rush-hour buses now come every 15 minutes instead of 20, which is the kind of change that matters more at 8 a.m. than at 2 p.m. Outside peak hours you'll wait longer, and neither the city nor the operator has published a first or last departure time for this exact route, so pull up a map app the morning you go rather than trust an old blog's printed schedule.

Driving skips the bus, though not the clock. The appeal of bus 3000 is that it already handles the one awkward part, crossing from Seoul through Gimpo onto the island, without you hunting for parking near a bridge toll plaza. One regional report still puts the ride at close to two hours door to door, Gimpo traffic included, since there's no expressway shortcut yet. That's slower than it sounds for a place this close to Seoul, so pair it with an early start rather than a lazy brunch first.

🪨 Is the Ganghwa Dolmen Site worth the trip?

Yes, and it costs nothing: the Bugeun-ri dolmen field is free, open 24 hours, and it's one of the actual reasons Ganghwa made it onto a UNESCO list. UNESCO inscribed the site in December 2000 as part of a shared listing that also covers dolmen fields at Gochang and Hwasun, with the Ganghwa portion alone covering 12.27 hectares across the island's slopes. Sixteen dolmens cluster around Bugeun-ri, and the headline one, Ganghwa Jiseokmyo, is a table-shaped northern dolmen whose flat capstone runs about 7.1 meters and weighs roughly 75 tons, balanced on just two upright stones.

What is a dolmen (고인돌)?

A Bronze Age tomb built from huge stone slabs: usually two or more uprights holding a flat capstone like a table. Korea has more dolmens than almost anywhere else on earth, and Ganghwa's share is part of why UNESCO's World Heritage listing exists at all.

The stones themselves don't explain much on their own, which is where the paid half of the visit comes in. The Ganghwa History Museum sits right next to the dolmen field and runs ₩3,000 for adults and ₩2,000 for children, teens and soldiers, with infants and seniors free. It's open daily 09:00 to 18:00 and closed on Mondays, a detail an Incheon tourism page confirmed as recently as this past June.

🛕 What does ₩4,000 actually get you at Jeondeungsa?

₩4,000 buys adult admission to Korea's oldest standing temple, and the ticket also walks you through a 2.3-kilometer stone fortress most visitors never notice they've entered. Individual admission runs ₩4,000 for adults, ₩3,000 for teens and ₩1,500 for children, with free entry for anyone 65 or older. Hours shift by season: 08:00 to 18:30 in summer, 08:30 to 18:00 in winter, though the page doesn't say exactly when that switch happens.

Jeondeungsa sits inside Ganghwa Samnangseong, a fortress whose walls still run about 2.3 kilometers around Jeongjoksan, covering roughly 260,536 square meters. The Academy of Korean Studies traces the wall back to at least the 13th century and records that it was rebuilt in 1660, the same year a royal archive went up inside it to store the dynasty's records.

What is Samnangseong (삼랑성)?

A stone mountain fortress that predates the temple it now shelters. Local legend credits it to Dangun's three sons, which is where the name comes from, but its real claim to fame is a single afternoon in 1866.

What actually happened that afternoon is the part most guidebooks skip past. In the autumn of 1866, French forces occupied Ganghwa during a punitive campaign; a Joseon commander named Yang Heon-su led roughly 500 troops across the strait and into Samnangseong, then held the walls when about 150 French soldiers attacked on October 3 by the lunar calendar. The French were repelled and withdrew from the island soon after, and a monument to Yang still stands inside the fortress near its east gate.

On paper that's a cheap day, and it mostly is. The bus still eats more of it than the prices suggest: round-trip transit alone runs close to four hours once you add both legs. So the ₩14,000 (about $10.44) covering the bus, the temple ticket and a coffee is really buying a long, unhurried day, not a quick errand.

If a coffee stop appeals, Ganghwa-eup has an unusual one: Johyang Bangjik, a former textile mill turned cafe and gallery, charges around ₩7,000 for an Americano, which is steep for a drink but closer to an admission fee for walking through the old factory floor.

My take A two-hour bus ride for a dolmen field and a temple can sound like a lot to ask on a single Saturday, and that worry is fair. If I were weighing it, I'd stop worrying about "fitting in enough" and pick one anchor for the day, Jeondeungsa with its fortress walls, and treat the dolmen site as the free bonus you pass on the way rather than a second main event. In your shoes, I'd rather arrive unrushed at one place than jog through two.

FAQ
Is there a phone number to call ahead, for a group visit or just to double-check hours?
Yes, for both main stops. Jeondeungsa's office answers at +82-32-937-0125, and the History Museum's front desk is +82-32-934-7887, numbers worth saving before you leave Seoul rather than hunting for them on arrival.
Can I pay admission by card, or should I bring cash?
Bring cash for both counters. Neither the temple's nor the museum's official listing names a payment method, and small regional ticket booths in Korea skew toward cash far more often than big-city attractions do.

Ganghwa isn't a place that needs convincing once you're standing in it, just a morning set aside and a bus number written down. Check bus 3000's timetable the day before on a map app, not from an old post like this one, since neither city hall nor the operator has published a fixed first and last run.
