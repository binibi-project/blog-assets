Banpo Rainbow Fountain: Free to Watch, Weeks Left in 2026

Seoul's free Banpo Bridge rainbow fountain runs 3 shorter shows a night under a 2026 energy order and shuts for the season on Oct. 31, with the route and real costs.

Quick answer
  • Watching costs nothing: the fountain sprays off the underside of Banpo Bridge and anyone on the riverbank can see it, no ticket or reservation.
  • It runs three 20-minute shows a night now, not five: an energy-saving order cut the schedule starting June 1, 2026, to 19:30-19:50, 20:00-20:20 and 20:30-20:50.
  • This year's season ends October 31: go on a clear night before then, since rain or strong wind can cancel a show outright.

Phones go up along the railing a little before 7:30, nobody quite sure which direction the first jet will go. Then the underside of Banpo Bridge lights up red, then green, then a run of colors chasing each other down its length, and for twenty minutes the whole river smells faintly of spray and someone's grilling nearby. Little kids shriek at the first blast of water like it's personal. It's one of the few free things in Seoul that still manages to feel like an event.

What makes it worth the trip isn't just the color. Seoul's own account of the fountain says the water comes straight out of the Han River the bridge sits over, pumped up and sprayed back down into itself, so the fountain is quite literally made from the river it hangs off. That loop, plus the fact that it still runs for free every year, is most of the reason locals keep coming back even after watching it a dozen times.

🌈 How much does it actually cost to watch?

Nothing, as long as you're standing on the bank. The Moonlight Rainbow Fountain (달빛무지개분수) sprays from 380 nozzles along both sides of Banpo Bridge, pumping roughly 190 tons of river water a minute, and anyone on the Banpo Hangang Park side or the lower Jamsu Bridge can watch without a ticket. It was certified by Guinness World Records in 2008 as the longest bridge fountain at 1,140 meters.

What is a Hangang Park (한강공원)?

One of twelve public riverside parks the city runs along both banks of the Han River, each with its own name (Banpo, Yeouido, Ttukseom and so on). They're free, open all hours, and most Seoulites treat them the way other cities treat a public beach.

The catch is everything around the watching. A Han River park convenience store can legally charge up to 10% more than a shop outside the park, a rule Seoul put in place in 2024 after complaints about prices. When the city checked its own Yeouido and Ttukseom stores against nearby shops in April 2026, it found the gap was real but smaller than rumored: about ₩100 more per bottle of water and ₩200-700 more per can of beer, not the "double or triple" some reports claimed.

That 10% ceiling is worth knowing before you buy, so you're not left wondering whether you just got overcharged for a drink with a view.

🕢 What time does the fountain actually run in 2026?

Three shows a night, each 20 minutes, starting at 19:30, 20:00 and 20:30. Seoul cut the Moonlight Rainbow Fountain from five daily shows to three on June 1, 2026, as part of a city-wide energy-saving order responding to a Middle East-linked energy crunch, and the new times are fixed at 19:30-19:50, 20:00-20:20 and 20:30-20:50. The same order trimmed hours at Gwanghwamun Plaza's water features and suspended the Yeouido and Ttukseom fountains outright, so Banpo's is now one of the few still running every night.

Before the cut, the fountain ran a midday show at noon plus four evening slots from 19:30 to 21:00, with a fifth added at 21:30 during July and August. Losing the noon show stings less than it sounds: the lighting only reads clearly after dark, so the evening slots were always the ones worth planning around.

🚇 How do you get there, and will it be running when you arrive?

Ride Line 3, 7 or 9 to Express Bus Terminal Station and leave by Exit 8-1, marked "Hangang Park." A single T-money subway fare is ₩1,550 for an adult. Exit 8-1 is the station's own listed exit for the park, and an underground passage from the Goto Mall shopping arcade also leads straight there, through a corridor decorated with a Picasso-print mural, if you'd rather walk through shops than outside.

What is Sebitseom (세빛섬)?

Three artificial islands floating just off Banpo park, each lit in its own LED color scheme at night. They're a five-minute walk past the fountain and free to look at from outside, even if you don't go in for whatever's running inside that week.

Weather can cancel a show with no makeup time. The city's own notice warns that schedules "may change" and points visitors back to the operating agency for the day's real status, and rain or a gusty night is the usual reason a show gets skipped. Check a weather app that afternoon, not just the forecast from the day before, since river-level wind can pick up fast once the sun goes down.

My take A free fountain that lost two of its five nightly shows sounds like a letdown on paper, and it's fair to feel a little cheated if you planned a trip around the old noon slot. The three shows that are left still cover the only part of the day the lighting actually matters, so in your shoes I'd stop mourning the cut schedule and just pick one of the three times and commit to arriving fifteen minutes early for a railing spot. If I were choosing a night, I'd take a weeknight over a weekend one: the same twenty minutes of color, a fraction of the elbow room.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Walk five minutes past the fountain to Sebitseom. The three islands run their own LED color schemes after dark, so you get a second free light show without leaving the park.

  • Watch for October 31, 2026. That's this year's last scheduled night before the fountain goes quiet for winter; a visit after that date means waiting for spring.
FAQ
What happens if it's raining or windy the night I planned to go?
Treat it as a bonus for the evening, not the headline plan, since Seoul's own notice only promises hours "may change," with no refund or makeup slot offered. Light drizzle is often survivable if you duck beneath the upper roadway one level down; a genuine downpour usually keeps the jets switched off for good, so check conditions that same afternoon instead of committing from a forecast a day old.
Is the convenience store near the fountain actually profiteering?
Officials call the "double or triple" complaints overblown, yet admit a gap exists — and six of twenty-three vendors operating inside these riverside spots have folded anyway, squeezed between that same markup limit and competition from food-delivery apps. Grabbing a cold can on site rather than hauling one from home props up a struggling storefront more than it dents your wallet.

Pick the 20:00 show if you only have one shot at it. It's late enough for full dark and early enough that the 20:30 crowd hasn't arrived yet to steal your spot on the railing.
