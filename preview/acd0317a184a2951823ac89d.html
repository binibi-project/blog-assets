Seoul City Tour Bus: 15 Stops, One Ticket, a Zombie Season

Tiger Bus's hop-on-hop-off loop costs ₩27,000, plus a Halloween-season Zombie Attack course. Fares, the Gwanghwamun booth, and what's worth it.

Quick answer
  • The day course costs ₩27,000: Tiger Bus's own site lists its 15-stop Downtown, Palaces & Namsan loop at that price, marked down from a ₩36,000 regular fare.
  • Right now there's a zombie version: the same company runs a Halloween-season "Zombie Attack" day and night course, priced above the regular routes.
  • Buy straight from the operator's own site or the Gwanghwamun booth: the same discounted price shows up either way, so there's no separate code to hunt for.

Fifteen stops, one ticket, and a red double-decker idling in front of the Donghwa Duty Free building before most of the morning commute has even cleared Gwanghwamun Station. A staff member in the ticket booth's little window slides a paper pass across to a cluster of visitors still squinting at a map on their phones, and for the rest of the day, that's the whole decision made for them. It's not the fastest way around the palace district and Namsan, and it was never trying to be.

🚌 What does the ₩27,000 ticket actually buy?

The Downtown, Palaces & Namsan course costs ₩27,000 for an adult and ₩19,000 for a child on the operator's own site, both marked down from a ₩36,000 and ₩22,000 regular fare. Seoul City Tour Bus's own fare page lists that price next to "무제한 승하차가능 · 15개 정류장" (unlimited boarding and alighting across 15 stops). That markdown works out to about 25% off the printed rate, and it's applied the same way across every course the company runs.

The company behind it, Seoul City Tour Bus Co., Ltd., goes by Tiger Bus, and its ticket booth sits at 68-2 Taepyeong-ro 1-ga in Jung-gu, right by the Donghwa Duty Free building near Gwanghwamun. Visit Seoul, the city's own tourism organization, lists it as one of two companies authorized to run a "Seoul City Tour Bus" service.

What does "hop-on-hop-off" actually mean here?

One ticket covers the whole day. Ride the loop straight through, or get off at any of the 15 stops, spend as long as you want, and catch a later bus around the same route to keep going.

🌃 What does the night course add, and why is there sometimes a zombie on it?

The night course adds an N Seoul Tower stop with 25 minutes built in for photos, and the whole loop takes about 90 minutes, priced at ₩25,000 for an adult and ₩16,000 for a child, both marked down from ₩29,000 and ₩18,000. N Seoul Tower's own visitor guide points travelers toward this bus as one of its recommended ways up the mountain, and the 25-minute window is the only stop on either course built around a single view rather than a walk-around.

Right now, the same company is also selling a "Zombie Attack" version of both its day and night courses, priced above the regular fare. The day version runs ₩30,000 for an adult, down from ₩40,000, and ₩22,000 for a child, down from ₩29,500; the night version charges a flat ₩35,000 for adults and children alike, down from ₩47,000. Both are listed as live, bookable products on the company's site today.

It's a seasonal layer on the same routes, not a separate bus system. The operator has run costumed horror-themed night rides before, and this year's version lands squarely inside Korea's Halloween season. If a bus full of screaming strangers isn't your idea of sightseeing, the regular day and night courses run right alongside it at the lower price, unchanged.

Tiger Bus or Yellow Balloon City Bus?

Seoul licenses two separate companies to run "Seoul City Tour Bus" services. Tiger Bus departs from Gwanghwamun's Donghwa Duty Free stop; Yellow Balloon City Bus departs from Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station and runs a different set of courses. A ticket for one doesn't board the other.

🎫 Where do you actually buy the ticket, and what's nearby?

Buy it at the Gwanghwamun ticket booth itself or straight from the operator's own site before you go: both show the same marked-down price, so there's no separate discount code to dig up. The booth's listed contact number is 02-777-6090.

A few minutes on foot from Gwanghwamun Station's Exit 6, Gwanghwamun Gukbap serves its pork gukbap for ₩12,000. A Twosome Place location nearby prices a regular americano at ₩4,700.

Why does every price come with two numbers?

The higher number is the company's own listed regular fare; the lower one is what its site currently charges for the same ticket. Both appear side by side on the fare page itself, not behind a hidden coupon.

My take A bus that costs more than a subway ride can feel like a tourist trap before it's even left the curb, especially once you already know how to read a Seoul subway map. I'd still take it once, on a first morning in the city, for the exact reason it looks wasteful on paper: it hands you a lap of the palace district and Namsan without a single transfer to plan, and that's worth ₩27,000 on a day when you're jet-lagged and don't want to think about routes. After that first lap, though, I'd switch back to the subway and a T-money card for everywhere else. The bus is an orientation tool, not a transportation plan for the whole trip.

FAQ
Are there tours besides the two main loops?
Several: a roughly six-hour excursion into Bukhansan National Park, bundled with a day-loop ticket, lists at $28 per person, and a 70-minute backstage peek at Sejong Centre charges an identical rate, each its own separate listing apart from the two main itineraries.
Does the ride come with headphones for the audio guide?
Yes, per Visit Seoul: single-use headphones ship with every seat, and the commentary itself plays in 11 languages, useful well past English and Korean.

Riding it once on a first morning and switching to the subway for the rest of the trip is the call this fare table leads me to. The bus will still be circling Gwanghwamun on your last day in Seoul either way, zombies or no zombies.
