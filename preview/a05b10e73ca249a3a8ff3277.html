What Happens When You Call 112 or 119 in Korea

Korea's 112 and 119 are free and interpreted in English and Chinese 24/7, and here's what to dial if your language isn't one of them.

Quick answer
  • 112 is police, 119 is fire and ambulance: both are free from any phone in Korea, no area code needed.
  • English and Chinese interpreters join a 112 call automatically, any hour, and 119 covers several languages in-house too.
  • Save the backup number too: put 1588-5644 (BBB Korea) in your phone now, free human interpreting in 17 languages, before you actually need it.

Two numbers, three digits each, cover almost everything: 112 for the police, 119 for a fire or a medical emergency. Simple enough to memorize before you land, which matters, because the moment you actually need one rarely arrives with a clear head and a strong signal. Picture it: light fading on a trail above Bukhansan, an ankle that won't take weight anymore, and a phone screen you can barely read past the panic. What happens in the next sixty seconds used to come down to whether the person on the other end spoke your language.

That's changed faster than most visitors realize, and not by accident.

What is a three-way interpretation call?

A setup where a caller, a Korean official, and an interpreter are all connected on the same line at once. The official asks you to hold for a moment while the interpreter is patched in, then the three of you talk it through together.

📞 Which number do I call, and what happens after?

For a crime in progress or immediate danger, call 112; for a fire, an injury or any medical emergency, call 119. Both are free from any phone, with no area code to dial first, and both run around the clock. If you're not sure which one fits, pick either: 112 can patch you through to 119 and back, and so can the travel hotline further down this page.

On 119, an operator already has in-house interpreters covering English, Chinese, Japanese, Mongolian, Vietnamese, and the ambulance ride itself is free regardless of your nationality or insurance. Give your location as exactly as you can: a Korean address or a nearby landmark gets a crew moving faster than a pin you're not sure is accurate.

If you can read a sign, a subway exit number, or a store name nearby, say that too.

That free ride only covers the trip. Everything after you reach the hospital, the exam, the tests, any medicine, gets billed on its own, and the total lands differently depending on whether you carry Korean insurance.

🗣️ How does the language barrier actually get handled?

Call 112 and an English or Chinese interpreter can join the same call, any hour, since March 18, 2024. Before that, the service only ran weekdays, 09:00 to 21:00. A pilot had launched in Seoul in June 2023 with four interpreters, then went nationwide on July 3, 2023.

By March 2024, twelve more interpreters joined, bringing the Seoul center's team to 16. The push traces back to a specific failure: the night of the Itaewon crowd crush, none of the 93 calls that came into 112 were from a foreign national, even though dozens of foreign visitors were among the crowd.

It's working, in the sense that people are actually using it. The Korea Herald's review of police data shows foreign-language calls to 112 hit 21,252 in 2025, more than double 2024's 10,348, and 2026 had already logged 21,012 by the end of August alone.

Read it as a graph of the language barrier shrinking, one logged call at a time, not a graph of Korea getting more dangerous for visitors.

What is BBB Korea?

A volunteer human-interpretation phone service, named for the Tower of Babel. Dial 1588-5644, choose a language, and a volunteer joins your call in real time, in 17 languages, for free.

🌐 What if your language isn't English or Chinese?

Dial 1330 for interpretation in nine languages, or 1588-5644 for a free human interpreter in 17, and neither one is the emergency line itself. Hanam city's English page lists 1330's phone coverage as Korean, English, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay, Indonesian, with the first four running all day and the rest limited to 08:00-19:00.

Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Save both 1588-5644 and 1330 as contacts named "EMERGENCY" before you travel, not after something goes wrong. A free interpreter only helps if you remember the number while you're rattled.
  • What locals argue about Whether throwing an AI interpreter at the gap actually fixes it. Seoul alone had 629 civilian interpreters as of September, but only 97 spoke English against 198 Chinese speakers, and police are now planning an AI-based 112 system covering English, Chinese, Japanese, Vietnamese, Thai, at a proposed ₩13.6 billion (about $10.3 million) for 2027 alone. Experts quoted in the same report still want a human checking the machine's work in the field, so the shortage doesn't just move from people to software.
  • Watch for The timeline. That AI system isn't due to finish development until the end of 2027, with upgrades running into 2029, while foreign-language calls are already climbing faster than the current roster of interpreters.
My take Needing help somewhere you can't read the signs already feels like losing some control, and realizing you don't know the right number loses you a little more of it before anyone's even picked up. If I were landing in Korea without a word of Korean, I'd save 1588-5644 and 1330 the evening the ticket gets booked, not the night something actually goes wrong. A free interpreter is only useful if panic hasn't already shortened your patience for digging through old texts to find the number, so put both numbers in your contacts before you travel, labeled so you'd actually find them in a hurry.
FAQ
Will the ambulance ride cost me anything if I'm a tourist without Korean insurance?
The ride itself, no: nationality and insurance status don't change that part. Where the bill actually starts is the hospital door. Ask the front desk for a written estimate before agreeing to anything that isn't urgent, since an uninsured visitor pays the full rate for the exam and whatever comes after it, not a subsidized one.
What if my language isn't on either the 112 or the 1330 phone list?
BBB Korea picks up with a live volunteer and no closing time, so dial 1588-5644 directly. Prefer typing? The hotline also answers through its own Messenger app, KakaoTalk, Facebook, Line and WhatsApp, in the same four tongues it covers over the phone, at no data cost on Wi-Fi. None of this swaps in for an interpreter once things turn urgent, but it spares a roaming bill for anything slower. That same number doubles as Korea's tourist complaint line, so a taxi driver who overcharges you or a shop that won't honor a posted price goes to the same operator, not a separate desk.

The two numbers worth losing sleep over, if you're the type who likes to be ready, are 112 and 119. Everything else on this page is backup for when neither alone gets you understood. Save 1588-5644 before you need it, not during.
