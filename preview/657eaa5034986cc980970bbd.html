Everland's Horror Nights and Its New ₩71,000 Ticket

Everland's top adult pass hit ₩71,000 on October 6. The free Blood City horror zone runs to Nov 22, and a new 1-hour bus skips the old transfer.

Quick answer
  • Every price tier rose ₩3,000 on October 6: the top-tier adult pass is now ₩71,000, the first time a major Korean park's standard one-day price has crossed ₩70,000.
  • Blood City Zero runs through November 22: five zombie-themed zones, free once you're past the gate, performing nightly from 4 to 9 p.m.
  • Check which price tier your date falls under first: confirm the season tier on Everland's own booking calendar before you lock in a date, since the gap between tiers is ₩16,000 a ticket.

Five zombie-themed zones, seventy-two days of them, and one new bus that finally skips the transfer. Walk through Everland's gate after four o'clock this fall and the park's usual parade music starts sharing the air with a low siren drifting over from past the garden, the signal that Blood City's cast is already in position. Families who came for the rides end up staying for the horror; by early evening the two crowds blur into one long line of phones held up at the same dark corner, waiting for the same scare.

If every picture in your head of this place is a daytime one, the after-dark version is the one worth actually planning a trip around. The catch this year is that the plan costs more than it did last month, and getting there changed too.

What is Blood City (블러드시티)?

Everland's outdoor horror zone, built as an immersive live performance rather than a haunted house you simply walk through. Actors stay in character across several linked sets, and visitors move between them completing small missions instead of following one fixed path.

🧟 What's new at Everland this fall?

The headline act is "Blood City Zero: Gate of Hell," Everland's horror event running September 12 through November 22, 2026, included free with any regular day pass. Samsung C&T's resort division built this year's edition around five themed zones, among them "지옥의 문" (Gate of Hell) and "광기의 서커스" (Circus of Madness). Director Lee Seok-hoon, known for the films The Pirates and Confidential Assignment 2, shaped the cinematic horror concept.

The story casts visitors as the "White Z" team fighting off a zombie outbreak, and you're free to wander between zones finishing missions rather than queuing through a single haunted corridor. Performances run every day of the festival from 4 to 9 p.m., with no separate fee once you're inside the park.

The daytime side changed too: Lost Valley opened a new walking safari called Wild Savanna Expedition on the same day the horror festival started, September 12. The route winds past 15 animal species on foot rather than from a vehicle, with giraffes and elephants among them, plus rhinos.

Everland's best-known ride is still T Express, a wooden roller coaster that opened in 2008 and now also carries the name MonimoRUSH. The park itself goes back further: it opened on this site in 1976 as Yongin Farmland, took the Everland name in 1996, and drew 5.85 million visitors in 2018, the most recent figure Wikipedia's entry covers.

What is MonimoRUSH (모니모러쉬)?

The current name for T Express, Everland's wooden roller coaster. It opened in 2008 and is still one of the park's signature rides under its new branding.

💸 How much does a day actually cost now?

A top-tier adult day pass now runs ₩71,000 (about $53.15 at ~₩1,335.80/$1), up ₩3,000 from ₩68,000 as of October 6, 2026. The same ₩3,000 bump hit every tier: the mid-season (성수기) adult pass rose to ₩65,000 (about $48.66) and the regular/off-peak tier rose to ₩55,000 (about $41.17). Everland's own calendar decides which tier applies to a given date, and the gap between the cheapest and priciest tier is ₩16,000 a ticket, so it's worth checking before you commit to a weekend.

It's a fair question whether a ticket is still worth it at that price, and the honest answer is that it depends which tier your date lands in. This is the first time a major Korean park's standard adult pass has passed the ₩70,000 line, and it comes only three years and seven months after the last hike, from ₩64,000 to ₩68,000 in March 2023.

Lotte World raised its own adult pass from ₩64,000 to ₩67,000 this past April, so Everland's top tier now sits ₩4,000 above Lotte World's. Both are still well under Tokyo Disney's top adult ticket of ¥12,400 (roughly ₩107,000), though comparing across countries this way only goes so far given the differences in local prices and incomes.

On paper the ticket is the whole bill, but festival food has a way of running past what you'd budget for a theme park snack. Last fall's K-pop: Demon Hunters tie-in snack sets sold for ₩36,000-38,000, and plenty of reviewers said the portions were generous but the food itself tasted like ordinary frozen park fare. Nothing confirms whether this year's Blood City-themed menu lands anywhere near that range, so treat food as a separate, flexible line in your budget rather than a fixed number.

🚌 What's the fastest way in from Seoul?

A new express bus, route 4105, gets you from Seoul Station to Everland in about an hour with no transfer, roughly half the time of the old subway-and-bus route. It launched August 1, 2026, departing from Seoul Station Bus Transfer Center and passing Myeongdong, Sungnyemun, Namdaemun Market and Itaewon before it gets on the expressway.

What is an express-seat bus (직행좌석버스)?

A Gyeonggi-run bus class built for longer commutes between Seoul and its suburbs, with assigned seating and a flat regional fare rather than a city-bus rate. Route 4105 and the older Gangnam route both fall into this class.

If you're not staying near one of those stops, the universal fallback is still the subway: ride the Suin-Bundang Line to Giheung Station, then transfer to EverLine, the light-rail line running directly to Jeondae-Everland Station, with a fare-integrated transfer in place since September 2014. It's the route Samsung C&T itself used for comparison, putting the old multi-transfer trip at roughly 1 hour 40 minutes to 1 hour 50 minutes against the new bus's one hour.

Neither announcement published a fare or a timetable for route 4105 itself, so the exact seat-reservation details and first and last departures aren't confirmed; check Everland's own site for the current schedule before you build a day around a specific bus.

My take A ₩16,000 gap between the cheapest and priciest date on the same calendar is the kind of detail that's easy to miss until the total at the gate doesn't match what a friend paid last month. If the schedule allows it, I'd pick a regular-tier weekday over a weekend this fall and treat Blood City as the free upgrade it actually is, since the horror zone costs nothing extra once you're inside either way. The bigger question isn't whether the festival is worth it; it's whether the date on your ticket happens to land on the expensive side of a gap that size.

FAQ
Is there a way to enjoy Blood City without actually getting chased by the actors?
Mostly, yes. Beyond the chase itself, this fall's lineup adds 38 pieces of horror-branded merchandise and a makeup counter where guests paint themselves to look the part instead of fleeing an actor who already does. Skip the sprinting entirely and the merchandise table alone is worth stopping for.
Blood City has five zones. Does that mean the whole park only has five things to see?
No, those are two different fives. Everland's permanent layout splits into Global Fair, Zootopia, European Adventure, Magic Land and American Adventure, and Blood City's zombie sets are built as a seasonal overlay inside that existing footprint, not a replacement for it. Treat the festival as one evening layer on top of a park that's otherwise the same five sections, year-round.

The ticket costs more than it did last month, the ride in from Seoul Station is faster than it's ever been, and the horror zone is free once you're past the gate either way. Pick a regular-tier weekday if your schedule bends that far, and let Blood City be the part of the day you didn't pay extra for.
