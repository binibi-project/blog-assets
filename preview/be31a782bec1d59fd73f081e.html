Ever stood outside a Seoul guesthouse at midnight, phone in hand, craving fried chicken, and hit a sign-up screen asking for a resident number you don't have? The smell from the chicken place down the block drifts up anyway, mocking you through a closed door.

Somewhere between the app store and the first bite, a huge share of visitors to Korea now make it through that screen, foreign-card orders on one app alone climbing by the hundreds of percent this year. The gap between craving and ordering is smaller than it used to be. It just isn't zero.

📲 Can You Order Without a Korean Phone Number?

Baemin will take your order without a Korean phone number; Coupang Eats, by most accounts, still won't. The Korea Times reported on February 4, 2026 that Baemin, Korea's biggest delivery app, now lets foreigners register with either a Korean phone number or a foreigner registration number, and lets anyone place a one-time order without signing up at all by verifying an international number through SMS. The same update added English, Chinese and Japanese ordering, with menus translated by a large-language-model engine rather than word for word.

Coupang Eats is the one to budget extra time for. A foreigner-focused guide to using Coupang in Korea says the app's sign-up still wants a Korean phone number and an Alien Registration Card, with the interface itself in English but some restaurant menus left untranslated. That account hasn't been confirmed on Coupang's own help pages, so treat it as the current word from people who've tried it rather than an official policy.

What is an ARC (외국인등록증)?

The Alien Registration Card, issued to foreigners staying in Korea past 90 days. It carries a resident-style registration number that Korean apps often ask for in place of a citizen's ID, and it's the credential several delivery sign-up guides say Coupang Eats still wants.

A Korean business-news feature published July 5, 2026, on tourism-tech startups, followed a Chinese tourist by the Han River who tried to order chicken through a delivery app and got stuck at the Korean-number verification step; he ended up borrowing a Korean friend's phone to finish the order. Payment is the easier half once you're past sign-up: Baemin accepts overseas-issued credit cards, Chinese mobile payments and Apple Pay with a foreign card on file.

🍗 What People Actually Order, and What It Costs

Fried chicken and late-night snacks are driving the surge, and a typical order needs to clear about ₩14,000 before delivery even applies. A Korea-wide survey of 502 restaurant and cafe owners, run by the Consumer Public Interest Network in the second half of 2024 and published in mid-2025, found average merchant-set minimum orders of ₩14,079 (about $10.50) on Baemin, ₩14,404 ($10.75) on Coupang Eats and ₩14,724 ($10.99) on Yogiyo, against ~₩1,340 per US dollar in early October 2026.

That's not a small order for one person eating alone, and the survey's own authors made the same point: over a third of owners said they'd raised their minimum specifically because of commission pressure, so the figure keeps drifting up rather than down.

Chicken is still the single biggest winner of the foreign-tourist boom, with orders up 281% year over year in the first half of 2026, and late-night snacks grew fastest of any category at 520%. That pattern has a simple explanation: people finish sightseeing, get back to the guesthouse, and order the one meal a restaurant visit can't easily replace at 11 p.m.

What is K-delivery (K-배달)?

Korea's own shorthand for its delivery culture: a rider on a scooter, usually carrying several bags at once, who parks, calls or texts, and hands the order over at the door. No tip is expected, and it's fast enough that Seoul's own tourism office now markets it as something to try on purpose.

🌃 Where Seoul Is Pushing K-Delivery Right Now

Seoul's city government and Baemin's operator signed an agreement on August 5, 2026 aimed squarely at tourists who'd given up on delivery apps over language and payment. Woowa Brothers, the company behind Baemin, framed it this way: "Customers who hesitated to use the app due to language or payment differences will now be able to enjoy K-delivery anywhere in Seoul with ease," said Yoon Seokjun, the company's chief brand communication strategy officer.

The deal ran alongside a month-long campaign at Yeouido and Ttukseom Hangang parks, Seoul's riverside picnic spots, with multilingual banners, marked night-delivery zones and a ₩3,000 discount coupon for orders over ₩20,000, aimed at the city's "Han River Night+Camping" crowd. It's the kind of push that shows up in the growth numbers directly: foreign-card payments through Baemin were already running about 300% ahead of the year before as of December 2025, then accelerated to roughly 370% year over year by the second quarter of 2026.

My take Getting stuck at a sign-up screen when you're hungry and tired is a specific kind of frustration, worse than a menu with no English, because the app itself is the menu. If I were landing in Seoul without a Korean SIM yet, I'd put Baemin on the phone before the flight, switch it to English, and test the overseas-number verification once while there's still wifi and patience to spare. I'd treat Coupang Eats as a backup for later in the trip, once an ARC or a local number is already sorted, not as the first thing to fight with on night one.

FAQ
What happens if the delivery rider can't reach me directly?
Whoever's number is on the order is who gets the call, which can turn a simple handoff into a relay race if that's not your own phone. The fix costs nothing: list the exact gate, floor or unit in the delivery note field rather than trusting a voice call to sort it out, and keep whoever's number you used free during the delivery window.
I'm only passing through for a few days. Is any of this worth setting up?
Yes, mainly because the setup is close to nothing. A passport holder with nowhere permanent to live here still clears the lightest route, since it only checks that a text message landed, nothing about residency or a card on file. Save the heavier rival for a longer stay, when there's time to sort out a local line or a resident card anyway.

For a first order in Korea, start with Baemin, in English, before dinnertime actually matters. Save Coupang Eats for after you've settled a Korean number or an ARC, if you need the comparison at all.
