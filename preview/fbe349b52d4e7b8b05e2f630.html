Kalguksu in Seoul: Where Locals Actually Eat It

Three Seoul kalguksu shops rated 4.0+ on Google Maps: two Michelin Bib Gourmand regulars in Myeongdong and Bukchon, plus a Sindang Market favorite.

A bowl of kalguksu doesn't look like much until the steam hits your face and you realize the broth has been going since before the kitchen opened. The three shops on this list have nothing in common on paper: one is a 1966 Myeongdong institution with a line that never seems to shrink, one is a Bukchon regular Michelin inspectors keep coming back to, and one is a Sindang Market counter that got popular enough to need a waiting list of its own. What ties them together is the same wide, hand-cut noodle, and the fact that none of them needs a trend to stay full.

What is kalguksu (칼국수)?

Wide wheat noodles, cut by knife rather than extruded, served in a hot broth that can be built from beef bone, chicken, seafood or clams depending on the shop. "Kal" means knife and "guksu" means noodles, which is the whole dish in two words.

🥟 Myeongdong Kyoja: the 1966 bowl with a line that never shrinks

Start at 명동교자 본점 (Myeongdong Kyoja, main branch) and order the ₩12,000 (about $8.98) kalguksu, a short walk from Myeongdong Station on Line 4. The address is 서울 중구 명동10길 29. A newer station-front branch opened at Exit 8 in late 2024, but the original has run from the same address since 1966; the exact exit and walk time for the main branch itself aren't confirmed, so plug the address into a map app.

It's open daily 10:30-21:00, with a last order around 20:30. The menu is short on purpose: kalguksu and mandu year-round, with bibim guksu and a seasonal kongguksu (April to October) rounding it out. Mandu run ₩13,000 (about $9.73) and bibim guksu is ₩12,000.

What is Bib Gourmand (빕 구르망)?

Michelin's tier below a star: good cooking at a reasonable price, decided by the same inspectors who award stars. It's a reliability signal more than a hype badge, which is why a ₩12,000 noodle shop and a tasting-menu restaurant can both carry it.

Myeongdong Kyoja has held a Bib Gourmand since the Michelin Guide Seoul's first selection, and the garlic-heavy house kimchi is made in-house, same as it was decades ago. Michelin's own description notes the line outside is a fixture, not a seasonal spike.

Google Maps rates it 4.2 from 14,934 reviews, checked today.

🏯 Is Hwangsaengga's Bukchon bowl worth the detour?

Yes, if beef-bone broth is what you're after: order the ₩12,000 (about $8.98) sagol kalguksu at 황생가 칼국수 본점 (Hwangsaengga Kalguksu, Bukchon main branch). The address is 서울 종로구 북촌로5길 78, in the Bukchon hanok village area; the nearest station is Anguk on Line 3, but the exact exit isn't confirmed, so check a map app before you go.

It opened in 2001 as Bukchon Kalguksu and was renamed Hwangsaengga Kalguksu in 2014, and it's open daily 11:00-21:30. Wang mandu (large dumplings) run ₩13,000, kongguksu is ₩15,000 and the mushroom hot pot is ₩20,000 (about $14.97).

Like Myeongdong Kyoja, it carries a Michelin Bib Gourmand, and Michelin's own write-up says a line is typically outside no matter what time of day you show up. That's the trade-off with both Bib Gourmand picks on this list: the badge is reliable, but so is the wait.

Google Maps rates it 4.2 from 3,010 reviews, checked today.

🐚 Hani Kalguksu: the Sindang Market shop that got too popular for its own waitlist

Near Sindang Station, Exit 12, order the ₩12,000 (about $8.98) algoni kalguksu (surf clam) at 하니칼국수 (Hani Kalguksu) in the central market's furniture district. The address is 서울 중구 퇴계로 411-15.

What is dongju (동죽)?

A small Korean surf clam. It's cheaper than the bajirak clams used in other seafood kalguksu, and less briny too. Hani cooks it into a lighter, slightly sweet broth that reads differently from the beef-bone bowls on this list.

It's open daily 10:30-22:00, with a break from 14:30 to 17:00 on weekdays (no break on weekends) and last order at 21:00. The dongju version is ₩11,000, and hani bossam (boiled pork) is ₩18,000 (about $13.48). One market write-up calls it the shop people in Sindang now line up for the most, which wasn't true a few years ago.

On paper all three look like the same wide-noodle soup, but the broth underneath splits three ways: Myeongdong Kyoja's is a lighter all-purpose stock, Hwangsaengga's leans on hours of simmered ox bone, and Hani's swaps beef for shellfish entirely. If you only have time for one bowl, decide whether you want beef, clam or "the classic" before you pick a stop, not after.

Say "칼국수 하나 주세요" (kal-guk-su ha-na ju-se-yo, "one kalguksu, please") at any of the three. None has a confirmed English menu, so screenshot the Korean name before you go.

A locked door at 3 p.m. when you'd planned your whole afternoon around one bowl is its own small disaster.

My take I eat out fairly often around Gangnam, Samseong and Apgujeong, and I go to Michelin-listed places fairly regularly, so a Bib Gourmand tag carries real weight for me over a random "best of" list someone wrote for clicks. That's why I'd trust Myeongdong Kyoja or Hwangsaengga on a first visit if you want a safe bet, queue included. If you'd rather skip the Bib Gourmand crowd entirely, Hani is the one worth the trip to Sindang, especially now that the wait has eased off its old peak.

FAQ
Can I phone ahead to check a kitchen isn't mid-break?
Hwangsaengga answers at 02-739-6334, and Hani at 02-3298-6909. No working number turned up for the Myeongdong shop, so that one is simpler to just visit.
Will a foreign credit card work at any of these?
Nobody could confirm it for these three. Carry won instead: one bowl at each location runs ₩35,000 total, close to $26, so ₩40,000 in bills covers every stop with room left over.
