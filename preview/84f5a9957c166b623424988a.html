Suwon Hwaseong: This Year's Festival and What It Costs

Suwon's 63rd Hwaseong Cultural Festival runs Oct 4-11, 2026. The 5.7km fortress wall is free; here's the train, the palace fee, and where to eat nearby.

Quick answer
  • The festival runs a full week: October 4-11, opening with a 1,000-person historical reenactment plus another 1,200 citizen marchers, so a quieter weekday still catches most of the programming.
  • Walking the wall costs nothing: only Haenggung Palace and the Hwaseong Eocha train ride charge admission inside the fortress.
  • Book the train before October 4: that's the day the procession fills the streets you'll be walking.

Half past six on an October Saturday, and drummers are already tuning up in the plaza below Paldalmun, working out a rhythm that will carry sixty horses and over two thousand costumed marchers through the gate by afternoon. A grandmother in a rented hanbok adjusts her granddaughter's hairpin against the stone wall while a food cart's steam drifts over both of them. This is Suwon in its loudest week of the year, and underneath the noise the fortress itself never changes: the same tiled roofline finished in 1796, still standing over a city that grew up around it.

🎊 What's actually happening during festival week?

The 63rd Suwon Hwaseong Cultural Festival runs October 4 to 11, 2026, marking 230 years since the fortress itself was completed. UNESCO's own listing dates the build to 1794-1796, and this year's eight-day program spreads across Haenggung Palace, the Yeonmudae training ground, Yongyeon pond, and the Traditional Culture Center rather than staying parked on one plaza.

The opening event is the one to plan around. On October 4, sixty horses and a thousand costumed performers reenact King Jeongjo's 1795 royal procession, joined by another 1,200 citizen marchers, starting from Noseong district at 12:30 p.m. and passing through Janganmun around 4 p.m. on the way to Haenggung Palace Square.

What is a haenggung (행궁)?

A temporary palace a king used while traveling outside the capital. Hwaseong Haenggung was built so King Jeongjo had somewhere to stay when he visited his father Crown Prince Sado's tomb near Suwon.

Wear hanbok and Haenggung Palace waives its admission fee for the whole festival week. A rented hanbok gets a visitor free entry to the palace, a shot at 29 free seats for the nightly "Haenggung Yahwa" program, and a chance at one of fifty daily tickets to "Yajo," a nighttime military-drill reenactment. Skip the costume and the regular ₩2,000 palace ticket still covers Haenggung Yahwa, so nobody pays for the evening show twice.

🚆 How do you get from Seoul to Suwon?

Take the Mugunghwa or ITX train from Seoul Station: most runs cost ₩4,800 and take 30 to 38 minutes, with 31 departures a day between 05:54 and 22:24. KTX covers the same stretch in a similar time for ₩7,500, which only makes sense if you're already chaining Suwon onto a longer KTX trip.

Line 1 of the metropolitan subway also runs the route with no reservation needed, priced by the network's usual distance formula instead of a flat ticket: a ₩1,550 base fare for the first 10 km, then ₩100 added for every 5 km after that. Book a train seat if you're traveling on October 4, since that's when the procession's thousand-plus performers and their families fill Suwon's streets.

From Suwon Station, board city bus 11 or 13 at Exit 4 or Exit 9 and get off at the "Hwaseong Haenggung, Suwon Seongji" stop, about five minutes on foot from the palace gate.

What's the difference between Mugunghwa, ITX or KTX?

All three are Korail trains on the same tracks. Mugunghwa is the older, cheaper standard-seat train; ITX is a newer train at the same fare tier; KTX is the high-speed service that costs more for a barely shorter ride on a hop this short.

🎫 What actually costs money inside a free fortress?

The 5.7-kilometer wall itself has been free to walk since April 7, 2022, when Suwon gave up on ticketing an open-air site with dozens of entry points. Haenggung Palace is the one building that still charges: ₩2,000 for adults, ₩1,500 for teens and military, and ₩1,000 for children, open 09:00-18:00 with last entry an hour before close.

The palace also stays open late on weekends and holidays from May 1 through November 1, 18:00-21:30 with last entry at 21:00, so an evening visit only works on a Friday, Saturday, or Sunday inside that window.

The Hwaseong Eocha, a replica royal train, loops from Yeonmudae past the main gates and back in about 30 minutes for ₩6,000 a seat, ₩3,500 for teens and military, and ₩2,000 for children. Online booking covers all 36 seats per trip and opens through the end of the following month. Skip it, and the ₩6,000 saved would cover three more trips through Haenggung Palace's own gate at full price.

A block into Haenggung-dong, Jeong Ji-young Coffee Roasters pours a ₩4,000 americano, open daily except Mondays from noon to 10 p.m..

My take A trip that costs almost nothing to enter can still feel like a bad bet if you've built your whole plan around one festival date and it rains, or the procession runs late. I'd treat October 4 as a bonus, not the whole plan: the festival runs eight days, and the wall, the palace, and Jidong Market are exactly as good on the 8th with a third of the crowd. If you can only pick one train time, take an early one and give the wall an hour before the palace's afternoon rush.

FAQ
Is there a cheaper way to Suwon than the ₩4,800 train?
Only sometimes. A transit card charges extra past the first ten kilometers, roughly ₩100 every five, so the total isn't fixed like a printed ticket and depends entirely on your starting point within the network.
Does the Eocha ride have a senior or child discount?
Yes: riders 65 and older, registered disabled guests, and national merit recipients get half off with proof of ID, and a toddler under three sits free on a guardian's lap, though a claimed seat of their own costs full price.

Pick a weekday over the October 4 opening if crowds wear you down, and put the Eocha reservation on your phone before the train from Seoul even leaves the platform. The wall looks the same either way, gate lit up at night, drummers or no drummers.
