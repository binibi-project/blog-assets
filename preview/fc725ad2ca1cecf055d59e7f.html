K-Drama Filming Spots in Seoul: 3 You Can Actually Visit

Bukchon's Goblin alley, Deoksugung's stone-wall path and N Seoul Tower: the subway exit, walk time, hours and cost for each, every figure sourced.

Quick answer
  • Three real spots: Bukchon's hanok lanes (with Goblin's Jungang High School and Yunposun-gil scenes right nearby), Deoksugung's Stone-Wall Road (the same drama's meeting spot), and N Seoul Tower (My Love from the Star's finale).
  • Only one costs real money: Bukchon and the stone-wall sidewalk are free to walk; the tower's observatory ticket is ₩29,000, plus ₩15,000 more if you take the cable car up.
  • Timing decides your day: get to Bukchon before 10 a.m. and check Deoksugung isn't closed for its Monday rest day before you build a route around it.

You've seen the alley before you've ever set foot in Seoul: a hanok roofline dropping in steps toward the skyline, two people meeting under a streetlamp, a wall of stacked stone under autumn trees. Korean dramas return to the same handful of streets so often that the streets have become characters in their own right, and unlike a studio backlot, you can actually stand in them. None of it requires a tour bus or a ticketed package. It requires a subway card, a rough sense of when each place opens, and knowing which street is which before you arrive.

What is a hanok (한옥)?

A traditional Korean house built with wood framing, a tiled or thatched roof, and heated floors (ondol). Bukchon's hanok are still mostly private homes, not museum pieces, which is why so much of the neighborhood's charm comes with a "please be quiet" sign attached.

🏘️ Is Bukchon's famous alley worth the crowd?

Get there before 10 a.m., while the lane is still quiet. Bukchon Hanok Village sits about 516 meters from Exit 3 of Anguk Station (Line 3), roughly a seven-minute walk, according to Visit Seoul's official guide. Visitor entry runs 10:00-17:00; from 17:00 to 10:00 the next morning, the neighborhood's residential lanes are closed to visitors, since real families live behind those gates.

The steep, hanok-lined lane most visitors photograph is Bukchon-ro 11-gil, where the 10:00-17:00 visitor window officially applies. The actual on-screen Goblin connections sit in the same Anguk-dong neighborhood: Jungang High School, a short walk further in, is one of the drama's confirmed filming spots, and Yunposun-gil, past the former president's old residence, is where Kim Shin first appears to Ji Eun-tak on screen.

Bukchon calls its visitor rules "silent tourism": keep groups to 10 people or fewer, skip the megaphones, and don't photograph through open gates into someone's actual living room. Entry is free.

🧱 Why do Koreans warn against walking Deoksugung's stone wall with a date?

Because for decades, the road led straight to a divorce court. One explanation traces the superstition to the old Seoul Family Court, once located where the Seoul Museum of Art stands today, at the far end of this path. Couples going there to file for divorce would have had to walk the stone wall together first, and that's the version most often repeated for why couples who walk it are said to break up. Whether or not you believe it, the path keeps showing up in the show's most quoted meetings, since Goblin filmed several scenes with its two leads along this stretch of wall.

Getting there is simple: City Hall Station (Lines 1 and 2), Exit 2, about a two-minute walk to the palace's stone wall. The sidewalk along the outside of the wall has no gate and no ticket. Going inside the palace grounds is a separate ₩1,000 (about $0.72) adult admission, open 09:00-21:00 with last entry at 20:00, closed every Monday.

What is Doldam-gil (돌담길)?

Literally "stone-wall road," any street that runs alongside a traditional stacked-stone wall. Seoul has a few, but "the" Doldam-gil almost always means this one, the roughly 900-meter stretch bordering Deoksugung.

If you have one more hour here, spend it inside the palace itself. The same ₩1,000 ticket that gets you past the gate covers the full grounds, not just the stretch of wall you already walked for free.

🗼 Is N Seoul Tower's ₩29,000 ticket worth it, or does the cable car cover it?

The cable car gets you to the mountaintop; the observatory ticket is a separate line for going up the tower itself, and you only need both if you actually want the indoor view. At the ticket windows on Namsan you'll see two prices, not one: a Namsan cable car round trip is ₩15,000 (about $10.81) for an adult, running 10:00-23:00, about a ten-minute walk from Exit 3 of Myeongdong Station (Line 4). Riding up the mountain doesn't put you inside the tower; for that, the observatory's own adult ticket is ₩29,000, with a ₩23,000 discounted rate offered on clear-air days. Do both, and you've spent ₩44,000 (about $32) just to see Seoul from two different heights in one afternoon.

This is where Do Min-joon and Cheon Song-yi's love story ends in My Love from the Star: the tower's final-episode embrace, which aired February 12, 2014, is the scene that turned N Seoul Tower's fence lines into one of the city's most photographed spots for padlocks in the years since.

On paper the tower looks like the priciest stop by far, and it is. But it's also the only one of the three where you're paying for something Bukchon and Deoksugung give you for nothing: an actual view from above the city, not just a street to stand on.

What does "silent tourism" mean in Bukchon?

A set of visitor rules (small groups, no loudspeakers, no photos through private gates) adopted after years of residents complaining that a real neighborhood had turned into a photo backdrop. It's not a suggestion; volunteers on the street enforce it.

You don't need all three in one day, and doing all three back to back is a long walk plus a subway transfer or two. Bukchon and the stone-wall road pair naturally, since both are free and both reward an early start; N Seoul Tower is its own half-day once you count the cable car line and the walk up from Myeongdong.

My take I haven't planned a day around chasing drama sets myself, but I've walked both Bukchon and the Deoksugung stone wall more times than I could count just living in Seoul, and the two don't compare on a spreadsheet. Bukchon and the stone-wall road cost nothing between them, however long you linger, so I'd fold them into one free morning before deciding whether the tower's ₩29,000 is worth spending that same day. If I only had time for one paid stop, I'd pick the observatory over the cable car alone. The cable car ride is nice, but it's the view from inside the tower that people are actually flying to Seoul to see.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour: use it inside Deoksugung's grounds. The ₩1,000 ticket covers the whole palace, not just the stretch of wall out front.
  • What locals argue about: whether a residential neighborhood like Bukchon should keep absorbing tourist traffic at all, which is exactly why the "silent tourism" rules exist in the first place.
  • Watch for: N Seoul Tower's discounted ₩23,000 rate isn't automatic. It depends on the day's air quality reading, not just showing up with a ticket in hand.
Check before you go
  • Get to Bukchon before 10 a.m.: visitor entry only runs to 17:00, and the lanes get crowded fast once tour groups arrive.
  • Confirm Deoksugung isn't closed: the palace (not the outer sidewalk) is shut every Monday, with last entry at 20:00 on open days.
  • Buy cable car and observatory tickets separately: they're sold at different counters for different parts of the trip up Namsan.
FAQ
Is Bukchon Hanok Village free to visit?
Yes. Walking the lanes costs nothing; you're only asked to keep quiet and keep your group small, since it's a residential neighborhood.
Do I need a ticket to walk Deoksugung's stone-wall road?
No. The sidewalk along the outside of the wall is open and free; a ticket is only for entering the palace grounds themselves, at ₩1,000 for an adult.

None of these three needs a guided tour to make sense once you know the subway exit and the hours. Start with whichever is closest to wherever your day already has you, and treat N Seoul Tower as the one worth budgeting for on its own.
