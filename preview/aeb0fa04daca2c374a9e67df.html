Inside Korea's K-Pop Photocard Market: What It Costs

A ₩15,000 album's bonus card can resell for ₩160,500. How Korea's photocard trading market actually works, and how fans avoid fakes.

You tear the plastic wrap off, slide out the CD sleeve, and there it is: a small glossy card, face-down, no way to know who's on it until you flip it over. For half a second it could be anyone in the group. Then it's someone, and depending on who, that two-by-three-inch piece of card stock is either exactly what you hoped for or the start of a new search.

That small moment of chance, repeated across millions of albums a year, is the whole engine behind a market most visitors never hear about until they're standing in a Seoul shop full of tablets, each one showing a grid of faces for sale.

What is a photocard (포토카드, poca)?

A credit-card-sized print of one group member, bundled randomly inside a physical album. Fans shorten it to "poca." Since no two fans want the same member's face, a stack of identical albums can produce a stack of completely different outcomes.

💸 Why does a ₩15,000 album's card resell for ₩160,500?

Because the card is scarce in a way the album itself isn't, and buying more albums is the only official way to try again. A standard physical album in Korea runs about ₩13,000-20,000, and that price usually buys a CD, a photobook of 80-120 pages, a poster, and one or two random photocards. Two cheaper formats exist too: Weverse Albums skip the CD for a QR-linked digital photo book and NFC streaming card, at roughly ₩6,000-9,000, and kit albums swap the disc for a small Bluetooth card, at roughly ₩9,000-15,000. All three still hide the same thing inside: one or two random cards, member unknown until you open it.

On paper, that's a cheap way to gamble on a face card. In practice, if the member you want isn't the one you pull, the only paths left are buying another album or paying someone who already has that card.

🛍️ Where fans actually buy and sell cards

Through apps and stores that check a card before it's listed, not through a stranger's message. Pocamarket, a mobile app with Korean and global versions, buys cards from fans, runs its own authenticity check, and only then lists them for other fans to buy. The company also runs physical shops: POCA SPOT, near Hongik University Station in Mapo District and in Myeongdong, where the Hongdae branch alone lets visitors browse more than two million photocards on in-store kiosks and pick up same-day. A smaller, unmanned shop called Pocaboo sits near the same Hongdae station.

What does "random pull" actually mean here?

Each album's photocards come from a set pool, and you can't choose which one lands in your copy. Buy ten albums and you might get ten different members, or the same one twice. That's the randomness fans are either chasing or trying to trade their way around.

Prices at these shops show how wide the gap actually gets. A Karina (aespa) card has been priced at ₩160,500, and a staff member told reporters an An Yu-jin card sold for ₩1,000,000. For a cheaper way in, Pocamarket's blind-box machine, Poca Gacha, sells for ₩49,990 and hands you ten random boy-group cards or six random girl-group cards, and Pocaboo's own lucky-draw machine charges just ₩3,300 per random card. Girl-group cards run pricier than boy-group ones across the board, staff told the paper.

Buying from outside Korea is worth it if you use a service built for cross-border trading, since Korea's biggest secondhand platform just made that easier. Bungaejangter's overseas arm, Bungae Global, saw overseas transactions rise 56% year over year in the first half of 2026. Photocards alone made up 35% of those overseas purchases, and once you count other star goods too, the share rises to 60%, or six in every ten overseas transactions. The average price paid per card was ₩42,000 (about $31 at ₩1,336/$1). BTS, Ateez, Stray Kids and PLAVE cards alone accounted for 34% of all overseas trades, with 11 other groups, including QWER, Illit and CORTIS, making up another 15%.

What is Bungae Global?

The international arm of Bungaejangter, Korea's largest secondhand marketplace. It lets fans outside Korea buy directly from Korean sellers, with the platform handling cross-border shipping and payment instead of leaving that to a private deal.

The growth isn't just more buyers, it's better plumbing. The platform added two new shipping partners, Able Union and FD, alongside its original Delivered Korea, plus real-time tracking from the seller's dispatch to your door, and it added Apple Pay and Google Pay to a payment list that used to stop at credit cards, PayPal, Alipay and WeChat Pay.

🚨 How do I avoid buying a fake?

Stick to a platform that authenticates the card before you pay, because photocards have no hologram, no serial number, nothing printed on them to prove they're real. That gap is exactly why counterfeits move in such volume: Korea's customs service seized over 1.2 million fake photocards between 2023 and August 2025, based on data a lawmaker released on Sept. 30, 2025. IVE cards made up the largest single share at 713,593 seized, followed by NewJeans at 99,888, IU at 77,344, and Stray Kids at 75,404.

Check that a seller or platform actually inspects cards before listing them, rather than taking a seller's word for it. Pocamarket's whole pitch is that it mails-in, checks, then lists, and Bungae Global's growth is built on the same kind of structure, not an open DM thread.

My take Chasing one specific member's face through a run of random pulls is a gamble dressed up as a purchase, and it's worth knowing the odds before starting. If an album is genuinely the only way to get the card you want, buying two or three is a reasonable bet; buying a dozen to force one member out of a random pool is the point where the math stops making sense, since resale usually beats it by then anyway. In your shoes, I'd rather pay a known price on a platform that already checked the card than chase a lower number from a stranger.

FAQ
Is it safer to buy from someone I found through a fan Instagram or Facebook group instead of these apps?
No guarantee exists either way, but a private deal removes the one thing that actually protects you: a third party willing to step in if something goes wrong. Ask whoever you're dealing with how long they've been trading and whether anyone else can vouch for them, since a brand-new account with nothing behind it is the single biggest warning sign collectors bring up. A live video call beats any still image, because reused pictures from someone else's old post are the most common trick going.
Could my photocard package get flagged at customs even if the card is real?
Genuine mail rarely gets stopped; screening targets bulk shipments of obvious fakes, not a single item mailed between two fans. The bigger practical worry is a parcel going missing in transit, which is exactly the gap new tracking features were built to close. Buying somewhere that already vetted the item before it shipped removes both worries at once.

A budget album and a six-figure card are the same hobby wearing two different price tags, and which one you pay depends entirely on how badly you want the face inside to be the one you were hoping for. The next check-in on this is whenever a major comeback lands, to see whether Bungae Global's growth curve holds or whether a bigger platform undercuts it on shipping.
