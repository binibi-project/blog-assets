Three gopchang counters Seoul regulars actually trust, each Google-rated 4.1+ from 30+ reviews, with real prices, hours and how to order.

Grilled gopchang scares off more curious visitors than almost anything else on a Korean barbecue menu, and the counters that have kept serving it for decades never needed a hashtag to prove themselves. At a good one, the smell reaches the sidewalk before the smoke does: char, garlic, a faint mineral note that either pulls you through the door or sends you back out. The draw was never bravery. It's a soft, faintly sweet bite that nothing else on a Korean grill quite replicates, and three very different Seoul neighborhoods each have a counter worth trusting with it.

What is gopchang (곱창)?

Small intestine, usually beef, grilled whole or in a hot pot. The chewy exterior and the soft, faintly sweet "gop" (fat and marrow left inside) are the whole point; daechang (대창, large intestine) and makchang (막창, abomasum) are the thicker, fattier cousins often ordered at the same table.

🥘 Why does Gangnam's 24-hour haejang spot keep a gopchang hot pot on the menu?

중앙해장 (Jungang Haejang) in Daechi-dong is open nearly around the clock, and its gopchang jeongol (곱창전골) is the reason regulars come back after the soup crowd goes home. The restaurant sits about 220 meters in a straight line from Samseong Station on Line 2, close enough that a late dinner doesn't mean a long walk back. Its Google listing shows hours of Tuesday through Saturday 24 hours, Monday 11:00 to midnight, and Sunday midnight to 20:30, so the only real gap in the week is Sunday evening.

The hot pot is priced by size for the table rather than a single confirmed per-person number, so ask at the counter how many it feeds before you order; menus also list yangseon-ji haejang-guk and naejang-tang, plus fried rice to finish. Order with "곱창전골 하나 주세요" (gop-chang-jeon-gol ha-na ju-se-yo, "one gopchang hot pot, please"). The number is 02-558-7905.

Google Maps rating 4.3 from 5,782 reviews (checked 2026-11-27).

🔥 Is Mullae's garlic gopchang worth the trip to a factory district?

Yes, if you want the grilled version done with a heavy hand on garlic: 원조마늘곱창 (Wonjo Garlic Gopchang) sits in Mullae's small-factory neighborhood, about 530 meters in a straight line from Mullae Station on Line 2. ELLE Korea's roundup names singer Sung Si-kyung as the one who started the buzz around this specific shop, and its Google rating has held at 4.9 since.

Its Google listing shows hours of 16:00-22:30 Monday through Thursday, 16:00-23:00 Friday, 12:30-00:00 Saturday, and 12:30-22:00 Sunday, with no closed day. A food blogger's October 2024 visit priced the garlic gopchang at ₩25,000 for 200 grams, with the grilled daepa daechang at the same ₩25,000 for 300 grams, so expect those figures to have crept up some since.

What does "대파대창" (daepa daechang) mean on a gopchang menu?

Daechang (대창, beef large intestine) grilled with a heavy pile of daepa, Korea's long green onion. It's a common second order at a gopchang table, milder and fattier than gopchang itself.

Say "마늘곱창 2인분 주세요" (ma-neul-gop-chang i-in-bun ju-se-yo, "garlic gopchang for two, please"). The shop's number is 02-2678-0170.

Google Maps rating 4.9 from 471 reviews (checked 2026-11-27).

🏮 Does City Hall's 1972 gopchang counter still open on weekends?

No, and that's the detail worth knowing before you plan around it: 한성 (Hansung), a 곱창전골 counter near City Hall that's been in the same Jung-gu storefront since 1972, only opens Monday through Friday. Esquire Korea's 2021 write-up called it a City Hall-area noodle-era holdover still drawing a lunch crowd after nearly five decades, and its Google listing today confirms weekday-only hours of 11:00-21:30 with Saturday and Sunday closed. It's about 430 meters in a straight line from City Hall Station.

Esquire Korea's 2021 piece priced the gopchang jeongol at ₩15,000, next to ₩29,000 for grilled loin cuts and ₩24,000 for grilled stomach, so treat that number as a floor rather than today's receipt.

Order with "곱창전골 주세요" (gop-chang-jeon-gol ju-se-yo, "gopchang hot pot, please"). The number is 02-752-2056.

Google Maps rating 4.1 from 413 reviews (checked 2026-11-27).

On paper, Mullae looks like the priciest stop, but it's also the only one of the three with a Google rating in the high 4s and a published hour-by-hour schedule you can actually plan a late dinner around. The other two are cheaper on record, except both of those prices are years old, so the gap may already be smaller than the table suggests.

Every English write-up found on this topic covers exactly one trendy shop, prices it once, and stops there. None of them cross-check a Google rating, pair a Korean name with directions a taxi driver can read, or flag that a listed price is years out of date before handing it to a reader as current.

FAQ
Will my foreign card work at any of these?
Nothing published confirms it either way for any of the three, so keep some cash on hand just in case. Settling up through a phone payment app sidesteps the question entirely if the till won't take plastic.
Is any of this realistic to order solo?
Not really: every listed figure here is sized for two or more, and none of the three publish a single-diner option. Bring company, or call ahead and ask whether a smaller portion exists before you commit to the trip.

If a weekend trip is the only option, Mullae is the safe bet of the three: it's open every day of the week, it's a short walk from a major Line 2 station, and its rating is the strongest on record. The City Hall counter is worth building a weekday lunch around, not a weekend plan.
