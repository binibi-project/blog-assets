Naejangsan's Fall Peak Hits Nov 7: What It Costs to Go

Jeongeup's 2026 city plan puts Naejangsan's peak at Nov 7-8. The KTX fare, the free park and temple, and what the cable car actually costs.

Quick answer
  • Naejangsan turns last: Jeongeup's own 2026 plan puts the peak at November 7-8, roughly two and a half weeks behind Seoraksan.
  • Getting in costs nothing: the park itself has been free since 2007, and Naejangsa's temple fee joined it in 2023.
  • Beat the rush: book the 1h40m KTX from Yongsan before Jeongeup's Oct 31-Nov 15 crowd window, when the city expects around 350,000 visitors.

Ever stood under a tree so red it tints the light falling on your own hands? At Naejangsan, that's not one tree but a whole corridor of them: a paved path arched over with maples so thick the sky disappears, the ground below holding the same color as the branches above. Eleven species of native Korean maple grow here, enough to see nearly every maple variety the country has in one stretch of park.

Locals call the smallest, reddest leaves aegi danpung (애기단풍), "baby maple," because they're no bigger than a newborn's palm. It's the kind of red that makes people drive three hours for one afternoon.

Two different offices have now put a date on that afternoon, and they don't quite agree, which matters more than it sounds like for anyone booking a train this week.

🍁 When does Naejangsan actually peak this year?

Jeongeup city's 2026 plan expects the color to start around October 29 and peak November 7-8, the window its traffic and safety office is building its whole autumn operation around. That's a city watching its own mountain up close, not a national model.

The Korea Forest Service's nationwide forecast, published a few weeks earlier, puts Naejangsan's peak slightly sooner, on November 4, about a week behind Songnisan and two days ahead of Hallasan.

What is aegi danpung (애기단풍)?

The small-leafed Korean maple that colors both Naejangsan and nearby Baekyangsa a deeper red than most other mountains manage, named for leaves no bigger than a baby's hand. It's thinner than ordinary maple too, which is part of why the color reads so bright against the light.

🚄 Getting from Seoul, and timing around the crowd

Take the KTX from Yongsan, not the bus, if the schedule lines up: the fastest train covers the route in 1 hour 40 minutes for ₩35,200 in general seating. KTX-산천 401 leaves Yongsan at 05:08 and reaches Jeongeup at 06:48, and several later KTX-산천 departures match that same 1h40m run throughout the day.

The intercity bus from Seoul's Central City Terminal runs seven times a day, first departure 07:00 and last at 20:00, taking close to three hours: ₩26,500 for a standard (우등) seat, or ₩20,400 on the single 18:00 general-class (일반) run.

What's the last stretch from Jeongeup into the park?

Jeongeup's train and bus stations both sit in town, not at the mountain. From there, city bus 171 reaches the park in about 45 minutes, but it runs on a wide gap between buses, so a 20-minute taxi is the more reliable option on a tight schedule.

From Jeongeup, that last leg is the one number none of the official sites publish a fare for, so budget a flat taxi fare rather than count on the bus timing.

Plan to arrive outside Oct 31-Nov 15 if you want a quiet trail: Jeongeup is budgeting for roughly 350,000 visitors and 4,388 person-days of staff across its 28-day watch period, October 26 to November 22. The intensive-management stretch inside that window, October 31 to November 15, is when the city expects the heaviest traffic.

From October 30 to November 15, the park office closes part of the road to private cars and runs a free shuttle between the Naejangho parking lot and Wolryeong Bridge instead. If a packed access road sounds like the opposite of the quiet walk you pictured, that shuttle is the fix: park at Naejangho, ride in, and skip the search for a spot closer to the gate.

🎫 What a day at Naejangsan actually costs

The park and Naejangsa temple are both free to enter, but the cable car and a sit-down meal aren't, so budget for those instead. Naejangsan has charged no admission since January 1, 2007, when Korea abolished entrance fees at national parks nationwide. Naejangsa went further in 2023: it's named among the 65 temples nationwide whose cultural heritage viewing fee became free on May 4 that year, so the temple grounds on the main path cost nothing either.

What is Uhwajeong (우화정)?

The blue-roofed pavilion that sits on a small island in Naejangho reservoir, the pond most of Naejangsan's postcard photos come from. It marks one end of the main walking route, and its reflection in the water is what most visitors are actually chasing when they say they're going to see the "lake".

The Naejangsan cable car runs from the visitor center up to a midway viewpoint on Yeonjabong peak, with round-trip tickets listed at ₩10,000 for adults and ₩7,000 for children, one-way at ₩7,000 and ₩5,000. Other listings online quote different numbers for the same ride, so call the operator at 063-538-8120 to confirm the day's fare before you queue.

My take Two forecasts and one 350,000-visitor estimate point the same direction: the Nov 7-8 weekend is the single worst time to arrive, whichever date turns out to be right. In your shoes, the Monday right after it is the smarter booking, when the color is still close to full but the crowd has gone back to work. A KTX seat that morning beats the bus by three hours, right when standard-class seats are the first to sell out.

FAQ
What if I can't make the Nov 7-8 window?
Go a few days early or late and the trip still pays off. "Peak" is only the point where at least half a canopy has already turned, a threshold set because the display builds for roughly two weeks beforehand and lingers for several days afterward. A visit the Tuesday or Wednesday right after Jeongeup's window should land past that halfway mark without the weekend crush.
Is the cable car worth the ride?
Only if the view matters more than the walk. The ride covers the lower climb to a midway viewpoint on Yeonjabong, not the summit, so hikers headed further up the ridge are paying for a shortcut they'll still have to continue past on foot. If you're short on time, it's the easier ₩10,000 than a steep staircase hike would be.

The numbers point the same way twice over: go in the week after the Nov 7-8 weekend if you can choose, and book the morning KTX before the standard-class bus becomes the only seat left. Prices and hours were checked on 2026-10-03; they change, so double-check before you go.
