Jokbal in Seoul: Where Locals Actually Eat It

Jangchung-dong's boiled original versus Changsin Market's spicy grilled version: three shops, their prices, hours and the Google ratings that back them up.

Quick answer
  • Jokbal means two different meals here: Jangchung-dong boils and slices it, Changsin Market debones it and grills it with a spicy glaze.
  • A plate runs ₩29,000-40,000: the cheapest is Changsin Market's mini portion, the most is Jangchung-dong's two-person medium size.
  • Go on a weekday: skip Monday, since it closes two of these three shops at once.

The smell hits you before the alley does: soy and ginger and simmering fat, the kind of smell that's been sitting in the same pots in Jangchung-dong for longer than most of its customers have been alive. Order a plate there and the shop will tell you, almost proudly, that the broth under your trotters started cooking decades ago and has never fully stopped.

Ten minutes away by subway, in a different Seoul altogether, the pork at Changsin Market goes a completely different direction. It's boiled first, then boned out and pressed onto a wire rack over flame with a sauce that'll make your lips tingle before the second bite. Both are called jokbal. Neither one apologizes for what it is.

What is jokbal (족발)?

Pig's feet and lower legs, slow-braised in soy sauce with ginger and spices until the skin turns glossy and the meat falls off the bone in slices. It's a late-night, share-the-plate food, usually ordered with lettuce leaves for wrapping and a sharp side of radish water kimchi to cut the richness.

🥘 Jangchung-dong: the broth that never stopped boiling

평안도족발집 (Pyeongando Jokbal) sells the boiled original, a medium plate for two at ₩40,000 (about $29.97), in the alley across from the old Taegeukdang bakery in Jangchung-dong. The address is 서울 중구 장충단로 174-6, and the nearest station is Dongguk University Station on Line 3, though the exact exit and walk time aren't confirmed. A visitor review puts the two-person medium portion at ₩40,000, ordered alongside a bowl of makguksu (buckwheat noodles).

The shop opened in 1961, not as a jokbal restaurant but as a bindaetteok (mung-bean pancake) stall, according to Biz Hankook's history of the alley. It was run by a North Korean refugee named 이경순 and her sister-in-law, both of whom had fled Pyongan province. Customers got tired of pancakes alone, so by the late 1960s the stall started boiling trotters too, and that side dish became the whole business.

The broth itself is the real recipe: each day's leftover liquid is topped up with water and soy sauce and ginger and onion, then boiled again. The owner said in a 2016 interview, when she was 84, that the cycle had already run for more than 40 years.

It's open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00-21:00, last order 20:10, and closed Mondays; the listed phone is 02-2279-9759. Order with "족발 중자 주세요" (jokbal jung-ja ju-se-yo, "medium jokbal, please"). Google Maps rates it 4.2 from 1,478 reviews, checked 2026-10-16.

What does wonjo (원조) mean?

"The original." Korean restaurant signs use it constantly, and Jangchung-dong's jokbal alley is full of shops claiming the title. This one's sign goes further and calls itself "원조의 원조," the original of the originals, a claim the shop next door would dispute.

🌶️ Is Changsin Market's spicy jokbal worth the detour?

Yes, if you like heat: 창신동매운족발 (Changsindong Spicy Jokbal) debones a boiled trotter and grills the meat on a wire rack with a hot glaze, and a front-leg portion runs ₩36,000 (about $26.97). The address is 서울 종로구 종로51길 23, inside Changsin Market a short walk from Dongdaemun Station (Lines 1 and 4), though the exact exit isn't confirmed. It's open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:30-22:00, closed Mondays. A back-leg portion is ₩33,000, and the spicy boneless chicken feet side is ₩14,000.

A food guide says MBC announcer Kim Dae-ho visited on the variety show I Live Alone, and that the shop was already a destination for spicy-food fans before that. Order with "매운석쇠순살족발 앞다리 주세요" (mae-un seok-swe sul-sal jok-bal ap-da-ri ju-se-yo, "spicy grilled front-leg jokbal, please"). Google Maps rates it 4.1 from 919 reviews, checked 2026-10-16.

What is seokswe-gui (석쇠구이)?

Wire-rack grilling. The meat is already cooked from boiling, so the rack is there to char the surface and set the sauce, not to cook the pork through. It's the step that turns a plate of soft braised trotter into something closer to barbecue.

A second shop inside the same market, 옥천매운족발 (Okcheon Spicy Jokbal), is worth knowing about if you're eating alone or just want to taste rather than commit to a full plate. Its address is 서울 종로구 종로51나길 23, also inside Changsin Market near Dongdaemun Station. It's open daily, 10:00-24:00, closed Tuesdays, the one day of the week the other spicy shop is open and this one isn't.

A large mini-jokbal portion is ₩29,000 (about $21.73), and boneless chicken feet and spicy grilled pork belly are each ₩14,000. Google Maps rates it 4.1 from 262 reviews, checked 2026-10-16.

The three prices aren't actually comparable, because the portions aren't built the same way. Pyeongando's medium is sized for two people sharing one plate; Okcheon's "large" mini-jokbal is closer to a single generous serving. Order the wrong size expecting the other shop's math and you'll either run short or take half of it home.

My take Picking one jokbal to represent "Korean jokbal" is the wrong move, because the boiled and the grilled versions aren't really competing for the same craving. If I only had one stop, I'd take the detour into Changsin Market for the spicy grilled version: it's a few minutes off a route tourists already walk, and the smaller portions at Okcheon mean you're not committing to a whole plate just to try it. Save the slower, broth-first version in Jangchung-dong for a day when you've got nowhere else to be, because that one rewards sitting down rather than grabbing a bite between subway stops.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Walk a few doors down from Pyeongando to 뚱뚱이할머니집, the shop it once shared an owner with. It didn't clear this guide's 4.0 rating bar (3.8 from 802 reviews on Google Maps), but it's the other half of the same rivalry and worth tasting for comparison.
  • What locals argue about Who actually gets to call themselves Jangchung-dong's "원조." Pyeongando and its former partner shop split from a single business run by two women, and both now sell themselves as the original.
  • Watch for Monday. It closes both Pyeongando and Changsindong Spicy Jokbal on the same day, which only leaves Okcheon open if you show up expecting a full jokbal crawl.
FAQ
Does it matter which part of the trotter I order?
Its founder said in 2016 that apdari (앞다리) tastes best cold and dwitdari (뒷다리) tastes best warm. Ask for apdari to take out, dwitdari to eat right there.
Will my card work, or should I bring cash?
None of the listings checked for this guide confirm card payment, so carry cash. If you're low on won, a convenience store or bank machine is never far in this kind of dense, older shopping street.
