What an Official K-Pop Fan Club Membership Actually Costs

JYP cut Stray Kids' kit price from ₩22,000 to ₩10,000 after January backlash. What official fan club membership actually buys, and what it never has.

Quick answer
  • It's cheap but uneven: official membership typically runs ₩10,000-35,000 ($7.55-$26.42) a year, and JYP still cut Stray Kids' kit price in half mid-sale this January after fans objected.
  • It buys priority, not guarantees: earlier entry to weekly music-show tapings and a 1-2 day head start on fan-meeting tickets, but never a fansign and not always a concert seat.
  • Paying doesn't lock in access: read the current notice before you buy, since a membership bought in good faith can still land you outside a cap, the way it did for BLACKPINK's own 10th-anniversary event.

Stray Kids fans didn't stay quiet when they saw the price on the sixth-generation STAY membership kit. By January 24, 2026, member Hyunjin was on Instagram Live telling fans he'd pushed back on the company, though the call wasn't his to make. The next day, JYP's fan platform apologized and cut the price.

It's a small story about a cardboard box of photocards and a keyring, but it says more about what an official fan club membership actually is than any price list does: a yearly bet on a company's goodwill, sold mostly on trust.

What is an official fan club, and how is it different from a fancafe?

A fancafe is a free online community, historically hosted on Daum, where anyone can read news; leveling up (sometimes paid, sometimes just active posting) unlocks chat and sub-boards. An official fan club is a separate, always-paid membership an agency sells directly, usually through Weverse or an app like JYP's FANS, that comes with real-world perks like presale access. Many companies now run both, or have folded the fancafe's functions into their app.

💳 What membership actually costs

A year of official membership has typically run ₩10,000-35,000 ($7.55-$26.42), and the physical kit that ships with it is usually priced separately. The exact number depends on the group and the year, which is exactly what makes a sudden mid-sale change like Stray Kids' feel so out of line with what fans expected to pay.

Stray Kids' own STAY 6th generation, covered in Soompi's report on the price cut, shows how unsettled that pricing still is. The kit was always separate from the membership fee itself, which is why JYP could cut just the kit's price the next day, from ₩22,000 to ₩10,000.

JYP also dropped a planned album plush keyring from the kit and pushed sign-up back a day, to "stabilize the system and operational schedules". FANS, the platform that ran the sign-up, said it would "take full responsibility" and apologized for giving fans too little notice.

What's actually "the kit"?

A physical box of membership goods: usually a membership card, a set of photocards, and one or two extras that change each generation, like a keyring or a poster. It ships once per sign-up and is separate from any ongoing digital perks the membership unlocks.

✅ What it guarantees, and what it doesn't

Membership reliably buys two things: priority entry to weekly music-show tapings and a 1-2 day head start on fan-meeting tickets before the general public. Fansign entry has never come bundled with simple membership, and early access to a group's own domestic concerts is inconsistent from group to group. Where a membership-gated concert presale does exist, it's now typically the first of three windows before a seat goes to the general public: domestic members first, then international members, then everyone else. That order, and the specific price tiers it unlocks, gets a full breakdown in How K-Pop Concert Tickets Actually Work in Korea.

Even a paid membership can still leave you outside the room. For BLACKPINK's 10th debut anniversary this August, YG capped the celebration at 40 fans, open only to applicants who'd held a Weverse membership since July 31. The notice landed two days before the date itself, and fans pointed out that Weverse had seen almost no live content since 2024, so the membership they'd kept paying for had bought them little until that single 40-person lottery.

If a sign-up window has already closed, or the lottery odds just aren't worth it to you, Hongdae and Myeongdong are where a lot of fans shop instead: Seoul's two dedicated photocard stores sit in those neighborhoods, a backup economy covered in full in Inside Korea's K-Pop Photocard Market.

What does a "fan club presale" actually sit on top of?

A block of tickets an agency reserves for verified members before anyone else can buy. For a big act, that block is most of the good seats, so general sale is largely what members didn't take. Membership gets you into the draw; it doesn't guarantee you win it.

Joining from outside Korea is mostly fine now, too, since membership has moved onto apps like Weverse and FANS, though a Korean phone number can still matter for some of what it unlocks. As recently as 2016, YG ran fan clubs that excluded international fans outright, and JYP split membership into a domestic Gold tier with full perks and an international Silver tier without music-show or fan-meeting priority. That older, closed structure doesn't match how YG itself now runs things: the BLACKPINK anniversary event above sold through Weverse to any paying member, no mention of a nationality or domestic-only rule. What hasn't gone away is the domestic-first order on concert tickets themselves, which still runs through separate local and global presale pages.

🤔 Is it actually worth paying for?

Only if you'll use what it unlocks within the year you're paying for. A 2023 report found real complaints behind that math: Rocket Punch fans got exactly one meet-and-greet across three paid membership periods, and GFriend's members held active memberships through the group's 2021 inactivity, with refunds only offered after backlash, first as Weverse Shop points and then, after more pushback, as cash. The same report found HYBE already shifting its groups toward lighter digital memberships, with physical kits sold as an optional add-on rather than bundled in by default.

My take Treat the fee the way you'd treat a subscription that's easy to forget about: fine if there's a specific thing this year's membership buys, like a tour already on the calendar, risky if you're hoping it pays off eventually. If I were deciding, I'd buy the smallest tier that still gets into the presale window rather than the full kit, and let that one event decide whether a bigger membership is worth it next year. The Stray Kids and BLACKPINK stories land on the same lesson either way: the price on the sign-up page is the easy part to check. What the membership actually delivers that year is the part you're trusting the company to get right.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more day before a sign-up window closes, read the current notice in full rather than last year's guide; Stray Kids' own kit price moved by more than half in a single day this January.
  • What locals argue about whether fan clubs still mean anything once a group barely uses the platform tied to it; Korea JoongAng Daily quotes one critic who wants agency-run clubs to go back to being "pure," run by fans rather than companies.
  • Watch for the renewal window itself: most groups still open sign-ups only once a year for a set stretch, so missing it can mean waiting a full year for another shot.
FAQ
Why did fans get so upset over a box that cost about seven dollars either way?
The dollar figure was never really the point. What stung was finding a bigger charge waiting with zero warning, after money had already changed hands under different terms. A company that can quietly raise a price mid-sale can do it again, which is the trust problem underneath the small math.
Can someone overseas even pay for this without a Korean bank card?
Usually, yes — a foreign card typically clears fine through apps like Weverse or FANS, since both are built for buyers outside the country. What a traveler can still run into is a currency surcharge or a failed charge from a card issuer flagging an unfamiliar overseas vendor, so confirming the card works before a short trip beats discovering it at checkout.

The honest math is simple: a membership is a cheap mistake if the group goes quiet on you, and genuinely good value if even one presale or fan meeting lands while you hold it. Check what's actually scheduled before you pay, not just what the membership page promises.
