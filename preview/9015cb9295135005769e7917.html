BIFF 2026: How to Get to Busan, Tickets, and Costs

Quick answer
  • Oct 6-15, 2026: the 31st BIFF runs 10 days at Busan Cinema Center in Centum City, its first edition since being named a FIAPF "A-list" festival.
  • The train just got cheaper: the KTX from Seoul to Busan dropped to ₩54,400 one-way on Sept 1, 2026, after Korail and SRT merged their fares.

Every October, Centum City's glass towers turn into a backdrop for a very long line, a red carpet, and ten days of the newest Korean cinema before anyone else outside Korea has seen it. Under a roof that holds the Guinness World Record for the longest cantilever anywhere, 85 meters with no support underneath it, directors take questions after their own screenings and hand-carry pieces of paper for someone to sign. Locals from Haeundae stand in line next to visitors who flew in for this one week alone. If a Korean film you loved ever made you wonder where its cast goes to be applauded first, this is usually the place.

This year the festival is carrying a new title into its 31st edition, and that changes who's likely to show up.

🎬 Is BIFF 2026 worth planning a trip around?

Yes, and this year more than most. The Busan International Film Festival runs October 6 through October 15, 2026, ten days centered on Busan Cinema Center in Centum City, Haeundae-gu. It's the 31st edition of a festival founded in 1995, and this year it opens carrying real weight: in March 2026 FIAPF, the international federation of film producer associations, named BIFF one of just 17 "A-list" festivals worldwide, putting it in the same certification tier as Cannes, Berlin and Venice, and making it the first Korean festival on that list.

What does FIAPF's "A-list" actually mean?

FIAPF replaced its old four-tier ranking with a single "A-list" in 2026, scored on film selection, industry connections, media reach and audience turnout over two years. Only 17 festivals hold it. For a visitor, the practical effect is a bigger, more competitive program: more premieres, more industry guests, and tighter ticket demand than a normal year.

The lineup backs that up. This year's edition officially invited 246 films from 59 countries, its largest slate since 2019, including 95 world premieres. The opening film, directed by Kim Jong-kwan, screens October 6 at 6 PM at the Busan Cinema Center Roof Theater.

🚆 How do you get from Seoul to the festival, and what does it cost?

Take the KTX. It's the only realistic option if you're doing this as a day trip or a short festival weekend rather than a full relocation. The fastest direct Seoul-Busan KTX covers the route in about 2 hours 15 minutes, with most trains running closer to 2 hours 30 to 2 hours 50 minutes depending on stops. That guide's schedule figures come from a single travel-guide source, not an official Korail timetable, so treat the exact first and last train times as approximate rather than fixed.

The fare itself just moved. On September 1, 2026, Korail and SRT merged their ticketing and pricing, cutting the average nationwide KTX fare by about 10%; on the Seoul-Busan route specifically, the one-way standard-class fare dropped from ₩59,800 to ₩54,400.

Why did KTX and SRT merge their fares?

KTX (run by Korail) and SRT (run by a separate operator) used to compete on the same Seoul-Busan tracks with different prices and separate booking apps. As of September 2026 they're sold together at one fare, which is why the price moved down toward what used to be the cheaper SRT rate.

From Busan Station, the venue is one transfer away. Busan Station sits on Busan Metro Line 1; ride it toward Seomyeon, change there to Line 2, and get off at Centum City Station, Exit 6, which is about a nine-minute walk from Busan Cinema Center. A single Busan Metro ride on a transit card costs ₩1,600 for one fare section, the rate in place since May 3, 2024.

Why not just fly or take the express bus? Because for a one-day or overnight festival trip, the KTX's short, frequent, walk-to-the-train service beats waiting at either end of a flight, and it drops you closer to Centum City than Gimhae Airport does.

🎟️ Tickets: prices, sale dates, and what a full day costs

A general screening ticket is ₩10,000, and most of what you'll buy costs the same or less. General screenings and Cine Class talks are ₩10,000; Actors House sessions and Master Classes are ₩15,000; the Midnight Passion late-night slot is ₩20,000; and the opening and closing ceremonies, the hardest tickets to get, are ₩30,000.

Tickets open in two waves. The first, on September 17 at 2 PM KST, covers the opening and closing ceremonies, Open Cinema, Midnight Passion, Actors House and Community BIFF. The second, September 21 at 2 PM KST, opens general screenings, Master Classes and Cine Class, the tickets most visitors will actually be buying. All of it runs through the official portal, ticket.biff.kr.

General screening vs. Open Cinema: what's the difference?

A general screening is an ordinary indoor showing in one of the festival's theaters, the ticket most visitors buy. Open Cinema is the festival's outdoor, large-screen program, usually reserved for higher-profile films and drawing bigger crowds. Both currently cost the same ₩10,000, but they go on sale in different phases.

Add food and a coffee, and a single festival day is cheap by big-festival standards.

On paper the KTX looks like most of the day's budget, and it is. But two general-screening tickets, a subway ride and a meal near the venue still add up to less than a single opening-ceremony seat.

My take I've been to Busan a handful of times in the last two years, though never during festival week. If I were building this trip from Seoul, I'd take the earliest KTX I could stand waking up for and come home the same night rather than book a Haeundae hotel during the festival's busiest stretch. A ₩10,000 general-screening ticket is cheap enough that I'd rather catch two ordinary films on a weekday than spend the morning fighting the queue for an opening-ceremony seat. The city is worth the trip even on the days the ticket app locks you out.

Check before you go
  • Book the KTX before the ticket: reserve your Seoul-Busan train seat first, since festival-week trains fill up separately from BIFF's own ticket rush.
  • Make your BIFF account early: register on ticket.biff.kr before Sept 21 so you're not creating a login while the good showtimes disappear.

FAQ
Can I do BIFF as a day trip from Seoul?
Yes, if you plan around one or two screenings. The fastest KTX covers Seoul-Busan in about 2 hours 15 minutes each way, which leaves a full afternoon and evening at the festival before a late train home.
Do I need to buy tickets before I arrive in Busan?
Yes. General screening tickets go on sale online at ticket.biff.kr starting September 21 at 2 PM KST, and popular slots can sell out the same day, so buy before you travel rather than at the venue.

BIFF's new A-list status is mostly a story about the festival's standing in the film industry, not about how a visitor's day runs: you'll still queue for the same general-screening tickets, ride the same Line 2 to Centum City, and pick between a beach morning and an extra screening. Grab a Sept 21 login reminder for general tickets and book your KTX seat before you do anything else.
