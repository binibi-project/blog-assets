Gangneung Day Trip: The KTX, the Coffee Street and the Cost

The direct KTX from Cheongnyangni puts Gangneung's coffee street and Chodang's tofu village about 90 minutes from Seoul. Fares, this year's Coffee Festival dates, and the full cost.

Quick answer
  • The Cheongnyangni KTX is the fast way in: ₩26,000 one way, about 86 minutes, no transfer needed.
  • Coffee, a lake and a tofu village fill one day: Anmok's coffee street, Gyeongpo Lake's flat shore path and Chodang's sundubu cluster all sit within a short bus ride of each other.
  • Time it with the festival if you can: book the KTX early for Oct 21-25, when this year's Gangneung Coffee Festival turns the street into its biggest weekend.

One coffee street, forty-some years old, and a KTX ride short enough to put you back in Seoul in time for dinner. Gangneung didn't plan to become Korea's coffee city: it started with vending machines lined up along Anmok Beach in the 1980s, coins clinking into a slot before a date on the sand, according to Segye Ilbo's account of the city's coffee culture. A couple walks that stretch today holding actual pour-overs instead, the ocean unchanged behind them. That gap between the plain original and the version worth a day trip now is exactly what makes the stop worth it.

What is KTX-Eum (KTX-이음)?

The newer KTX variant that runs non-dedicated lines like the one to Gangneung, including the Jungang Line out of Cheongnyangni. It isn't the bullet-shaped express you'd catch out of Seoul Station, and it's a bit slower on paper, but on this particular route it's also the station closer to where most Seoul mornings actually start.

🚄 How do you get to Gangneung, and what's the ride like?

A standard-class seat from Cheongnyangni Station runs ₩26,000 one way, and the fastest train covers it in about 86 minutes. That's the route to take if you're starting anywhere near eastern Seoul. It skips the longer run through Seoul Station, where the same ride costs ₩27,600 and takes close to two hours instead. Trains leave roughly every hour, with the first departure around 05:00 and the last around 22:30, so an early train and a late one both fit comfortably inside a single day.

From Gangneung Station, the last stretch to the coast is a short bus ride, not a second train. City bus 202 reaches Anmok Beach in about 20 minutes on the standard city fare, and once you're there, the Sea Tea tourist bus picks up the rest of the coastline: 16 round trips a day along the 23 km stretch from Anmok Coffee Street past Gyeongpo Beach to Jumunjin Beach, at the same fare as any other city bus. If a bus doesn't line up with your schedule, Kakao T calls a taxi for the same hop in less time.

☕ The coffee street, the lake and Chodang's tofu village

Anmok's coffee street isn't a marketing label; it's four decades of the same beach slowly turning into a coffee town. Barista Park Yi-chu, one of Korea's first professional baristas, settled in Gangneung in 2000 and brought hand-drip coffee culture with him. Terarosa's roasting facility opened two years later in the city's Gujeong area and became a destination of its own. What started as a dating spot built on vending-machine coffee is now a working coffee industry with a street-length view of the East Sea in front of it.

What is Anmok (안목)?

The name of the beach itself, in Gangneung's old port district, well before the coffee shops arrived. Locals still use it that way; "Anmok Coffee Street" is the newer, tourist-facing name for the same stretch of sand.

This year's Gangneung Coffee Festival runs October 21-25, its 18th edition, centered on Anmok Coffee Street and spreading to spots around the city like Jumunjin and Ojukheon. It's grown into one of the region's bigger draws, pulling in more than 300,000 visitors a year at its peak. Walking the street and watching the open-air programs costs nothing; it's only the hands-on cupping and brewing classes that come with a separate ticket, per the festival's own "partially paid" listing. Book the KTX early if you're aiming for that week, since a 300,000-visitor festival weekend fills the obvious trains first.

Gyeongpo Lake's shoreline path is flat, free and a genuine break from the coffee crowds. The full loop runs about 4.3 km, with almost no incline, split between a walking path and a separate bike lane: fifteen to twenty minutes by bike if you're moving, closer to an hour and a half with stops for photos. It sits close enough to Anmok that the same Sea Tea bus passes both, so there's no separate trip to plan for.

A fifteen-minute ride past the lake, Chodang village has been making tofu with seawater instead of salt since the 16th century. The method traces back to Heo Yeop, a Joseon-era scholar and the father of Heo Gyun, the writer behind The Tale of Hong Gildong. He soaked beans in spring water from his home and set them with seawater drawn from in front of Gyeongpo, because salt itself was scarce at the time. About 20 sundubu restaurants now cluster around that same village entrance. At Donghwa Garden, one of the longer-running shops, a bowl of the plain spicy version runs ₩11,000, and the seafood-loaded jjamppong sundubu is ₩15,000.

What is sundubu (순두부)?

Soft, uncurdled tofu, soft enough to eat by the spoonful instead of slicing. Chodang's version is famous specifically for how it's coagulated: seawater rather than the bittern or vinegar most tofu makers use elsewhere in Korea.

💸 What does the whole day actually cost?

The KTX round trip is most of the bill, and everything else barely moves the total. A coffee, a sundubu meal and a couple of local bus rides add up to a fraction of what the train costs on its own.

The train alone comes to more than every other line in that table combined, so the real decision on this trip isn't which café to pick; it's which KTX to book. Skip the sit-down sundubu and grab coffee only, and the whole day drops under ₩60,000 round trip.

My take Gangneung's on my own short list of Korean cities visited recently, and this itinerary is the kind that's easy to overpack: coffee street, lake, tofu village, maybe a beach walk too, all squeezed into a day with only so many KTX slots on either end. If I were planning it again, I'd pick two of the three: coffee and the lake, or coffee and lunch in Chodang, rather than rushing all of it to catch an evening train back. The coast isn't going anywhere, and neither is the coffee.

FAQ
If I only have time for one stop, the coffee side or the water, which wins?
Go with whichever matches the mood you're actually in that afternoon. A good pour-over and a slow walk by the water fill up roughly the same chunk of an afternoon, so skipping one isn't really saving time, it's just trading one kind of quiet for another.
Does the Coffee Festival cost anything to attend?
Only the hands-on parts do. A plain afternoon of browsing stalls and sampling what's already poured needs no ticket at all, so there's nothing to reserve ahead unless a specific brewing class or workshop is the actual reason for the trip.

Book the Cheongnyangni KTX first, especially for a festival-week date, since that single ticket decides more of this trip than any café or restaurant choice will. Go for the coffee street if that's the pull, but leave room for the lake. It's the one stop on this list that costs nothing and still slows the day down in a good way.
