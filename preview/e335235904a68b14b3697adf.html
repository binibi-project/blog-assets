Jongmyo Shrine: ₩1,000, and the Day You Can't Go

Jongmyo costs ₩1,000 to enter, Tuesday is always closed, and three weekdays mean a guided tour instead of a free walk through this UNESCO shrine.

Quick answer
  • It's ₩1,000 to get in: the same adult price whether you're Korean or a tourist, with free entry under a handful of age and ID categories.
  • Monday, Thursday and Friday mean a guided tour, not a free walk: Tuesday closes the gate completely, no exceptions.
  • Go free and self-guided instead: pick a Saturday, a Sunday, or the last Wednesday of the month if you don't want a guide setting your pace.

Step through Oedaemun and the traffic on Jongno drops away almost immediately. Gravel crunches underfoot, there's no souvenir stall in sight, and the long, low silhouette of Jeongjeon sits across an open stone courtyard like it's waiting for a ceremony that hasn't started yet. Seoul's four grand palaces were built to be looked at. Jongmyo was built so almost nobody would look at it at all, and that absence of spectacle is exactly why the first ten minutes inside feel different from every palace gate nearby.

Most visitors treat it as the quiet fifteen-minute add-on next to Changgyeonggung, not a site with its own reservation system, its own weekly schedule, and a fee now sitting inside a national review. All three decide which day you should actually go.

🎫 How Much Does It Cost, and Who Gets In Free?

Adults pay ₩1,000 to enter Jongmyo, the same price for Korean citizens and foreign visitors alike, according to the Korea Heritage Service's own admission page. A group of ten or more still pays the full ₩1,000 each, so there's no bulk discount here the way there is at some of the palaces. Free entry covers Korean citizens 24 and under or 65 and up, foreign visitors 18 and under or 65 and up, and a short list of other groups shown with ID.

What is Culture Day (문화가 있는 날)?

The last Wednesday of every month, when Jongmyo waives its fee and switches to free, self-guided viewing, the same format as a regular weekend.

That ₩1,000 puts Jongmyo in the cheaper half of Seoul's royal sites: Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung both charge ₩3,000, while Jongmyo sits in the same ₩1,000 bracket as Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung, alongside the Joseon royal tombs. That whole tier has held since 2005, and it's exactly what a current government review is looking at.

🕰️ Which Day Should You Pick: Guided Tour or Free Walk?

Three weekdays mean a guided tour, not a free walk, and Tuesday closes the gate completely, holiday or not. On Monday, Thursday or Friday, the so-called "시간제관람일," a Cultural Heritage Service guide leads you through on an hourly Korean-language session, running 09:20 to 16:20 from October through February, plus set English, Japanese or Chinese slots through the day. Weekday spots are capped at 200 a day, split evenly between 100 booked online and 100 sold on-site.

Saturday and Sunday flip the format entirely: no guide, no fixed start time, just a free-roam ₩1,000 ticket and however long you want to stay until last admission at 16:30 this time of year. Culture Day Wednesday behaves the same way, minus the fee.

What is Jongmyo Daeje (종묘대제)?

The main royal ancestral rite, usually held the first Sunday of May. In 2026 it ran on May 3 in two sessions, and for the first time the ceremony reached beyond the shrine walls, with screens at Gyeongbokgung's Heungnyemun Square and a royal procession through Gwanghwamun and Jongno.

🚶 Getting There, What's Inside, and What It Actually Costs

Ride Lines 1, 3 or 5 to Jongno 3-ga Station and leave by Exit 8: Oedaemun gate is about a five-minute walk. Past the gate, Jeongjeon holds 19 chambers behind matching plain doors, each one built to enshrine a single king's or queen's spirit tablet. A second hall, Yeongnyeongjeon, holds another 34 tablets; Gongsindang honors 83 meritorious subjects, and Chilsadang is kept for seven household guardian spirits, with Manmyoru pavilion and its old juniper tree marking the walk between them. The whole complex has carried UNESCO World Heritage status since 1995, and the ritual performed inside it was separately listed as UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2001.

Leaving through Jongmyo's west wall on a weekend, holiday or Culture Day Wednesday puts you on Seosullo-gil, an 800-meter stone-walled alley of hanok cafes, jewelry stalls and craft studios that's closed to cars from 10:00 to 18:00 on weekends. The same kind of weekend-only gate links Jongmyo to Changgyeonggung on the opposite side: it opens 9:00 to 18:00 on weekends, holidays, or Culture Day Wednesdays, but each site still charges its own ₩1,000, and leaving through that gate means you can't walk back in without buying a new ticket.

My take Worrying about which gate or which exit is the right one is the wrong worry here; the day of the week is what actually decides your visit. If picking one day over the five, going on a weekend or that last Wednesday is the move, since a guide sets a fixed pace and a free-roam hour doesn't. That's not a knock on the guided version, it just suits a different kind of visitor, someone who wants the tablets and the chambers explained rather than left to a courtyard's quiet. Either way, building in the extra twenty minutes to Seosullo-gil afterward is worth more than rushing to a sixth palace that same day.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Walk Seosullo-gil on the way out, or cross the weekend wall gate into Changgyeonggung next door; both only open on the same free-roam days Jongmyo itself does.
  • What locals argue about Whether ₩1,000 is too low to last. A public survey found visitors willing to pay an average of ₩9,730 for palace-and-Jongmyo admission, which is the exact number officials keep citing to justify a change.
  • Watch for the fee review itself: the Korea Heritage Service had planned to raise this ₩1,000 tier from Jan. 1, 2027, but an Oct. 8, 2026 National Assembly audit found the agency now lists that date as undetermined.
FAQ
Does wearing hanbok get me in free at Jongmyo too?
Yes, under the same dress rule used at every royal site in the capital, though the outfit needs a clear top and bottom cut close to traditional lines. A jeogori jacket paired with plain denim, or a skirt worn with a T-shirt, gets turned away at the kiosk, whereas a cut made for another gender has counted toward entry since a 2019 human-rights recommendation.
Can I pay for the ₩1,000 ticket by card, or do I need cash?
Not confirmed for the machine itself: a city report places the unmanned ticket machine right at Bukshinmun, the gate Jongmyo shares with Changgyeonggung's entrance, without saying whether it takes cards. Carrying a couple of 1,000-won bills is the safer bet than assuming a card works at an unstaffed kiosk.
