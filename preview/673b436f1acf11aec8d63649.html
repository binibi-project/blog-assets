Where Locals Actually Eat Fried Chicken in Seoul: 3 Real Picks

Three old-school whole-chicken shops locals trust, each rated 4.1+ on Google Maps, with real prices, hours and how to order.

Quick answer
  • Three old-school whole-chicken counters: one in Hannam-dong, one inside a Cheongnyangni market alley, one in Jayang-dong, each rated 4.1 or better on Google Maps from 30-plus reviews.
  • A whole bird runs ₩7,000-19,000 at the cheapest of the three, well under the roughly ₩23,000 the big three chains charge for their main menu item.
  • Pick your day carefully: skip Hannam-dong on a Sunday, since that's its one confirmed closed day; the Cheongnyangni alley runs every day of the week.

Saturday night in Cheongnyangni, and the smell of fry oil reaches the market gate before you do. Inside, a row of plastic stools faces a kitchen that's been frying whole chickens the same way since long before most of its customers were born. Across the city in Hannam-dong, a different counter has built the same loyalty among a very different crowd: comedians, a figure-skating legend, and a retail chairman who keeps coming back. Seoul has never agreed on a single best fried chicken, and that disagreement is the whole point: the real question isn't which shop wins a ranking, it's which decades-old counter near you is worth the detour tonight.

What is tongdak (통닭)?

Literally "whole chicken": a bird fried in one piece rather than cut into nuggets first. It's the older style of Korean fried chicken, usually served on a tray with a pickled radish cube or a simple cabbage salad, and it's what all three shops below serve.

🍗 Hannam-dong's chicken that doubles as a stomach remedy

한남동한방통닭 (Hanbang Tongdak, Hannam-dong branch) fries its chicken after brining it in Chinese medicinal herbs, and the TV host Hong Hyun-hee said on air that she finally understood why actress-comedian Lee Young-ja calls it better than a digestive pill. The shop is at 서울 용산구 대사관로34길 12, about 450 meters from Hannam Station on the Gyeongui-Jungang Line. ELLE Korea's roundup names Lee Young-ja as the one who started the buzz, with businessman Jung Yong-jin and figure skater Kim Yuna both named as regulars.

It doesn't open for lunch: hours run 16:10-00:30 Monday through Thursday, 16:10-01:30 Friday and Saturday, and it's closed all day Sunday. No menu price is posted on its Google listing or in press coverage, so budget isn't confirmed; ask at the counter or check the window menu when you arrive.

Say "한방통닭 한 마리 주세요" (han-bang-tong-dak han ma-ri ju-se-yo, "one herbal chicken, please"). The shop's phone is 02-797-8677.

Google Maps rating 4.3 from 1,177 reviews (checked 2026-11-21).

🏮 Is Cheongnyangni's chicken alley worth the trip across town?

Yes, if you want to see where Seoul's chicken habit actually started: 남원통닭 (Namwon Tongdak) sits inside the 청량리통닭골목 (Cheongnyangni Chicken Alley), a two-minute walk from Exit 1 of Cheongnyangni Station on Line 1. The alley is part of 청량리전통시장 at 서울 동대문구 경동시장로2길 49-3, and 남원통닭 itself is at 서울 동대문구 홍릉로1길 47.

What does "대" (dae) mean on an old-style chicken menu?

Size, not a dish name: "소" (so) is small, "대" (dae) is large. These shops size by the chicken, not the piece count, so "대" usually means one full-grown bird rather than a half order.

It's open daily, 10:30-00:00, with no closing day, according to its current Google listing. Seoul's own city news desk put the alley's going rate at about ₩18,000 for a large plain fried chicken and ₩20,000-plus for a large seasoned one, as of June 2024, with a half-and-half large running around ₩20,000. No single shop posts its own price list online, so treat that as an alley-wide range rather than 남원통닭's exact menu.

Order with "후라이드 대 주세요" (hu-ra-i-deu dae ju-se-yo, "one large fried chicken, please"). The number is 02-968-9841.

Google Maps rating 4.1 from 301 reviews (checked 2026-11-21).

🌙 Jayang-dong's ₩7,000 bird for one

아따통닭 (Atta Tongdak) sells its old-style whole chicken one bird at a time, starting at ₩7,000 (about $5.24) for a single bird, ₩13,000 ($9.73) for two, and ₩19,000 ($14.22) for three, as of April 2025. That sizing makes it the easiest of the three to order for just yourself, and K-pop member Doyoung of NCT is said to order delivery from it often.

The shop is at 서울 광진구 자양로 328, about 490 meters from Achasan Station on Line 5. Neither its hours nor an English menu are listed on Google Maps or in press coverage, so they're not confirmed; call ahead if the timing matters.

Say "옛날통닭 두 마리 주세요" (yet-nal-tong-dak du ma-ri ju-se-yo, "two old-style chickens, please") if you're ordering for a group. The shop's number is 02-458-0550.

Google Maps rating 4.4 from 36 reviews (checked 2026-11-21).

On paper the Jayang-dong bird looks like the deal of the three, but it's also the one shop with no posted hours anywhere online. Show up on a whim and there's a real chance the door is locked; the Cheongnyangni and Hannam-dong counters at least publish something to check against.

Farm-gate chicken prices fell 37.7% between March and September 2026, from ₩5,308 to ₩3,308 per kilogram, yet the big three fried-chicken chains haven't moved their menu prices at all. The Korea Broiler Association tracked that drop after a bird-flu cull pushed prices up earlier in the year, and once the cull's effect passed, farm prices came back down fast.

Think of it this way: the raw chicken in a ₩23,000 fried-chicken order now costs the chain roughly a third less than it did in March, but the price on the receipt hasn't changed since Kyochon last raised it in April 2023, bhc in December 2023, and BBQ in June 2024. That gap is exactly why Korea's Fair Trade Commission is now investigating the three chains for possible price collusion, with on-site inspections already carried out at all three headquarters.

What is chimaek (치맥)?

A portmanteau of chikin (fried chicken) and maekju (beer): the standard pairing for a casual night out, delivered or eaten at a hof (beer hall). It's less a specific dish than a whole evening plan.

The three chains say it's unfair to compare one ingredient's wholesale price to a finished menu item, and that argument isn't baseless, since rent, labor and packaging haven't gotten cheaper. Still, if a chain order has felt like it's been stuck at the same price for years, this is the number behind that feeling, not just inflation in general.

My take If you're trying to decide between a delivery app's usual chain order and crossing town for one of these three, the chains aren't where I'd spend a Saturday night budget right now. A price that hasn't moved while the ingredient got cheaper isn't a scandal you need to solve, but it's a reason to let an independent counter earn the money instead, especially when the independent one is also the better story. I'd start with whichever shop is already near a subway line you're using that day; crossing Seoul just for chicken is a commitment, not a snack run.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Cheongnyangni's alley has a second old shop, 동경통닭, right alongside 남원통닭; the Seoul city writeup covers both as a pair, so it's an easy second stop without changing stations.
  • What locals argue about Whether the Fair Trade Commission's collusion probe is fair, given the chains' point that wholesale chicken is only one input among many, against the plain fact that farm prices dropped more than a third while receipts didn't move at all.
  • Watch for Hannam-dong's Sunday closure and its late 16:10 opening; the other two have no confirmed rest day, but older single-location shops like these change their hours without much notice, so a phone call before a special trip is worth it.
FAQ
Will my foreign card work at any of these?
Nothing published confirms plastic either way, so keep paper bills on you just in case. Settling the bill through an app sidesteps the question entirely, since that payment runs on your phone no matter what the till accepts.
When's the quietest time to show up without a wait?
Arrive right as the doors unlock, ahead of the evening crush. One pick starts serving mid-morning, well before lunch, and the other stays shuttered until late afternoon, so its first thirty minutes beat the after-dark rush that fills every seat once people get off work.

For a first try, the Cheongnyangni alley is the one I'd protect on the calendar: it's open every day, it's two minutes from a major station, and it's the closest thing Seoul has to a chicken museum you can eat your way through. The other two are worth building a specific trip around, not a detour.
