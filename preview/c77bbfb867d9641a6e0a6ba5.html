What a K-Pop Dance Class in Seoul Actually Costs

A class at 1MILLION, TWICE's choreographer's studio, costs ₩30,000. It's in Seongsu, not Hongdae, and good slots vanish fast once booking opens.

Walk into 1MILLION Dance Studio in Seongsu-dong on a weekday evening and the lobby already sounds like a concert warming up. Past the front desk, a mirrored room fills with strangers in clean sneakers, most of whom won't exchange a word beyond counting beats out loud. The instructor runs the same eight counts a third time, a touch faster, and by the end half the room is already moving like they've done this before.

You haven't, and that's fine. For one class, you train under the label of the same choreographer who built routines for TWICE, Sunmi, and MAMAMOO, in a studio where over half the students now fly in from outside Korea to be in that room.

What is 1MILLION Dance Studio?

A Seoul dance academy co-founded by choreographer Lia Kim in 2014, known worldwide through its YouTube channel of cover and original choreography videos. It teaches public drop-in classes alongside its choreography work, and its founder is credited with routines for groups such as TWICE and MAMAMOO.

💸 What a class costs, and which level to book

A single drop-in class is ₩30,000 ($22.57), and the per-class price drops as low as ₩12,500 ($9.40) if you commit to the 40-class ticket, according to 1MILLION's own ticket page.

On paper the 40-class ticket looks like the obvious buy, but it only pays off if you're staying in Seoul long enough to use all 40: unused tickets can't be exchanged for a smaller package, and the refund schedule only protects you for the first two weeks. Ask for a refund in the first 10 days after a ticket's start date and you get two-thirds of what's left back; days 11 to 15 drop that to half; after day 15, nothing.

For a one-week trip, a 5-class ticket at ₩130,000 ($97.78) is the safer bet: it covers a class every day without betting ₩500,000 on a vacation that might get cut short.

Pick "Learner" over "Master" for a first visit: of the six class slots on the studio's own board for Tuesday, Oct. 20, 2026, three are Learner-level, one is Step By 1M, one is K-Pop Cover, and only one, taught by Yechan, is marked Master.

The names aren't perfectly consistent between instructors. One guide for exchange students found that a "learner" class from one teacher could run about as hard as another instructor's "masterclass," so the level tag is a starting point, not a guarantee.

What do "Learner" and "Master" actually mean here?

Rough skill tiers the studio uses to sort its own weekly schedule, running Starter, Learner, Step By 1M, and Master, plus a separate K-Pop Cover track for full song routines. They describe the class, not a test you pass to enter one, and anyone can book any level that's open.

📅 Booking before you land, not after

Make your account on the official site or app before you leave home: classes are reported to open for booking about a week ahead, and the well-known instructors' slots can fill within minutes.

Payment runs through the app or website only. One guide for foreign students warns that cash is rarely accepted at the counter for class tickets and that your card may need 3D Secure verification, so it's worth telling your bank you'll be charging from Korea before you go.

What is 3D Secure, and why does a Korean ticket need it?

An extra bank verification step, usually a code or app confirmation, that your card issuer adds for an international charge it doesn't recognize. A 1MILLION ticket charges from a Korean app, so a card used to buying things only at home can get blocked mid-checkout unless the bank already expects it.

Bring a change of shoes. Street shoes reportedly aren't allowed on the dance floor, so pack sneakers you haven't worn outside that same day.

🚇 Getting to Seongsu, not Hongdae

1MILLION Dance Studio is in Seongsu-dong, about a 5-minute walk from Exit 3 of Seongsu Station on Line 2, a different part of Seoul from where most tourist-oriented K-pop dance classes advertise.

Hongdae has its own cluster of K-pop dance experiences built for visitors: a 90-minute group session at one Hapjeong-area studio runs about $37.50 (₩49,856), booked through a tour platform rather than a working choreography studio. Those are a fine one-off souvenir, but they're a different business from 1MILLION, not a cheaper way into the same room.

Seongsu-dong still mixes both kinds of block: small industrial shops sit streets away from the design studios and storefronts that have moved into the same low-rise buildings, and the walk from the station passes both kinds side by side.

My take Showing up as the one person in the room who's never danced before sounds like the risk here, but it isn't: more than half the studio's own students are visitors trying this the same way you'd be. The real risk is money and timing, not skill, so buy the smallest ticket that covers your trip, and book a Learner slot early enough in the day that your legs still work for whatever's next on your Seoul itinerary.

FAQ
Do I need to speak Korean to follow a class?
No. A routine gets shown and counted out loud, eight beats at a time, so nobody needs a shared language to follow the moves. If a question comes up beforehand, the homepage itself switches between three languages, English as well as Korean and Japanese, so reaching out ahead of time doesn't require a translator either.
What if the class I want shows "Sold Out"?
Don't give up on it that night. One guide for foreign students reports that cancelled spots reopen late, often between 10 p.m. and midnight KST the evening before the class, so checking back then can land you a seat that showed full that morning.

One drop-in class is a cheap way to find out whether a Seoul trip can fit a dance studio into it at all. Book the smallest ticket your schedule can actually use, and set a Korea-time alarm for whenever booking opens, not the morning you land.
