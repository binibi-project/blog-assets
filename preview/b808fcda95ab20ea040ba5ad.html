Seoul's Christmas Markets 2026: Dates, Prices, What's Free

Three Seoul Christmas events for 2026: the city's own Gwanghwamun Market, the record-breaking Cheonggyecheon lantern walk, and Lotte's market, now free to just walk into.

Quick answer
  • Lotte's market is free this year: general admission at Jamsil dropped the old day/night fee split, so walking in costs nothing.
  • Gwanghwamun and Cheonggyecheon are both city-run and free, part of a 59-day Seoul Winter Festival, though the market's own exact dates aren't announced yet.
  • Go on a weekday evening: skip Saturday if you haven't booked anything, since weekend slots have sold out within minutes in past years.

Three events, one season, and only one of them has ever actually needed a ticket. Walk Sejong-daero on a cold December evening and the market huts glow red and gold against the dark; a few minutes south, paper lanterns shaped like a royal procession drift on the Cheonggyecheon's black water; across the river in Jamsil, a glass tower goes blue at dusk over a lawn full of stalls that used to charge for exactly this view. None of them ask much of you beyond showing up at the right hour, and that's the part worth knowing before you plan around a price that quietly changed this year.

What is the Seoul Winter Festival (서울윈터페스티벌)?

The city's umbrella name for its whole winter lineup: markets, light displays and the lantern walk, spread across six sites: the Han River, Gwanghwamun Square, Cheonggyecheon, Seoul Plaza, Bosingak, plus the DDP. Last year's edition, under the older name Seoul Winter Festa, ran 24 days and drew about 11 million visits citywide.

🎄 Gwanghwamun Market: free and official, dates still pending

Gwanghwamun Market is the city's own Christmas market, free to enter, and folded into the 2026 Seoul Winter Festival's Dec. 4, 2026-Jan. 31, 2027 run across six sites citywide, Gwanghwamun Square among them. The market's own start and end dates inside that 59-day window aren't confirmed yet as this is written, so treat any specific date you see before an official announcement as a guess.

Last year's version ran December 12-31, 2025, inside the wider festival. Paired with the lantern festival next door, the two drew 7.4 million visits combined, Seoul's highest winter-festival turnout on record.

Get off at Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5), Exit 5, a one-minute walk from the square itself. The market sets up right along Sejong-daero, past the King Sejong statue, so you'll see the pavilions before you reach street level.

🏮 Cheonggyecheon's lantern walk, a few minutes from the market

The Seoul Lantern Festival along the Cheonggyecheon broke its own attendance record last year: 3.83 million visits over 37 days, up 55% on the year before, averaging more than 100,000 people a day. It ran free, 18:00-23:00 daily, from Cheonggye Plaza down to Samil Bridge.

What's actually floating on the water?

Lit sculptures built around a theme that changes every year, some shaped like traditional figures in a royal procession, others simple paper lanterns strung low over the stream. Past editions have mixed both: large lit forms along the banks and small floating lanterns, some inscribed with handwritten wishes, drifting on the water itself.

Like the market next door, this year's exact dates haven't been confirmed as this is written. If the city follows its own pattern, expect a mid-December start, since the last three editions all opened between December 11 and 13. Three stations have served as entry points in past years: Gwanghwamun (Line 5), Jonggak (Line 1), City Hall (Lines 1/2).

The lantern walk and the market share one city budget, which is exactly what locals argue about when a "free" festival still needs you to book a Fast Pass somewhere else to avoid a line. That tension carries over to the one market on this list that actually used to charge.

🎅 Lotte Town Christmas Market: the one that dropped its entry fee

Lotte Town Christmas Market, at Lotte World Tower's outdoor World Park plaza in Jamsil, runs Nov. 19, 2026 through Jan. 3, 2027. General admission is free this year, a change from the day/night fee split of past seasons.

Lotte expects about 450,000 visitors, roughly matching last year's turnout.

Take Jamsil Station: Line 2's underground plaza, Exits 1 or 2, or Line 8's underground passage, Exits 10 or 11, both connect straight into the Lotte complex. A hosted "Christmas Time Show" runs on the hour, 18:00-21:00 daily, with carols and live music added on weekends and Christmas Day.

Free doesn't mean no line. Bizwatch reported that Klook sells a paid Fast Pass for faster entry, and more than 10,000 international visitors had already booked one as of November 18, 2026. A private lounge with champagne and cake runs 7 reservation slots a day, which is the one part of this market that was never going to be free. Indoor seating for food now holds up to 100 people, double last year's 50, and 21 F&B booths are selling on-site.

On paper, three free events sound like a simple plan, but only one of them has a confirmed date you can actually book around right now. Lotte already has 10,000-plus people holding a Fast Pass reservation three weeks out, while the two city markets are still asking you to watch an announcement that hasn't posted yet.

If you've watched a drama scene where a character gets handed a paper lantern by a stranger on a bridge, the Cheonggyecheon walk is the closest a visitor gets to that feeling, minus the scripted stranger.

FAQ
Does the stream display have any hidden ticket tiers?
No. The city's own wrap-up of the prior edition states plainly that every installation could be seen without paying a won, a detail buried in a post-event recap rather than printed on any ticket page. Budget zero for this one and spend your cash in Jamsil instead.
Is a sold-out date gone for good?
Not necessarily. The prior edition opened extra booking waves well after the first batch vanished, adding a fresh set of dates on Nov. 21 and again on Dec. 8. A vanished November weekend doesn't rule out a December wave bringing it back.

Honestly? I'd do Gwanghwamun and the lantern walk back to back on the same free evening, since they're a few minutes apart, and save Lotte for a weeknight when the Fast Pass line doesn't matter. Check the city's own festival page before you lock in a date, since neither Gwanghwamun nor Cheonggyecheon has posted one yet.
