Where Locals Eat Haejangguk in Seoul: 3 Real Picks

A single-dish pollock soup shop rated 4.5, a 70-year ox-bone institution, and a ₩3,000 bowl that hasn't moved in price for years: three real Seoul haejangguk picks.

Ever eaten a bowl of soup before nine in the morning because the night before demanded it? In Seoul, that bowl has a name, and it's served without apology: a clear or blood-dark broth, a mound of greens or dried fish gone soft, a spoon already working before you've taken your coat off. Office workers in wrinkled white shirts sit shoulder to shoulder with men who never made it home, and nobody at the counter asks which one you are. If a Korean co-worker has ever mentioned "해장" the morning after a company dinner, this is the whole answer, in three very different bowls scattered across the city.

What is haejangguk (해장국)?

Literally a "soup to chase away" a hangover. The name covers a whole family of dishes: ox-bone or ox-blood broths, dried-pollock soups, napa-green stews, all built around the same goal, a hot bowl eaten to settle a rough morning.

Treat these as three separate trips, not one route: Mugyodong Bugeogukjip works a single counter near City Hall, Changseongok sits inside a market that's been open since 1948, and Somunnan-jip keeps one pot going near Nakwon Arcade in Jongno.

🍲 Mugyodong Bugeogukjip: one dried-pollock bowl, closed Mondays

Mugyodong Bugeogukjip (무교동북어국집) has sold exactly one dish, a dried-pollock hangover soup, since its founder gave up running a meat restaurant to study the recipe on his own. Seoul's own city media desk reported in 2015 that the founder, then 78, had already kept the shop going for roughly half a century, which puts its start sometime in the 1960s and its current age past 60 years. His two sons took over about 20 years before that report, running it as the second generation.

It's at 서울 중구 을지로1길 38, about a 3-minute walk from Euljiro 1(il)-ga Station's Exit 1, or roughly 330 meters from City Hall Station's Exit 4.

Hours shift by day: on Tuesday, Wednesday or Friday it runs 07:00-20:00 (last order 19:30), on Saturday or Sunday it cuts off at 15:00 (last order 14:30), and it's closed entirely on Mondays. Call 02-777-3891 if a visit is tight on timing.

There's nothing to choose beyond saying "북어해장국 하나 주세요" (bugeo-haejangguk hana juseyo, "one dried-pollock soup, please") at ₩11,000 ($8.21). The owners have reportedly turned down franchise offers, saying the broth's timing is too hard to replicate at a second location.

🏚️ Is Changseongok's ox-bone broth worth the trip to Yongmun Market?

Yes, if a 70-year-old bowl with its own trade-secret topping sounds worth a detour: Changseongok (창성옥) was named a Seoul Future Heritage site in 2016 for a "70-year tradition," founded in the last years of Japanese colonial rule and now run by a third generation. It sits inside Yongmun Market, which itself opened in 1948, about a 3-minute walk from Hyochang Park Station's Exit 3 (Line 6 and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line).

The broth is beef-bone stock boiled repeatedly in a cast-iron pot, seasoned with doenjang rather than the spicier red style, and finished with ox-blood curd, cabbage-heart greens and a topping of aged kimchi the owner calls a trade secret. The dining room is small, so a weekday lunch can mean a short wait.

A solo bowl of haejangguk runs ₩10,000 ($7.46); the shared bone jeongol (뼈전골, a size listed as "중") is ₩29,000 ($21.64), usually finished by stirring a fried egg into the leftover rice and broth. It's open daily, 06:00-23:00.

What is seonji (선지)?

Ox blood that's simmered until it sets into a soft, dark curd. It shows up in several hangover-soup styles, including Changseongok's, for a mineral, slightly iron-rich flavor rather than any meaty taste.

🥬 Somunnan-jip: a ₩3,000 bowl that hasn't moved in years

Somunnan-jip (소문난집), near Nakwon Arcade in Jongno, sells one thing, uggeoji haejangguk, a napa-green soup with a little tofu, and it's held the same ₩3,000 ($2.24) price across at least two years of local write-ups. The shop describes 60 years of experience making the soup. Sit down and a bowl, plus rice, is brought to your table automatically per person, no ordering required.

What is uggeoji (우거지)?

The tougher outer leaves of napa cabbage, or dried radish greens, boiled until soft and added to a stew for bulk and a slightly bitter edge.

It's at 서울 종로구 수표로 131, near Nakwon Arcade; a tax-office direction page for the same block points visitors to Jongno 3-ga Station's Exit 5, about 150 meters away. Hours run 04:20-21:30 daily. The late broadcaster Song Hae was a regular here, which is part of why locals still bring it up.

On paper, Somunnan-jip looks like the clear budget pick at ₩3,000 ($2.24), but it's also the earliest opener and the one with no backup dish, so the lowest price comes with the least flexibility if you show up wanting anything else.

My take Walking into any of these three without a menu to point at is the part that trips people up, not the soup itself. If I were a visitor nursing a rough morning with no Korean and no plan, I'd head for whichever of these has exactly one dish on the board, Mugyodong Bugeogukjip or Somunnan-jip, since there's no decision left to get wrong once you sit down. Save Changseongok's doenjang-based bowl for a day you're curious enough to handle an actual menu, because its two items and trade-secret topping reward a little more attention than a hungover 8 a.m. usually allows.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Yongmun Market surrounds Changseongok, so a walk through its stalls costs nothing extra once you're already there for the bowl.
  • What locals argue about Whether a dried-pollock soup like Mugyodong's even belongs in the same category as the blood-and-bone styles, since some drinkers treat bugeoguk as its own gentler, separate tradition rather than a true haejangguk.
  • Watch for Mugyodong Bugeogukjip's day-by-day hours. A Tuesday closing time won't match a Saturday one, and Monday means no soup at all.
FAQ
Is any of these actually spicy, or is that just how hangover soup looks in photos?
Mostly mild, despite the deep red color in plenty of online photos of the category. Changseongok leans on doenjang rather than chili, and locals describe Somunnan-jip's broth as plain and clean-tasting. Mugyodong's version is the gentlest on paper, built around egg and dried fish rather than any real heat.
Which of these fits best into a short layover or a quick transit stop, rather than a dedicated trip?
The dried-pollock counter, by a wide margin: its downtown spot sits a few minutes off a train transfer, something neither the market stall nor the pre-dawn-only option can match without extra planning on your end.

If you only have time for one, let the hour decide it: before 6 a.m., it's Somunnan-jip; any other time on a weekday, Mugyodong Bugeogukjip's pollock broth is the safer single-dish bet. Check the day of the week before heading to Mugyodong so a closed Monday doesn't end the trip before it starts.
