Korean Wedding Cash Gifts: How Much Goes in the Envelope

Invited to a Korean wedding? What Koreans actually put in the envelope, why the meal price matters, how the day runs, and what not to wear.

Saturday, a little before noon, in a wedding hall somewhere in Seoul. The elevator opens onto a lobby of people in their good clothes, and before anyone looks for the bride, they look for the table by the door. That's where the wedding really starts: a short line, envelopes changing hands, a friend of the couple behind the desk.

You'll feel the pace before you understand it. Everything here runs on a clock, and the envelope in your pocket is how you join in.

💸 How much should you put in a Korean wedding envelope?

If you're attending and eating in Seoul, ₩100,000 (about $75) is the safe number; ₩50,000 is still the single most common gift, but its share is shrinking. NH NongHyup Bank's report on wedding gifts looked at 5.33 million gift transfers from 1.15 million customers between January 2023 and December 2025, and the average went from ₩110,000 in 2023 to ₩114,000 in 2024 and ₩117,000 in 2025, about 6.9% in two years.

What is chugui-geum (축의금)?

The cash gift guests give at a Korean wedding. It goes in an envelope with the giver's name written on it, so the couple knows who gave what, and many married guests later weigh what a person once gave them when that person's own wedding comes around.

The table shows bank transfers only, so it can't tell you who actually showed up and ate. Segye Ilbo points out that the bank's data doesn't separate guests who attended from those who only sent money. Read ₩50,000 as the floor, not the norm.

Age and city move the number too. In 2025 givers in their 20s and 30s averaged ₩138,000, the highest of any age group, and Seoul led the regions at ₩134,000 (about $100).

The reason ₩50,000 now feels thin is the plate in front of you. At the desk you'll trade your envelope for a meal ticket, so think of the envelope as paying for your own lunch first, with whatever's left over as the actual gift. Data from the Korea Consumer Agency's price survey puts the median meal cost per guest at Seoul wedding halls at around ₩80,000 as of June 2026. So ₩100,000 covers your plate and leaves about ₩20,000 as the gift, while ₩50,000 leaves the couple paying roughly ₩30,000 to feed you.

🎫 What actually happens when you arrive?

You hand your envelope in at a reception desk near the entrance, get a meal ticket back, watch a short ceremony, then eat at a buffet that's often shared with other weddings in the same building. A Korea JoongAng Daily student reporter described exactly that sequence: envelopes are handed in at the reception desk and exchanged for a food ticket, the ceremony comes first, and guests then move to a dining area on another floor that's combined with other weddings running at the same time.

What is a sikgwon (식권)?

The meal ticket you get at the reception desk in exchange for your envelope. It's your pass into the buffet, so keep it on you between the ceremony and the dining room.

The whole thing, ceremony plus meal, ran about two to three hours in that account. Being on time matters. The same reporter arrived around 30 minutes late and walked into the wrong wedding first; by the time the right building was found, the first part was over and the meal tickets were gone.

Behind that little ticket sits a contract: the couple has already committed to paying for a minimum number of meals. The Korea Consumer Agency found the median minimum guarantee at 200 guests, in peak and off-peak season alike.

What is a minimum guarantee (최소 보증인원)?

The number of meals a couple must pay the wedding hall for, whether or not that many guests show up. In Seoul the median meal bill came to ₩15,840,000 as of June 2026, so an empty seat still costs them.

👗 What should you wear, and what counts as rude?

Wear anything except white; in one survey it beat every other guest mistake, including gossiping about the couple. In a 2019 survey by matchmaking company Duo, 25.5% of 380 unmarried men and women named "a guest in a white dress" as the most annoying wedding guest, the top answer.

The gender gap is the part to notice. Only 8.6% of men picked the white dress, against 42.0% of women. Women were about five times as likely to call it out, so don't count on a mixed crowd to shrug it off. The same survey found that 42.9% of respondents worry most about their outfit when attending, more than the cash gift at 20.3%.

My take If the envelope question is making you dread the invitation a little, you're in good company: Segye Ilbo opened its August piece on this by noting that everyone stops short for a moment when a mobile invitation arrives, unsure what amount to put in. The math is kinder than the worry. At a Seoul hall I'd put in ₩100,000 if you're eating, a bit more if you're close to the couple, and stop there. I'd skip trying to match what the couple's oldest friends give. What sticks with a couple is that you came, sat through the vows, and didn't wear white.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Clear the whole afternoon instead of planning around the ceremony time. With the ceremony and buffet on different floors and other weddings sharing the dining room, the two to three hours in the JoongAng Daily account is closer to reality than the time printed on the invitation.
  • What locals argue about Whether the number is about closeness or bookkeeping. In a survey of 1,008 people aged 25 to 39 by matchmaking firm Gayeon, reported in August 2026, single respondents said ₩130,000 felt right for a friend's wedding they attend, while married respondents said ₩290,000, and 60.6% of the married group said they factor in what that person gave at their own wedding.
  • Watch for Season. Hall meal prices nationwide had a median of ₩60,000 per guest in peak season (March to June, September to December) against ₩55,000 off-peak, so an autumn wedding like the ones happening now sits at the pricier end.
FAQ
Can I just send the money if I can't go?
Yes, wiring it ahead is perfectly ordinary. Of the bank's gift transfers, 20% left on Fridays, the eve of most ceremonies, against 33% on Saturdays and 16% on Sundays. Nobody's feeding you, so drop the lunch arithmetic. Among unmarried people polled, 76.7% called friendship depth their main yardstick; pick a figure that suits yours and send a short message alongside so it doesn't land as an anonymous deposit.
I'm bringing my partner. Do we give double?
Nearly, inside Seoul. Two diners there mean roughly ₩160,000 of food, and 39.2% of wedded people questioned said a companion changes their figure. Arriving as a crowd with one slim envelope also ranked among the most irritating habits, picked by 20.3% in the Duo poll.

A Korean wedding moves fast and asks very little of a guest: show up on time, hand in a named envelope, skip the white outfit. Decide your number tonight from the meal math, write your name on the envelope, and plan to be in your seat before the start time printed on the invitation.

Prices and hours were checked on 2026-10-12; they change, so double-check before you go.
