Clear immigration at Incheon, and the first real decision of the trip shows up before you've even found the exit: where do you get won, and will your card actually work here. Most visitors have been through this somewhere before, standing at a currency counter doing math in their head while a line forms behind them.

Korea just added one more option to that moment. A prepaid card, handed to you at a bank counter a few steps past baggage claim, built specifically so you don't have to solve that problem with your own card and a guess.

💳 What the NOL World Card Actually Does

It's a prepaid card for visitors that exchanges currency, pays at stores, or withdraws cash, all without a Korean bank account. Woori Bank built it with Nol Universe, a travel and ticketing platform, and Kona I, a card-manufacturing fintech firm, and it's meant to replace the guesswork of using a foreign card at a Korean ATM or exchange counter.

What is Nol Universe?

The travel and ticketing platform behind Nol World, with more than 10 million members across over 100 countries. Women in their 20s and 30s are its largest user group, and China, Japan, and Taiwan are its three biggest overseas markets.

The three companies signed their agreement on March 13, 2026, and The Korea Herald reported it as a plan to build the infrastructure first, with the card itself launching that April. Woori Bank began handing the cards out on-site at Incheon Airport the following month.

Four months separate the agreement from the airport rollout. That's the part that actually matters to a traveler: before May, there was nowhere to walk up and get one.

You currently pick it up only at Woori Bank's foreign exchange counters inside Incheon Airport, right after you clear arrivals. The Korea Times reports that visitors can also apply before their trip, then collect the card at the counter and exchange or load money onto it the same day.

What is Kona I?

The fintech firm that manufactures the card itself and builds its payment and transit functions. It also designed the commemorative version released after the card passed 10,000 issuances, which carries icons of N Seoul Tower, the Korean flag, and Gyeongbokgung.

Once you have it, reloading isn't limited to that one counter. Cards can be topped up at Woori Bank branches and ATMs nationwide, at unmanned exchange machines, and at partner kiosks, and payment balances specifically can be topped up at CU convenience stores. Woori has said it plans self-service pickup kiosks and new pickup points in Busan and Jeju, on top of the Incheon counter.

🛍️ The Discounts: What the Partner Brands Actually Give You

The card's benefits are tied to a short, named list of retailers, not a blanket discount everywhere. At launch in May, that list named Olive Young and Musinsa as discount partners, with GS25 added for convenience-store perks; by July it had grown to include Daiso and Artbox too, plus a 10% discount on Galaxy phone rentals aimed at K-pop concertgoers.

GS25 and CU look interchangeable to a visitor, but here they're not: one gives you a discount, the other is just where you add money. Mixing them up means showing up to the wrong counter.

A Kona I executive put the convenience-store tie-in plainly: "convenience stores at the airport are actually where many visitors make their first purchase" in Korea, which is why that partnership sits at the center of the card's pitch rather than at the edge of it. Woori Bank's Choi Byung-se framed the card's ambition the same way: "We don't see it as simply a payment card," he said, describing the goal as "connecting transportation, payments and tourism into one seamless journey".

💸 Is It Worth Getting, or Is Your Own Card Fine?

It's built for a specific gap, not as a universal upgrade: it exists because the number of people hitting that gap is climbing fast. Korea logged 4.76 million foreign visitors in the first quarter of 2026, up 23% from a year earlier, and by June the country had already passed 10 million arrivals for the year, putting it on track for a record 23 million by December.

That's cumulative arrivals through the year, not three separate one-off counts. The 23 million figure is the government's own target, not a final tally.

What is dynamic currency conversion (DCC)?

The option an ATM or card machine offers to bill your withdrawal or purchase in your home currency instead of won. It sounds convenient, but the conversion rate it applies is set by the machine, not your bank, and it's almost always worse than what you'd get by choosing won and letting your own card issuer handle the conversion.

The honest gap in the reporting is price. Six separate articles cover the card's launch and its later expansion, plus the 10,000-card milestone, and not one lists what it costs to withdraw from it, reload it, or carry a balance on it. That's not a small omission for a financial product, and it's the first thing worth asking at the counter before you commit any money to it.

My take If you've landed somewhere new and spent the first twenty minutes of a trip not sure which card in your wallet will actually work, that scramble is the exact itch this card is trying to scratch. I'd pick it up at the counter on arrival day and run something small and low-stakes through it first, like a convenience-store coffee, before trusting it with anything that matters. If a fee turns up at that first use that you don't like, you've lost nothing but a few minutes, and you've learned something before your real spending starts. Ask the Woori Bank staff directly what it costs to reload and withdraw before loading any real money onto it, since no published source will tell you that part yet.

FAQ
Is there a fee to withdraw cash or reload the NOL World Card?
No number is public yet, so ask in person before you load money on. If the quoted cost feels high, a Global-sticker ATM at any major bank still reads a plain debit card.
Can I get this card anywhere besides Incheon Airport?
Not yet, and that changes things if your flight lands at Gimpo, Busan, or Jeju instead. Until a local pickup spot opens, a debit card with no foreign-transaction markup is the safer plan for day one.

For a first trip to Korea, picking the card up at the Incheon counter costs you only a few minutes, so try it for small purchases before deciding whether it earns a spot over your own card. Ask about fees before loading real money, and if the Busan or Jeju pickup points matter to your route, check Woori Bank's own page again closer to your trip rather than trusting a plan with no launch date.
