K-Tour Pass: Is Korea's New ₩15,000 Busan Pilot Worth It?

Korea's new ₩15,000 K-Tour Pass cuts KTX, bus and AREX fares to Busan and adds one free attraction, launched Oct. 1, 2026 as a Busan-only pilot.

Quick answer
  • ₩15,000 buys either version: a single-trip pass for one Busan run, or a pass linked to a multi-day KORAIL rail pass.
  • It cuts your transit cost to Busan: 40% off KTX and intercity bus fares, and 50% off the AREX airport express train.
  • Pick your free attraction with care: spend the voucher on a site you'd already planned to visit, since only one of 35 paid Busan attractions is covered.

Anyone who has opened Korail's English booking page at midnight trying to figure out which fare class gets them to Busan before the ticket window closes knows the particular stress of planning a Korean train trip from abroad: too many fare tiers, a currency that doesn't convert in your head, and nobody to ask. The government's answer, launched quietly on a Thursday, is a single card that costs less than a Seoul taxi ride and shaves a chunk off the one leg every Busan trip actually needs.

It's a pilot, not a finished product, and it only works in one city so far. But if Busan is already on your list, the math is worth doing before you book anything else.

🎫 What exactly do you get for ₩15,000?

Both versions of the K-Tour Pass (케이-투어 패스) cost the same ₩15,000 (about $11.30), and the difference is just how long you're committing to. The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism announced the pilot in its own press release, run together with the Korea Tourism Organization and starting in Busan on Wednesday, October 1, 2026. A single-journey pass (구간권) is built for one Busan run: 40% off a one-way KTX or intercity bus ticket there, plus 50% off a one-way AREX express train fare. A period pass (기간권) instead pairs with a 2-to-5-day KORAIL Pass, giving unlimited standard-class train travel for the window you pick.

What's the difference between the K-Tour Pass and the KORAIL Pass?

The KORAIL Pass is an older, separate product: an unlimited-ride ticket for Korea's national rail network, sold for set numbers of days. The new K-Tour Pass doesn't replace it. The period version of the K-Tour Pass just rides along with a KORAIL Pass purchase, adding the Busan attraction and merchant perks on top.

Either version also unlocks free entry to one of 35 paid tourist attractions in Busan and discounts at roughly 50 partner merchants around the city, from cafes to tour experiences. The pass is only sold to foreign travelers, not residents.

🚆 How much do you actually save getting to Busan?

On just the train and airport-rail legs, the pass can save more than it costs. A one-way KTX standard-class ticket from Seoul to Busan runs ₩54,400 as of today, and the direct AREX express train between Incheon Airport and Seoul Station runs ₩13,000 one-way.

On paper that's a ₩13,260 saving before you've even used the free attraction, but it only works out if you're already riding both the AREX and a long-distance train to Busan on the trip. Someone flying into Gimpo instead of Incheon, or already holding a T-money-funded subway ride to Seoul Station, loses the AREX half of that math entirely.

What is AREX?

The Airport Railroad Express, Korea's dedicated train line from Incheon International Airport into Seoul. Its direct trains skip every stop in between; the slower, cheaper all-stop trains share the same track but take longer.

🏯 Which attraction should you pick, and how do you buy the pass?

Pick the one paid site you were already planning to visit, since the free voucher only covers one of the 35 on the list. Neither the ministry's release nor the English coverage names which 35 sites qualify, so check the list on the Klook or Hop&Hop listing before you lock in your plan.

Busan drew the second-largest share of inbound foreign travelers to Korea in 2025, at 15.7%, behind only Seoul's 76.8%. A hillside neighborhood like Gamcheon Culture Village is the kind of paid stop a first-time visitor often already has circled, so if it's on the list, it's a reasonable one to spend the voucher on.

What are Klook and WebTour (Hop&Hop)?

Two online travel agencies that sell tickets and tours to foreign visitors, and now this pass too. The K-Tour Pass is issued through their apps rather than at a Korean ticket counter, and the pass's entry barcode comes from there as well.

Buy the pass itself through Klook or WebTour (Hop&Hop) before your trip, then pick up a physical card at the airport or a major station if you want one. Kiosks at Incheon Airport, Gimpo Airport and Gimhae Airport, plus Seoul Station and Busan Station, handle the physical smart card's issuing and top-ups. That same card doubles as a reloadable transit card for Busan's subway and city buses and as a general debit card, while a digital entry barcode on your phone covers the attraction and merchant discounts without the physical card at all.

My take Busan is one of the regional stops I've actually made it to in the last couple of years, and the one thing that always ate more time than it should have was figuring out the Seoul-to-Busan leg from scratch every time. If I were planning around this pilot, I'd buy the single-journey version only if I already had both an AREX ride and a KTX ticket to Busan on the itinerary; otherwise ₩15,000 is a worse deal than just booking the train directly and skipping the attraction voucher.

FAQ
Is it worth buying two of these for a round trip to Busan?
Usually not. Coming home from Busan is full price unless you hold a second card, and the math rarely favors doubling up. Treat this as an outbound-leg deal, not a two-way one, and budget the return separately.
Who do you actually ask if Klook or Hop&Hop can't answer a question?
The pilot itself sits with a specific government desk, not the resellers: the ministry's International Tourism Service Division (국제관광서비스과), reachable on 044-203-2898. Expect Korean on that line; purchase problems still go through whichever app sold you the card.

For a single Busan trip that already includes both an airport-express ride and a long-distance train, ₩15,000 is cheap insurance against two separate ticket counters. Run your own two fares through the math above before you buy; if either leg isn't on your itinerary, the pass is a worse deal than it first looks.
