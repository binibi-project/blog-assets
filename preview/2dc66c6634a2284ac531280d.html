Most visitors treat the blue-spired castle gate as the finish line, snap one photo, and spend the rest of the morning standing in whichever line looks shortest. Cross the bridge into Magic Island on a clear afternoon and the crowd thickens fast: families pushing strollers past a candy-striped lamppost, a dozen people angling for the same shot of the spires everyone else just took. A ride operator waves the next group through before the gate behind you has even swung shut. None of that is the park's fault; it just means nobody told you which queue actually moves.

Lotte World splits into two halves that behave completely differently, and knowing which one you're standing in front of saves more time than any shortcut.

What is Magic Island (매직아일랜드)?

The outdoor half of the park, built on its own small island inside Seokchon Lake. The castle, most of the big outdoor coasters and the lake-view photo spots are here; it opened in March 1990, about eight months after the indoor Adventure hall opened in July 1989.

🎫 How much does a day at Lotte World actually cost?

A full day costs ₩67,000 (about $49.90) for an adult pass, with teen passes at ₩58,000 (about $43.21), child at ₩50,000 (about $37.25), and a pass for a toddler under 36 months at ₩17,000 (about $12.67). Every one of those rose by roughly 2,000-3,000 won on April 1, 2026, the first increase since the previous one in December 2024.

If you're arriving later in the day, the After-4 pass runs ₩55,000 (about $40.98) for an adult, usable from 4 p.m. and raised by the same ₩3,000 in this spring's round. It buys the same rides as the full pass, just fewer hours to use them in, so it only pays off if you're genuinely starting after lunch.

Online and app bookings are commonly cheaper than paying at the gate window, though how much cheaper isn't confirmed by an official price list, so pull up the app's price before you queue for a paper ticket.

What is the After-4 Pass (애프터4 이용권)?

A discounted ticket usable starting at 4 p.m., good for every ride the full-day pass covers. It's built for an evening visit, not a half-price shortcut to the whole day.

🚇 How do you get to Jamsil, and which exit?

Take Line 2 or Line 8 to Jamsil Station and leave by Exit 3, about 271 meters from the park gate. Both lines stop there, so a transfer from almost anywhere in Seoul lands you within a few stops of Jamsil without crossing the city on foot.

A single ride on a T-money card costs ₩1,550 for an adult since June 28, 2025, or ₩1,650 if you're paying cash for a single-use ticket. The park itself runs 10:00-21:00 Monday through Thursday and Sunday, and 10:00-22:00 on Friday and Saturday, with ticketing and entry closing an hour before the gate does, per Visit Seoul's official listing.

On paper the admission alone looks like the whole bill, but a bottled drink or snack inside the gate adds up faster than most people plan for, and no current source confirms what a hot dog or ice cream actually costs there today. Budget loosely for food and you won't feel it.

🎭 Is this fall's horror festival worth building your trip around?

Yes, if an immersive horror set sounds fun: "Invitation to Bizarre World," Lotte World's collaboration with manga artist Junji Ito, runs September 19 through November 15, 2026 across both Adventure and Magic Island. A preview section called the Mystery Party Zone opened in the indoor Adventure hall on September 12, and characters from Ito's manga, including Tomie and Souichi, appear across several attractions, immersive walkthroughs, photo zones and themed food and merchandise.

Who is Junji Ito (伊藤潤二)?

A Japanese horror manga artist known for body-horror and spiral-obsession stories like Tomie and Uzumaki. His art style, all fine linework and dread, is what Lotte World's designers are translating into physical sets this season.

Outside the festival, the park runs its usual daily parades at 2 p.m. and 7 p.m. except Mondays.

My take Lotte World isn't where I'd send someone chasing the scariest ride in the country. I've ridden coasters at Lotte World and at Gyeongju World, and Gyeongju World's newer rides left my stomach in a worse place than anything in Seoul does. What Lotte World does better is convenience: one gate, one lake, an indoor hall for a rainy afternoon and an outdoor island for golden-hour photos, all without a second ticket or a second commute. If the castle photo is really all you're after, the After-4 pass covers it for less, and you can put the savings toward the festival's themed snacks instead.

FAQ
What happens to my ticket if it rains?
Your pass still works either way. Magic Island's outdoor rides pause once rainfall passes roughly 0.2mm, along with strong wind or extreme heat or cold, while the indoor Adventure hall keeps running regardless. A drizzle that stays under that mark won't shut anything down, so check an hourly forecast rather than the daily icon before you write off an outdoor afternoon.
Do I need a separate ticket for the horror festival's attractions?
Not confirmed yet. Lotte World hadn't finalized which festival rides and sets come bundled with a regular day pass versus sold as their own ticket, per the Korea Times' early-September report. Keep a little extra in your budget in case one of the new sets turns out to need its own add-on, and check the official site the week of your visit rather than assuming your pass covers everything new.

A full-price adult pass buys a long day if you walk in knowing which exit, which gate and which pass actually fits your afternoon. Book the After-4 pass if you're not arriving before lunch, and skip the gate-window line by checking the app's price first.
