Where Locals Eat Samgyeopsal in Seoul: 3 Real Picks

A Michelin Bib Gourmand pick, a TV-featured shaved-pork joint and a kimchi-grilled plate: three Seoul samgyeopsal spots rated 4.0+ on Google Maps, with station, hours and price.

Three restaurants, one Michelin badge, and zero subway lines in common. A grill like this doesn't go quiet: fat hits the hot stone and spits, scissors click against the plate's edge, and the whole table leans in a little closer once the first batch turns golden at the rim. A Korean host will flip a piece before a guest even notices it needs flipping, reflex more than fussiness, the same way you'd catch a glass before it tips. If you've watched a drama dinner scene lit orange by a tabletop burner, this is the sound underneath it, three versions of it in three different neighborhoods.

Treat this as three separate trips, not one day's route. Geumdwaeji Sikdang sits in Jung-gu near Yaksu Station, Jayeonseokdolgui is on the Gangnam side near Hakdong, and Sinkimchi Saengsamgyeopsal waits in Mapo, each closer to a different half of the city than to each other.

What is samgyeopsal (삼겹살)?

Korean for "three layers," the name for pork belly without its skin, grilled at the table and eaten wrapped in lettuce with garlic, ssamjang and whatever banchan land on the table. Pork belly with the skin left on is a separate dish, oyeopsal (오겹살, five layers).

🥩 Geumdwaeji Sikdang: the Bib Gourmand pick that won't take your reservation

Geumdwaeji Sikdang (금돼지식당) earned a 2026 Michelin Bib Gourmand, the guide's badge for excellent food at a fair price, and its own listing still says it accepts no reservations. The MICHELIN Guide's own listing for Geumdwaeji Sikdang says its inspector found a line outside even between meal times. The shop is at 서울 중구 다산로 149, about 380m from Exit 2 of Yaksu Station on Lines 3 and 6.

It's open daily 11:30-23:00, with no closed day listed. A diner account on its DiningCode listing describes a workaround anyway: a once-a-month CatchTable release at 5 p.m. on the last day of the month, with weekend tables walk-in only. Treat that as a maybe, not a plan.

Order with "본삼겹 주세요" (bon-samgyeop ju-se-yo, "the bon-samgyeop, please"), the cut closest to the rib bone, at ₩19,000 ($14.34) for 150g.

What is bon-samgyeop (본삼겹)?

The section of pork belly nearest the rib bones, a slightly chewier bite than the center cut most places serve as default samgyeopsal.

The kitchen also grills 눈꽃목살 (snow-flake neck) at ₩23,000 ($17.37) and 한우 육회 (raw beef tartare) at ₩36,000 ($27.18), and finishes a shared table with 통돼지 김치찌개 (whole-pig kimchi stew) at ₩8,000 ($6.04).

Google Maps rating 4.3 from 3,061 reviews (checked 2026-10-22).

🔥 Is Jayeonseokdolgui's shaved pork belly worth the TV hype?

Yes, enough that tvN's Wednesday Food Talk featured it back in April 2015, and the shop is still grilling the same thin cut. It's at 서울 강남구 논현동 97-18 (언주로148길 9), about 868m from Exit 10 of Hakdong Station on Line 7.

It's open daily 11:00-22:30 according to its DiningCode listing, though Google's own listing shows a shorter day, 11:30-21:30; the two disagree by an hour on both ends, so call 02-548-9494 if the exact time matters to your evening.

Order with "대패삼겹살 주세요" (daepae-samgyeopsal ju-se-yo, "the shaved pork belly, please") at ₩16,000 ($12.08).

What is daepae-samgyeopsal (대패삼겹살)?

대패 is a carpenter's hand plane, and this cut is shaved almost as thin as wood curls on a machine built for the job. It crisps at the edge in under a minute while the center stays soft, a different texture from a thick-cut 삼겹살.

The same kitchen serves 차돌박이 (thin-sliced beef brisket) at ₩27,000 ($20.39) and a bowl of 칼국수 (knife-cut noodle soup) at ₩10,000 ($7.55) to close the meal. TV exposure can mean a queue at peak meal times, so it's worth arriving outside the lunch and dinner rush.

Google Maps rating 4.1 from 253 reviews (checked 2026-10-22).

🌶️ Sinkimchi Saengsamgyeopsal: the plate grilled on a bed of kimchi

Sinkimchi Saengsamgyeopsal (신김치생삼겹살) grills its pork belly directly on top of aged kimchi, so the fat renders straight into the kimchi instead of dripping away. The shop is at 서울 마포구 양화로 50, right by Exit 5 of Hapjeong Station on Lines 2 and 6.

It opens late for a reason: daily 16:30-23:30, last order 23:00, with no lunch service.

Order with "삼겹살 주세요" (samgyeopsal ju-se-yo). A serving runs ₩17,000 ($12.83), and 항정살 (pork jowl) is ₩19,000 ($14.34). The kitchen also serves 물냉면 (cold noodles) at ₩6,000 ($4.53) and 계란탕 (egg soup) at ₩5,000 ($3.78) to round out the table.

Google Maps rating 4.0 from 226 reviews (checked 2026-10-22).

On paper all three look open for lunch, but Sinkimchi Saengsamgyeopsal doesn't unlock its door until 16:30, so only the other two work for a midday plate. That ₩21,321 figure prices one 200g plate anywhere in the city; a full signature cut at any of these three undercuts it by at least ₩2,000, badge or no badge.

My take Crossing the city for pork belly sounds like overkill until you factor in the badge. I visit Michelin-listed places often enough, around Gangnam, Samseong, Yeoksam and Apgujeong, that a Bib Gourmand barely registers there anymore. That's exactly why Geumdwaeji Sikdang's got my attention from across town: it's a no-frills counter in Sindang, not a tasting menu, and it earned the same badge my usual spots chase. If I only had one stop on this list, I'd make it that one and treat the wait as the price of admission; the other two sit closer to home for most visitors, so save them for a night you don't want to queue at all.

FAQ
Can I drive and park nearby?
Not at Geumdwaeji Sikdang: no dedicated lot, per its own page. Neither remaining address posts parking details either, so skip the rental altogether; a T-money card covers all three stops better than a tank of gas would.
What if there's no English menu?
Save a screenshot of the dish's Korean spelling before you leave your phone's data behind, then point and say "이거 주세요" (i-geo ju-se-yo) if the staff can't read a translation app. Splitting the bill is the bigger surprise for most visitors: a shared grill table usually pays as one check, not separately per diner.

If you only have one night for samgyeopsal in Seoul, make it Geumdwaeji Sikdang and treat the wait as the cost of the badge. Call ahead to Jayeonseokdolgui if its hour-long gap between listings would wreck your schedule, and save Sinkimchi Saengsamgyeopsal for whichever evening you're already in Mapo.
