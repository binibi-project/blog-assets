Seoul Plaza Ice Rink: Price, Hours, and When to Go

Seoul Plaza's rink has charged ₩1,000 since 2004, but the 2026-27 dates aren't posted yet. Last season's hours, fees, and exactly when to check back.

Quick answer
  • The 2026-27 season isn't announced yet: as of late November, the city's own notices and every English write-up still describe last winter's season, which already ended.
  • Last season cost ₩1,000 (about $0.74) an hour, skates and helmet included, a price that hasn't moved since the rink opened in 2004.
  • Check back in early-to-mid December: bookmark seoulskate.kr and look there first, since that's when the last two seasons' announcements landed.

By seven in the evening in late December, Seoul Plaza turns into a sheet of white light. Office workers in heavy coats slow down on their way to City Hall Station, lean on the railing, and watch a toddler in a borrowed helmet wobble past a traffic cone. Behind the rink, the old stone City Hall glows gold against the dark, the kind of backdrop that looks staged until you remember it has always just been there. You can be out there with them for less than the price of a coffee, once the city finally says when.

💸 Is Seoul Plaza's rink still ₩1,000, and when does it open?

It cost ₩1,000 last winter, and the city hasn't said yet when, or even whether, the rink comes back this season. Seoul's official season notice set the 2025-26 fee at ₩1,000 for a full hour, skates and helmet rental included, running from an opening ceremony on Dec 19, 2025 through Feb 8, 2026, 52 days in total. Searching this week, in Korean or English, turns up nothing newer: every result, including the English coverage a visitor would actually find, still describes that same finished season as if it were current.

That gap is normal, not a bad sign. The rink has returned almost every winter since its 2004 debut, missing only the two pandemic seasons after an early closure on Jan 31, 2020, before coming back on Dec 21, 2022. That 2022 relaunch ran under the headline "19th year, still ₩1,000," which is as clean a confirmation as you'll get that the fee hasn't budged since 2004. A 2011-era city post already called it the rink's "8th year," with about 200,000 visitors that winter alone, so this is a fixture, not a pop-up that might quietly disappear.

🚇 How do you get there, and how do you grab a session?

Take City Hall Station on Subway Lines 1 or 2 and follow signs for Seoul Plaza, out Exit 5 or Exit 6 (the exact number has shifted between recent seasons). The rink sets up at 12 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu, directly in front of Seoul City Hall. Last season's city notice pointed to Exit 6, about 186 meters away, while older seasons' coverage more often cites Exit 5, so following the signage beats memorizing one number.

Online booking opens exactly 7 days before the date you want, and each one-hour session is split evenly: 300 seats sold online, 300 more sold on-site the same day. Cancel an online seat by 23:59 the day before for a refund; an on-site ticket can be refunded any time before that session actually starts. If you'd rather skip the booking app, a weekday morning session still holds 300 walk-up spots, so arrive before the hour you want and buy at the window.

What's a "session" (회차) here?

The rink runs in fixed one-hour blocks all day rather than free skate. You buy a ticket for one specific hour, staff resurface the ice between blocks, and arriving mid-session just means watching from the rail until the next one opens.

🧤 What should you actually bring, and what could shut it down early?

Bring your own gloves, required rather than optional, plus a thick pair of socks, since rental skates run stiffer than the ones you'd lace up at home. Skates and a helmet come with the ticket price, and a separate mini rink handles the youngest kids; one press report from the tail end of last season lists admission to the main ice from age 7 up, so check current signage before bringing a younger child.

The 2022 reopening season barred children under 4 and anyone needing a skate size under 150mm, and capped walk-in groups of 20 or more to Monday through Thursday with advance booking, a rule worth assuming still applies until the new season's notice says otherwise. Last season's rental counter stocked skates from 180mm to 300mm and let you swap sizes on the spot, charged ₩1,500 for gloves if you showed up without a pair, and rented out a coin locker for ₩1,000. Skip packing snacks: food and drinks, water included, aren't allowed on the ice itself, so eat and use the restroom before you line up.

It's an open-air sheet of ice downtown, so weather runs the schedule more than the clock does. Seoul shuts the rink without warning once ultrafine-dust readings spike or snow and rain move in, and sends a text alert to anyone who booked online while posting live updates on a monitor at the ticket booth.

What's "초미세먼지" (ultrafine dust)?

Particles so small they pass straight into the lungs; Korea issues alerts once concentrations cross a set threshold. Outdoor venues like this rink close on those days rather than risk skaters breathing hard in bad air.

Seoul's Han River water bus and its Christmas markets both run on the same honesty-first logic: check the live notice, not last year's write-up, because a city this size changes its own schedule constantly.

FAQ
Does a Discover Seoul Pass get you in free?
Last season, yes: holders of the Discover Seoul Pass walked onto the ice at no charge, on top of whatever else the pass already covers that day. Worth factoring in if you're already weighing that pass against buying attraction tickets separately.
What happens if you drop a phone or a glove on the ice?
Send a direct message to the rink's Instagram account, @seoulwinterfesta: last winter's operator held lost items there through Jan 9, well after that season's closing day. Snap a photo of where you were sitting beforehand so you can describe the spot if you need to.

I'd treat this one as a calendar reminder, not a plan carved in stone. A dollar-ish hour of skating in front of City Hall is worth building an evening around once the dates exist, but there's no reason to promise a December night out on a schedule the city hasn't posted yet. Check seoulskate.kr in the first half of December, book the moment slots open, and bring your own gloves either way.
