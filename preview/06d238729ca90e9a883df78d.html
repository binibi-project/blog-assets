The Discover Seoul Pass: Is the ₩90,000 Card Worth It?

Seoul's official tourist pass bundles 70+ attractions and a 5-day eSIM into four tiers, ₩49,000-130,000, on sale through Oct. 31, 2026.

Quick answer
  • Four tiers run ₩49,000 to ₩130,000: pick a 72- or 120-hour all-access version, or a Pick 3 version for exactly three attractions, and every tier bundles a 5-day eSIM on top.
  • The 72-hour tier is the one Seoul's tourism office itself calls the best value: its own sample itinerary adds up to ₩294,400 in attractions and transit for a ₩90,000 card.
  • Enter the October promo code before Oct. 31: and don't activate the pass until you're actually ready to start sightseeing, since the clock starts at first use, not at purchase.

Gate 13 at Incheon's Terminal 1 never closes. Long after most counters nearby have gone dark, that booth keeps handing new arrivals a plastic card bundled with a data SIM, a transit card and free entry to places they haven't picked yet.

A Seoul local notices something a first-time visitor might not: a round-the-clock pickup counter isn't an accident, it's a bet that most tourists land exhausted and under-planned, not refreshed and ready to book attractions one by one. The Discover Seoul Pass is built around exactly that moment, and it mostly earns the trust. The real question is which of its four versions is worth the money once you're actually standing in the city.

The pass is easy to buy and easy to misjudge, and the two mistakes run in opposite directions: paying for more pass than you'll ever use, or skipping it right before it would have paid for itself twice over.

💳 Which pass is actually the best deal?

The 72-hour All-Inclusive pass is the only tier Seoul's own tourism office tags "Best Value," and its own numbers back that up. Four tiers exist, split between two all-access passes sold by the hour and two Pick 3 passes sold by the attraction.

What's the difference between the All-Inclusive and Pick 3 passes?

The All-Inclusive pass lets you visit every partner attraction once each, for as long as its 72- or 120-hour clock runs. The Pick 3 pass instead asks you to choose just three partner attractions or services up front, and the Theme Park version swaps one of those three for a full-size theme park (Lotte World, Everland or Seoul Land).

The clock itself has a quirk worth knowing before you land. Think of it less like a parking meter that starts the second you grab a ticket, and more like one that waits until you actually pull into the spot. The pass doesn't start counting down the moment you pay for it: it starts the moment you redeem your first free benefit, whether that's scanning a QR code at an attraction, receiving your AREX airport-train code, or boarding the K-Limousine airport bus.

Downloading the eSIM or topping up the card pass doesn't start anything. Grab your AREX code the moment you land just because the counter's open, and by the time you've slept and had breakfast, a chunk of your 72 hours is already gone before you've seen a single palace.

That timing matters because the value case for this pass rests on using most of it fast. Seoul's tourism office built its own sample three-day itinerary and tallied ₩294,400 in regular admission against the ₩90,000 card price.

That math only holds if you'd genuinely visit most of this list anyway. Skip the aquarium, the cruise and the exhibition, and you're left with just the hanbok rental, the tower and the airport bus: ₩79,000 (~$59.65) of real value against a ₩90,000 card. At that narrower pace, the pass has already cost more than it saved.

✈️ What "free transportation" actually means

The All-Inclusive passes cover one-way rides on the airport train and airport bus, plus one city-tour-bus route each, not everyday subway and bus fare. The pass's own FAQ spells out the fine print: Airport Railroad Express (AREX) and the K-Limousine airport bus, each good for one one-way trip, plus a single day's course on the Tiger Bus and the Yellow Balloon City Bus, choosing either the daytime or nighttime route on each, never both.

What is AREX?

The Airport Railroad Express, the train line connecting Incheon International Airport to central Seoul. It runs both an all-stop local train and a faster, fewer-stop express train, and the pass covers one one-way ride on it.

What are the Tiger Bus and Yellow Balloon City Bus?

Seoul's open-top sightseeing bus routes, each running a daytime and a separate nighttime course past the city's main landmarks. The pass covers one course on each bus, so riding both the day and night version of the same bus isn't included.

The physical Card Pass also doubles as an ordinary transit card, which is a different benefit than the free rides above. Regular subway and city-bus fares still draw down a balance you top up yourself, the same as any other transit card in Seoul. The "Transportation" line on the pass's own feature list is really two separate things: a short list of specific free rides, and a reloadable card for everything else.

🎟️ Who can actually buy one, and the sale that ends this month

Korean residents can't use this pass at all, even as a gift, and anyone who can should grab October's discount codes before they expire. The pass is built for foreign tourists and overseas residents holding foreign citizenship or permanent residency abroad; a Korean national living in Korea is denied entry at the attraction gate even standing next to a foreign friend who paid for the card, and the only way a Korean resident can be involved at all is buying one as a gift for someone else.

For anyone who does qualify, Seoul's tourism office is running an Autumn Sale from Oct. 12 through Oct. 31, 2026, with a separate promo code for each tier.

My take The worry I'd guess most visitors carry isn't whether this pass is a scam, it's whether they're disciplined enough to actually use ₩90,000 worth of it in three days. I live in Gangnam-gu, Seoul, which puts me on the wrong side of this pass's own rules: a ticket booth would turn me away even standing right next to a foreign friend who bought one. That's not a loophole in the design, it's the point, since Seoul built this card for people who'd otherwise juggle a dozen separate tickets, not for someone who already carries a local transit card. If I were visiting instead of living here, I'd pick a tier only after writing down the four or five things I'd actually do, and I'd skip Pick 3 Basic entirely unless I already knew the exact three stops before I paid.

FAQ
Can I get a refund if I buy the wrong tier?
Yes, but only before you redeem any part of it, and the process depends on where you bought it. An unused Mobile Pass purchased directly from the operator can be canceled within 30 days through the app's own order menu, with the money landing back in roughly 5-7 days; buy through a third-party reseller instead, and that platform's own cancellation policy governs, not the operator's. An unused physical card needs its receipt handed over in person at a walk-in counter within that same 30-day window.
Is this pass actually popular, or a niche product for a handful of visitors?
Independent press coverage put 2025 sales at 710,000 units, a figure the operator doesn't publish on its own website. That volume is also a clue to why the round-the-clock counter exists at all: enough travelers grab one straight off an overnight flight to justify staffing it every night of the year.

This pass earns its price on a packed, see-everything trip, and loses money on a slow one. Write down what you actually want to see before you choose a tier, not after, since the cheapest version of this mistake is buying Pick 3 Basic for three things you were always going to visit for free anyway.
