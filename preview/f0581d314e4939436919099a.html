Quick answer
  • Buy it for ₩3,000-5,000: at any CU, GS25 or 7-Eleven, or an info desk at a Seoul subway station, no forms involved.
  • The card itself saves you ₩100 a ride, and free transfers within 30 minutes are the part that actually adds up over a day.

Ever tapped a card at a Seoul subway gate and wondered why the light turns green before you've even glanced at a fare screen? That's the whole design: you're not supposed to think about it. The gate reads the card, deducts something, and moves on to the next person before the doors even finish opening behind you. Underneath that two-second non-event is a fare system with real numbers attached, and most visitors never see them until they're standing at a machine wondering how much cash to feed it.

The card is called T-money, and it's the same piece of plastic whether you're riding the subway, a city bus, a cross-town taxi, or grabbing a canned coffee from a vending machine on the platform.

What is T-money?

A rechargeable prepaid card (and now a phone-based option for some Korean-registered devices) used across Seoul and most of the country for public transportation and small purchases. You load cash onto it and it deducts fares automatically at the gate or reader.

💳 How much does a T-money card cost, and where do you buy one?

A basic card runs ₩3,000-5,000 (about $2.20-$3.65), and it's sold at convenience stores nationwide, including CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, as well as information desks at Seoul subway stations on Lines 1 through 8. Character-design or novelty cards at gift shops tend to sit at the higher end of that range; a plain card at a convenience store counter is usually the cheaper option.

The card starts empty, so you load cash onto it right after buying it, either at the same convenience store counter or at a subway station's ticket vending and card reload machine. You can add up to ₩500,000 in a single top-up, which is more headroom than almost anyone needs in one visit, but it does mean you're not stuck reloading in small amounts if you're staying a week or two.

If you'd rather not carry a physical card, some Korean-registered phones can hold T-money digitally, but that setup generally needs a Korean carrier account, so most visitors are better off with the plastic version.

🚇 Is the card actually cheaper than a paper ticket?

Yes, by ₩100 a ride, and the bigger saving is the free transfer. An adult subway ride costs ₩1,550 with a T-money card, versus ₩1,650 for a single-journey paper ticket bought at a machine. Teen fares (ages 13-18) run ₩900 with a card, and child fares (ages 6-12) are ₩550. These have been the fares since June 28, 2025, when the city raised the adult base fare by ₩150.

Tap out of the subway at Gangnam, then hop on a bus ten minutes later: the card treats that as one trip, not two. Think of your T-money balance as a running tab for the whole leg of your day, not a separate ticket for every vehicle you board.

As long as you tap the next ride within 30 minutes of tapping out (stretched to 60 minutes between 21:00 and 07:00), the transfer is free or you only pay the difference in distance. A subway-to-bus commute that would cost ₩3,100 as two separate cash fares runs ₩1,550 once the transfer kicks in.

What's hwan-seung (환승)?

Korean for "transfer." It's the word printed on transit-app screens and the reason locals rarely think twice about switching from a bus to the subway mid-trip: within the transfer window, it's priced as one continuous ride, not two.

There's also a quieter discount: tap in before 6:30 a.m. with a card and the first ride of the day is 20% off, which brings the adult fare down to ₩1,240. It only applies to that first tap, and only with a card, not cash.

🔋 How do you reload it, and get money back before you leave?

Reload it the same places you bought it: convenience stores or a subway station's vending and reload machine. Cash goes in, balance goes up, no card swap needed, and the same ₩500,000-per-transaction cap applies.

Refunding a leftover balance is where the fee shows up. Convenience stores will hand back cash on the spot, minus a ₩500 fee, for balances up to ₩20,000 at GS25 or 7-Eleven, or up to ₩30,000 at CU or emart24. If your balance is smaller than the ₩1,550 base fare, the fee is waived and you get the full amount back. Anything between ₩20,000 and ₩50,000 goes through a subway station's customer service desk instead, and refunds over ₩50,000 have to go through T-money Town, near Exit 10 of Seoul Station.

What is T-money Town?

The T-money operator's customer service counter near Seoul Station, Exit 10. It's the only place set up to handle large refunds, card replacements and other issues a convenience store clerk can't process at the register.

My take I'm a CPA, so a fare system that behaves exactly the way its own published table says it will is satisfying in a small way most people wouldn't notice. The ₩500 refund fee looks like a nuisance until you actually do the math on it: spend the last few thousand won down to under ₩1,550 at a convenience store before you fly out, and the refund is free instead of costing you ₩500 for nothing. That's the call I'd make in a visitor's shoes: treat the tail end of your balance as a snack budget, not a refund to go chase across town.

Check before you go
  • Buy at any convenience store, not just the airport: CU, GS25, 7-Eleven all sell and reload T-money cards, so there's no need to hunt for a specific kiosk.
  • Keep your balance under ₩1,550 near the end of your trip: refund a smaller amount and the ₩500 fee disappears.
  • Cash out anything over ₩50,000 at T-money Town, not a convenience store: the store-counter refund caps out at ₩20,000-30,000 depending on the chain.
FAQ
Is a T-money card worth buying for a two- or three-day trip?
Yes. The card itself costs ₩3,000-5,000, and it pays that back in about 30-50 rides' worth of the ₩100-per-ride discount over paper tickets, on top of skipping the line at a ticket machine every time.
Can I get a full refund when I leave Korea?
Only if your leftover balance is under the ₩1,550 base fare. Anything above that loses a ₩500 fee at a convenience store, so it's cheaper to spend the balance down to under ₩1,550 first.

None of this is complicated once you've done it once, and that's sort of the point of the system. Buy the card at the first convenience store you pass, load more than the bare minimum, and spend the last few thousand won on snacks instead of chasing a refund across town.
