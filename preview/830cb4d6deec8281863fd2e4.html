How to Make Sundubu Jjigae (Soft Tofu Stew) at Home

A sundubu jjigae recipe with real quantities, the step most home kitchens skip, and where to find sundubu and gochugaru outside Korea.

Quick answer
  • One pot, 25 minutes: kimchi, a little pork, and a tube of soft tofu simmered together, no separate stock to make ahead.
  • Kimchi stays a supporting player: a third of a cup is the amount in this recipe, not a doubled scoop, because the tofu is meant to be the star.
  • Check first: stir-fry the gochugaru in sesame oil before any liquid goes in, the one step that keeps the broth from tasting thin.

Most homemade sundubu jjigae comes out thin, a little sour, and nowhere near as red as the restaurant version, and kimchi usually takes the blame first. It rarely deserves it. The dish only needs about fifteen minutes once the pot is on the burner, but a wrong move in the first three changes the whole bowl: skip the right step and even good kimchi can't save a broth that was never built to carry flavor in the first place.

Pour the tofu in at the end and it barely holds together, soft white curds collapsing into broth that's gone from sharp red to a deep, cloudy orange. If you time the egg right, it's still trembling, half-set, by the time it reaches the table.

What is sundubu (순두부)?

Extra-soft tofu that hasn't been strained or pressed, sold in sealed plastic tubes rather than blocks. It's too delicate to cut cleanly, so recipes break it into rough chunks straight from the tube, and that looseness is exactly what gives the stew its texture.

🍲 The recipe: sundubu jjigae for two

Two servings, 25 minutes end to end, and one pot the whole way through. That's Korean Bapsang's kimchi-and-pork version, rated 4.72 out of 5 by 401 home cooks.

Ingredient Amount
Sundubu (extra-soft tofu) 1 package, 12 oz (about 340g)
Kimchi, thinly sliced ⅓ cup
Pork or beef 3 oz (about 85g)
Gochugaru (Korean chili flakes) 1-3 tsp
Garlic, minced 2 tsp
Sesame oil 1 tbsp
Water or anchovy broth 1 cup (about 240ml)
Kimchi juice 2-3 tbsp
Saewujeot (salted shrimp) or salt ½ tsp, to taste
Black pepper pinch
Scallion, chopped 1
Egg (optional) 1

Source: Korean Bapsang's kimchi sundubu jjigae recipe card, checked 2026-10-01.

Use fully fermented kimchi, not a fresh tub. The recipe's own author is specific about it: "for best results, always use fully fermented kimchi to cook with", since underripe kimchi hasn't built the sourness the broth is supposed to carry.

  1. Cut the meat and kimchi into small, thin strips. Add them to a pot with the gochugaru, garlic and sesame oil over medium heat.

  2. Stir-fry until the meat is almost cooked, 3-4 minutes.

  3. Pour in the water (or broth) and the kimchi juice. Bring it to a boil.

  4. Add the soft tofu in big chunks, then stir in the salted shrimp or salt. Let it simmer a few minutes, crack in the egg if using, and finish with the scallion.

🔥 Why does homemade sundubu jjigae taste thin?

The broth goes flat when the gochugaru never gets its moment in hot oil. Cooking the chili flakes in the sesame oil with the garlic, before any liquid touches the pot, "creates a chili infused oil flavor, which is delicious," in the recipe author's own words. Pour in cold water first and skip that step, and the color turns pale orange instead of a deep red, no matter how much gochugaru goes in afterward.

The other common mistake runs the opposite direction: too much kimchi. One commenter on the recipe doubled the amount to two-thirds of a cup; the author's reply was that "the tofu should be the star of the dish, not kimchi". A third of a cup is enough to season the broth without drowning out the curds it's named for.

What is gochugaru (고춧가루)?

Korean chili flakes made from sun-dried red peppers, seedless, with a coarser grind and a sweeter, smokier edge than the generic crushed red pepper sold at most Western grocery stores. It's not an interchangeable swap: recipes written for gochugaru assume that milder, deeper heat.

🛒 Buying the ingredients outside Korea

Sundubu and gochugaru both show up at any Korean grocery chain, no special trip required. A 14 oz tube of silken sundubu runs $1.99 at H Mart, and a 1 lb bag of coarse gochugaru is $5.99, enough for many batches of this stew.

Saewujeot, the salted shrimp, is the one item worth skipping if it's hard to find: the recipe itself lists plain salt as the alternative, with no separate amount to work out. Mushrooms are a solid stand-in for the meat, according to the same recipe, which makes a vegetarian bowl a small substitution rather than a rewrite.

What is Chodang (초당)?

A neighborhood in Gangneung, on Korea's east coast, named after the pen name of a Joseon-era official who made tofu with seawater instead of brine. Today it's a cluster of restaurants built around that same seawater method, officially promoted as Chodang Sundubu Village.

Go deeper
  • If you have one more day Let the kimchi sit out at room temperature overnight first if it's still mild. A few extra hours of fermenting gets it closer to the sourness the recipe is written around.
  • What locals argue about Whether seafood or meat makes the better bowl. Hamyang County's own regional version is prepared with galbi (ribs) and clams together, while most home kitchens pick one protein, not both.
  • Watch for Kimchi that's still crisp and fresh. The recipe is built around fully fermented kimchi, so a tub that's only a few days old will leave the broth tasting flatter than this recipe promises.
FAQ
Is store-bought kimchi actually vegan?
Often not, even behind a label showing nothing but vegetables. Most jars rely on jeotgal, salted squid, anchovy or shrimp, for their umami punch. Scan the ingredient panel for jeotgal or fish sauce before trusting a jar on looks alone.
Can I firm the egg up more than this calls for?
Yes — leave the lid on one minute longer after cracking it in, away from direct flame, so it sets rather than staying glossy and loose. Every quantity and every earlier step stays exactly as written.

Fifteen minutes at the stove is a small ask for a bowl this specific, and most of what separates a flat pot from a good one happens before the tofu ever goes in. Bloom the gochugaru in the oil, keep the kimchi to a third of a cup, and use a tub that's actually had time to sour.
