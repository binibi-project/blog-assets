Seoul's K-Drama Soul Spot Markers: 3 You Can Visit

Seoul tagged 13 real K-drama filming spots with blue-plaque markers in 2024. Three you can visit: a department store, a museum and a stream bridge.

Quick answer
  • Three real filming spots, one city program: The Hyundai Seoul (free), Woori Yetdol Museum (₩3,000) and a Cheonggyecheon bridge (free), each marked with an official blue-plaque-style sign.
  • The sign does the explaining: scan the QR code on each marker for a free, AI-narrated backstory in your language, no tour guide needed.
  • Set your expectations first: none of these looks like the show once you're standing in front of it, and that's fine; the story below tells you what you're actually looking at.

Ever recognized a scene the second a drama's camera panned across it, then had no real way to confirm you were right? That gap, between loving a show and knowing where it was actually shot, is exactly what Seoul's city government tried to close in September 2024, with a small blue sign and a QR code at each confirmed location. No guesswork, no fan-made maps with half the pins wrong, just an official marker standing where the crew actually stood.

It's a blue-plaque-style marker, modeled on London's, installed at real K-drama filming locations around the city. Seoul announced the first 13 markers on September 25, 2024, chosen from a shortlist of 30 locations picked through big-data analysis, a global popularity survey and expert review by Hallyu specialists. Dramas went first; film and K-pop locations are meant to follow.

What is a Soul Spot (소울 스팟)?

A small, slate-and-film-motif plaque the city installs at a confirmed filming location. Scan its QR code and an AI-generated voice, recorded by MBC announcer Kim Dae-ho from a script by culture critic Jeong Deok-hyeon, narrates what was filmed there, in Korean, English, Chinese or Japanese.

Two of the first 13 markers belong to Queen of Tears (눈물의 여왕), the 2024 tvN hit: one at The Hyundai Seoul, one at Woori Yetdol Museum. A third pair, at a Cheonggyecheon bridge and Nodeul Island, marks Marry My Husband (내 남편과 결혼해줘). All three spots below are easy subway rides apart, so a single afternoon covers them.

🏢 Is The Hyundai Seoul really a filming location, or just a department store?

Both: it's a working mall at 108 Yeoui-daero in Yeongdeungpo-gu, and it's also the real building that stood in for the drama's fictional "Queens" department store. Entry is free, since it's simply a shopping center. Getting there is straightforward: from Yeouido Station (Lines 5 and 9), Exit 3 connects via an underground moving walkway to the basement-level entrance, and Exit 4 leads straight to the main gate about 400 meters on. From Yeouinaru Station (Line 5) Exit 1, a short walk past Gongjak Apartment reaches the back entrance instead.

The store's own site generally lists hours around 10:30 a.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays, stretching to 8:30 p.m. Friday through Sunday, though the exact listing can shift around holidays and events, so it's worth a quick check before you go.

Walk in expecting a mall, not a set. The building opened on February 26, 2021, three years before the drama used it. Nothing inside is dressed up for Queen of Tears; you're looking at the same atriums and escalators any Seoul shopper sees, just with the knowledge that a camera crew once filmed here too.

🗿 What's actually inside Woori Yetdol Museum, the "home" from Queen of Tears?

A private open-air collection of centuries-old Korean stone sculptures in Seongbuk-dong, whose exterior played the drama's wealthy Baek Hyun-woo's family home. The museum sits at 66 Daesagwan-ro 13-gil, Seongbuk-dong, inside Seoul's old Embassy Street district. Outdoor-exhibit admission runs ₩3,000 (about $2.21) for adults, ₩2,000 for youth and ₩1,000 for children, as of a July 2025 update to the museum's own listing; indoor exhibits are priced separately by show, so the museum asks visitors to call ahead for those.

What is 옛돌 (yetdol, "old stone")?

Hand-carved Korean stone figures, like tomb guardians, grave markers and village totems, once common across the countryside but rarely seen together. This museum gathers hundreds of them into one outdoor collection.

Winter hours (December to February) run 10 to 5; summer hours (March to November) run 10 to 5 on weekdays and 10 to 6 on weekends, with last entry an hour before closing. It closes Mondays, the next day instead if Monday is a holiday, plus January 1st.

A Seoul city media hub route guide sends visitors in from Hansung University Station (Line 4), Exit 6, on village bus No. 02, getting off at the museum stop. It's still worth confirming the route on a map app before you climb aboard, since bus numbers can change.

Seoul's own travel feature on the drama's locations points visitors to walk on from here to Gilsangsa Temple, a short distance further into the neighborhood.

🌉 Which Cheonggyecheon bridge shows up in the most K-dramas?

Gwangtong Bridge, a stone footbridge in Seorin-dong, officially marked for Marry My Husband but also used in Queen of Tears and Wedding Impossible. It sits along the restored Cheonggyecheon stream, in front of the SK Seorin Building in downtown Jongno-gu. There's no ticket here: it's an open public footbridge, free at any hour.

What is Cheonggyecheon (청계천)?

A central Seoul stream covered over for decades and dug back out in a restoration project; the city reopened the main 5.84-kilometer stretch, from Cheonggye Plaza downstream, on September 30, 2005. Gwangtong Bridge is one of several historic crossings rebuilt along its route.

The bridge carries real history under its modern stonework. Its original stones came from the tomb of Queen Gang, Taejo Yi Seong-gye's second wife; after Prince Yi Bang-won seized power, he moved her tomb and had the leftover stones built into the bridge instead. The bridge was paved over when Cheonggyecheon was covered in 1958, and Jongno-gu cultural tours today use a smaller restored model standing in front of Shinhan Bank, since the carved originals remain buried in the culvert below.

On paper, all three sound like a tidy loop, but they sit in three different corners of the city, Yeouido, Seongbuk-dong and Jongno, so budget a subway transfer or two between stops rather than trying to walk the whole route.

My take If you've ever stood in a real filming location and felt a little let down that it just looks like itself, that reaction is normal, not a sign you picked the wrong spot. A department store is still a department store once the cameras leave, and a stone museum's charm has nothing to do with which drama used its gate. I'd go in for the thing itself, the stone carvings, the view over Cheonggyecheon, and treat the Soul Spot marker as a nice extra fact to scan on the way out, not the reason the trip was worth it.

FAQ
How do I find the other 10 first-phase markers, or check which 30 spots made the list?
Seoul publishes the full set on two official map tools: the Visit Seoul website's Hallyu section and the Smart Seoul Map. Confirmed names from the first batch include Deoksugung's stone wall path, Jeongdong Observatory and Baek In-je House (Reborn Rich), and Seokchon Lake's eastern bank with Garak Market (Crash Course in Romance), alongside the three in this guide.
Do I need to visit in person for the QR narration to work, or can I just look it up online?
The narration is tied to the physical marker's QR code, meant to be scanned on-site, but the same background on each location is also searchable through the Visit Seoul and Smart Seoul Map listings beforehand. Scanning in person still gets you the four-language audio version without typing anything.

None of these three needs a reservation or a ticket you buy in advance, so the easiest plan is simply whichever station you're already near. Start at The Hyundai Seoul if you're in Yeouido for the day, or work backward from Gwangtong Bridge if you're already walking Cheonggyecheon.
