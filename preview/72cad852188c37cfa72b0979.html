Busan in Two Days: What Every Stop Actually Costs

Seoul to Busan just got cheaper: the KTX fare fell to ₩54,400 in September. A real two-day route, with what every stop, ride and meal costs.

Quick answer
  • The fare just dropped: KTX from Seoul to Busan is ₩54,400 one way since September 1, ₩5,400 less than a month earlier.
  • Two days, two neighborhoods: day one is Gamcheon and the old downtown, day two is the temple, the beach and the sky deck.
  • Before you book: pick a specific KTX departure and check the observatory forecast before paying for the view.

Step off the platform at Busan Station and salt air reaches you before the exit gates do. Two hours earlier you were underground in Seoul, and now the escalator climbs toward container cranes and a harbor close enough that the ferry horns carry into the concourse. It's a port city stacked over hills instead of spread across a river plain, and the skyline keeps changing shape as you move through it. Two days is enough to see why people who've only done Seoul come back from Busan wanting to go back again.

Yes, the ₩54,400 KTX fare is real, and it's ₩5,400 cheaper than the same seat cost in August. Korail folded its old rival SR into one combined operator on September 1, 2026, and cut the standard-class Seoul-Busan fare from ₩59,800 to ₩54,400 as part of an average 10% cut across the KTX network, Hankyung reported once the new fare notice went out. The Fair Trade Commission's merger approval keeps that price fixed for three years, so it isn't a launch discount that vanishes by winter.

Why did the KTX fare just change?

Korail and its former rival SR, which ran the SRT trains, merged into one company in August 2026 after antitrust approval. The combined operator brought KTX prices down to SRT's cheaper level across the whole network, not just on this route.

The fastest train covers Seoul Station to Busan Station in 2 hours 18 minutes, with the first departure at 05:13 and the last at 22:49. A same-day round trip is technically possible, but it trades away Gamcheon's morning light and the temple's early quiet, which is most of what makes two days worth it.

Spring and fall suit this route best. Both days involve steep steps and open coastline, and Busan's summer humidity and monsoon rain turn the Gamcheon hillside and the temple's stone bridge into a much harder walk than the map suggests.

🏘️ Day 1: Gamcheon, the old downtown and one paid view

Start at Gamcheon Culture Village, then work down toward the harbor by early afternoon. Entrance to the hillside village is free, and the ₩2,000 stamp map sold at the Haneulmaru information center by the entrance routes you past all nine collection points, with two postcards waiting if you fill it in. The alleys are steep and narrow enough that a photo at every corner adds up fast, so budget more time than the map suggests.

What is Gamcheon Culture Village?

A hillside neighborhood of small, brightly painted homes, built by refugees during the Korean War and later turned into an open-air art village through a 2009 government project. It's still a residential area, so the quiet lanes behind the murals are someone's front yard.

From Gamcheon, a city bus or a short taxi ride puts you at Yongdusan Park, free to enter any time. Busan Tower's observation deck inside the park runs ₩8,000 for an adult ticket, open 10:00 to 22:00 with ticketing closing at 21:30. That leaves the whole evening open for Jagalchi Market or BIFF Square, both an easy walk downhill from the park gate.

An ₩8,000 ticket for a 360-degree view over the harbor is the cheaper of this trip's two paid viewpoints, and it stays open late enough to fit in after a slow dinner.

🏖️ Day 2: the temple, the beach and the ₩29,000 view

Start at Haedong Yonggungsa before the tour buses arrive. The clifftop temple opens at 04:30 and charges no admission, with last entry at 18:50. Early morning or just before closing are the two windows when the stone bridge to the main halls isn't shoulder to shoulder.

What is Haedong Yonggungsa?

One of the few Korean temples built directly on coastal rock instead of in the mountains, founded in 1376 and rebuilt after the Korean War. A stone bridge carries visitors from the parking-lot side to the temple halls, with the sea running underneath both ways.

The ₩29,000 ticket at Busan X the Sky only pays off on a clear day. Adult admission to the observatory in the Haeundae LCT towers is ₩29,000, ₩26,000 for children and seniors, open 10:00 to 21:00 with last admission at 20:30. On a hazy afternoon the view turns to gray haze instead of the postcard shot, so check the forecast before locking in a timed ticket.

Reaching either stop from downtown means at least one Section 2 fare on the subway, ₩1,800 by transit card or ₩1,900 for a single-use ticket, against ₩1,600/₩1,700 for a shorter Section 1 ride within downtown.

What does "Section 2" mean on the Busan subway?

Fares step up once a single ride passes roughly 10 km. A short hop within one neighborhood is usually Section 1; crossing from downtown out to Haeundae or Gijang, where Yonggungsa sits, is Section 2, which costs ₩200 more per ride.

My take I've been to Busan within the last two years, and if I went back for exactly two days, I'd spend more of day one wandering Gamcheon's back alleys than paying for the tower; the view from partway up the hill is free and nearly as good. Of the two paid tickets in this plan, I'd keep Busan X the Sky over Busan Tower, since the height over Haeundae is the one that actually changes what you see. I live in Gangnam and don't get to the coast often, so for me a Busan weekend is still a real break, not a routine.

  • Watch for The last KTX out of Busan Station leaves at 22:10 on the fastest run. Miss it, and a hotel night you didn't plan for is the next line item.
Check before you go
  • Book a specific KTX departure, not just a date: the ₩54,400 fare applies to standard class, and the fastest trains fill first.
  • Check the forecast before buying the Busan X the Sky ticket: ₩29,000 buys a view that disappears completely on a hazy day.
  • Time your last train back: the last standard KTX from Busan Station leaves at 22:10, and missing it changes your whole budget.
FAQ
Is two days really enough for Busan?
Yes, for the two neighborhoods in this route: Gamcheon and the old downtown, then the temple and the coast. It's not enough for Busan plus a side trip to Gyeongju or Jeju in the same weekend.
What's the cheapest way to do this exact trip?
Skip Busan X the Sky and keep Busan Tower instead. That trades the ₩29,000 ticket for the ₩8,000 one, a ₩21,000 difference, and neither the subway nor the KTX fare changes either way.

Cut Busan X the Sky if the budget is tight, but keep the temple; free stops are rarely the ones worth skipping.
