The Real Cost of a K-Pop Birthday Ad on Seoul's Subway

Hongik Univ.'s biggest subway ad panel runs ₩7,000,000 a month, and Hapjeong's price roughly tripled in 2025. What a K-pop fan actually pays for one.

Quick answer
  • Prices depend entirely on the station: a lighting panel can run from the ₩1 million range at a quiet stop to ₩7,000,000 a month at Hongik University.
  • You don't have to pay for one yourself: fan-voting apps give a free station ad to whichever idol wins that month's poll.
  • Booking one from abroad costs more than the raw station fee: compare a concierge service's package price against the agency rates below before paying.

Ever stood on a Seoul subway platform and noticed an ad with no product and no company name? Just a stranger's face, a birthday message, and captions in a language you might not read. That's not a glitch in the system. It's a rented screen, paid for one small transfer at a time by people who will likely never meet the person on it, and if you stay long enough to watch the full loop, there's something hard not to feel in that, whether or not you know the group.

🚇 How much does a subway birthday ad actually cost?

It depends almost entirely on which station you pick, and the range runs from the ₩1 million range to ₩7,000,000 a month. Korea JoongAng Daily's February 2022 reporting laid out a station-by-station rate card, topping out around ₩7,000,000 ($5,242) a month at busy transfer hubs like Gangnam and Hongik University, and bottoming out around ₩3,000,000 ($2,247) at quieter stops like Ewha Womans University. The full station-by-station breakdown is in the table below; those figures are from 2022, so treat them as a floor, not today's invoice.

What is a 조명광고 (lighting ad)?

A backlit poster panel mounted on a subway platform wall or screen door, usually sized in centimeters (a 250cm or 400cm panel, for instance). It's the classic format for a fan-funded birthday ad, distinct from a digital screen that cycles through video.

More recent numbers confirm prices keep moving. By June 2025, a 250cm lighting panel at Hongik University started at ₩2,500,000 ($1,872) and the station's largest, 400cm panel cost ₩7,000,000 ($5,242) a month. Over the same period, Hapjeong Station's 300cm panel jumped from the ₩1 million range (roughly $750) to more than ₩3,000,000 ($2,247), a change Financial News traced to one ad agency winning Line 6's rights at close to double the minimum bid, then pushing existing advertisers toward the new rate too.

Hapjeong's jump actually emptied the station out. Within weeks of the increase, only one or two campaigns were left on roughly 20 boards that fans used to fill, and the advertisers who stuck around either moved to Gangnam and Hongik University or agreed to cover the higher cost themselves. A station known for being the cheap, reliable option can stop being either almost overnight.

📈 Why has this gotten so big, and why do prices keep spiking?

The volume comes from a trend that took off around 2016, and the price spikes come from ad agencies bidding against each other for a line's rights, not from Seoul Metro itself. Seoul's subway carried just 76 fan-funded idol ads in 2014; that reached 1,576 by September 2018 and 2,166 in 2019, with BTS alone appearing on 227 of them. By 2025, Seoul Metro Corporation had reviewed 12,433 idol ads in a single year, with another 8,012 logged in just the first eight months of 2026.

What is a 총대 (chongdae)?

Literally "the one who carries the gun barrel first." In fandom, it's the fan who fronts a group-buy or ad campaign: collecting money, dealing with the ad agency, and taking the financial risk if not enough people chip in.

A fan account called Mokumoku gives a sense of what that scale looks like in a single week. Ahead of BTS member Jungkook's September 1 birthday, the account's campaign filled 54 digital panels across 24 Seoul subway stations between August 26 and September 1, 2026, including Hongik University and Gangnam, plus Jamsil and Express Bus Terminal. A Japanese tourist who visited Hongik University Station the day before the birthday waited at the platform screen until Jungkook's video played before raising her phone to film it.

What is 디지털 사이니지 (digital signage)?

A screen, not a printed panel, built into a platform's wall or screen doors. It cycles through several ads or video clips on a loop, which is why a fan campaign can book a short window instead of renting a whole panel for a month.

🗳️ Can you get your idol a subway ad without paying for it yourself?

Yes, in two different ways: for free through a fan-voting app, or for a few thousand dollars through an English-language concierge service. Fan-voting apps such as Youpick hand a free station ad to whichever idol wins that month's poll; one round sends a Hongik University Airport Railroad digital-signage ad to the winner of a "birthday idol" vote, timed to land on the artist's actual birthday. A separate monthly poll's category winners get a billboard on the COEX Media Tower in Gangnam, and that round's single top-ranked "Best Pick" winner goes on to a board at Shinsegae Centum City's Famille mall, also in Gangnam, plus an unannounced bonus reward.

For a fan outside Korea who wants to book one directly, Creatrip's "K-Pop Idol Subway Birthday Ad Service" sells a one-month Lightbox Ad (400cm by 225cm) or a three-month Poster Ad (77cm by 107cm), with staff who build the design from a fan's photos, visit the station to confirm the install, and communicate in English, Chinese or Japanese. The page lists a starting price of $7,117.04, discounted 14% from an $8,249.46 reference price, which converts to roughly ₩9,500,000 at the rate above, well above the raw station fees in the table earlier. That markup is the price of not having to negotiate with a Korean ad agency yourself.

My take If a wall of ads for someone you've never heard of looks less like a tribute and more like a fandom flex, that reaction is fair, and it isn't really the point of it. If I were weighing whether to put real money into one, I'd try the free route first: a vote costs nothing, and losing just means trying again next month instead of eating a concierge invoice priced in dollars. The one worth actually paying for is the one with a named station and a photo-confirmation step built into the price, not a stranger's promise that the ad went up somewhere.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour, ride through Hongik University or Gangnam Station around a known birthday or comeback date; both stations turn up again and again in these campaigns, and a loop usually plays every few minutes.
  • What fans argue about: whether a single agency winning a line's rights and then raising fees on everyone already booked, which is exactly what happened on Line 6 in 2025, is normal bidding or a squeeze on fandom goodwill.
  • Watch for price jumps timed right before a big birthday or comeback season; Hapjeong's increase landed in early June 2025, pushing campaigns that had already started fundraising to move stations mid-plan.
FAQ
Could the ad get rejected or pulled after a fan has already paid for it?
Yes, if it breaks the operator's content guidelines, and a birthday message isn't automatically exempt from that review. Reporting on the broader ad program notes that political messages face tighter limits, that some past pro-feminist fan campaigns were turned down, and that the pushback over those rejections led officials to revise the approval guidelines rather than loosen them outright. A design built around a slogan, not just a face and a date, is the riskier one to clear.
Does the idol ever actually acknowledge one of these?
Sometimes, informally. There are documented cases of Korean celebrities stopping by their own fan-funded ad in person and taking a selfie in front of it as a thank-you, which is about the closest thing to a direct response a campaign usually gets. A visit like that isn't guaranteed or scheduled, so don't plan a trip around catching one.

None of this requires a credit card to start. Voting on a fan-reward app costs nothing, and watching one of these ads loop through its full run costs even less. If a fee is genuinely on the table, the Hongik University vote-based route is worth trying before any invoice priced in dollars.
