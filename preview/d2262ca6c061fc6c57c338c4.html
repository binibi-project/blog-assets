Three samgyetang shops rated 4.2 on Google Maps in Seochon, City Hall and Yongsan, each with address, station exit, hours and bowl price.

Ever ordered a whole boiled chicken and called it comfort food? A bowl of samgyetang arrives still simmering in its own stone pot, broth gone milky with garlic and rice, one chicken leg tipping over the rim before your spoon even gets there. Koreans eat it hardest during sambok, the three hottest stretches of the lunar calendar, on the idea that a scalding broth does more for an overheated body than a cold drink ever could.

The first sip of that cloudy broth tends to go quiet in a way most soup doesn't. Seoul has hundreds of counters selling some version of this dish, but three keep coming up when you ask where the real one is.

Location, not flavor, is the thing to plan around. These three sit in three different neighborhoods on three different subway lines, so treat this as a shortlist to pick from, not a route to run in one day.

What is samgyetang (삼계탕)?

A whole young chicken stuffed with sticky rice, garlic and ginseng, then simmered until the meat falls off the bone. It's sold as a single-serving stone-pot meal, not a shared soup, and the broth is meant to be drunk straight from the bowl.

🍲 Tosokchon: the name most people already know

Start at 토속촌 삼계탕 (Tosokchon Samgyetang), about a 4-5 minute walk from Exit 2 of Gyeongbokgung Station on Line 3, and order the ₩20,000 (about $14.97) house bowl. The address is 서울 종로구 자하문로5길 5. It's been open since 1983 in the Seochon neighborhood, just west of Gyeongbokgung Palace, and is the address most people mean when they say "Seoul samgyetang".

It's open 10:00-22:00 every day, last order 21:00, with no closing day listed. The menu also runs to an ogolgye (black-chicken) version at ₩25,000 and a cultivated wild-ginseng bowl at ₩26,000, and a small glass of ginseng liquor comes with the meal.

Say "토속촌삼계탕 하나 주세요" (to-sok-chon-sam-gye-tang ha-na ju-se-yo, "one Tosokchon samgyetang, please"). This one earns its place on scale alone: it's rated 4.2 from 12,085 Google reviews, more than any other samgyetang address in the city carries.

🥢 Is Goryeo Samgyetang worth the walk from City Hall?

Yes, if the dish's own history matters to you: 고려삼계탕 (Goryeo Samgyetang), about a 2-3 minute walk from Exit 10 of City Hall Station, opened in 1960 as what a local heritage record calls Seoul's first restaurant built around this one dish alone. The address is 서울 중구 서소문로11길 1, and the phone is 02-752-9376.

What is sambok (삼복)?

The three hottest stretches of the Korean lunar calendar: chobok, jungbok and malbok, usually falling in July and August. Samgyetang is the dish most associated with these days, eaten on the logic of fighting heat with heat rather than cooling down.

It's open Monday to Friday 10:30-21:00 and weekends 10:30-20:30. The basic bowl is ₩20,000 (about $14.97), and versions with wild ginseng or abalone run ₩26,000.

Say "고려삼계탕 하나 주세요" (go-ryeo-sam-gye-tang ha-na ju-se-yo, "one Goryeo samgyetang, please"). Like Tosokchon, it lists ginseng liquor (인삼주) on the menu, so the two shops share that one ritual even if nothing else about the trip lines up.

Google Maps rating 4.2 from 2,541 reviews (checked 2026-09-30).

📺 Gangwonjeong: the bowl a food critic called his best

Finish in Yongsan at 강원정 (Gangwonjeong), about an 8-10 minute walk from Exit 1 of Namyeong Station on Line 1, where food critic Hwang Kyo-ik called this the best samgyetang he'd ever eaten on tvN's Wednesday Food Talk (수요미식회) in July 2015. eToday's report on the episode has him singling out the chicken's texture specifically, saying few places get it this right.

The shop opened in 1978 at 서울 용산구 원효로89길 13-10, an alley address without much foot traffic to spare it a TV-famous crowd. Namyeong Station's Exit 1 is the closer option; Hyochang Park Station's Exit 5 on Line 6 works too, about 10 minutes on foot either way.

It runs lunch 11:30-14:30 and dinner 17:30-20:30, with a break in between and the doors shut on the month's first and third Sundays. That break window is the one detail worth writing down before you go.

Featured on tvN's Wednesday Food Talk (수요미식회) in 2015.

TV exposure can mean a queue at peak meal times, so it's worth arriving outside the lunch and dinner rush.

Say "강원정 삼계탕 하나 주세요" (gang-won-jeong-sam-gye-tang ha-na ju-se-yo, "one Gangwonjeong samgyetang, please"). The bowl is ₩18,000 (about $13.47) now, up from the ₩12,000 the show reported back in 2015.

On paper Gangwonjeong looks open a full nine hours a day, but the 14:30-17:30 break locks the door for three of them, and it's the only one of the three that skips some Sundays entirely.

None of the three confirms card payment or an English menu in its listing, so screenshot the Korean name before you go and carry cash just in case.

My take A whole chicken sounds like too much food for one person, and it usually isn't: the meat falls off the bone by the time it reaches the table, and the rice inside does most of the filling work. Go to Tosokchon if you want the version every guidebook already knows, Goryeo if you'd rather eat where the dish was first sold as its own meal, and Gangwonjeong if a critic's word carries more weight with you than a crowd does. Just don't plan a Gangwonjeong lunch that spills past 14:30, or you'll be standing outside a locked door.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Both Tosokchon and Goryeo pour ginseng liquor with the meal, so budget a slower seat if you actually want to sit with it instead of rushing the bowl.
  • What locals argue about Whether ₩18,000-20,000 for one chicken is fair or just where dining-out prices have drifted. Gangwonjeong's bowl cost ₩12,000 when the show aired in 2015; it's up 50% since.
  • Watch for Gangwonjeong's break and its closed first and third Sundays. The other two don't take a day off at all.
FAQ
Is it worth driving instead of taking the subway?
Only for Seochon. Free parking covers a full sixty minutes at the pump, tight side street and all. The Yongsan kitchen has nothing of its own nearby, so leave the car behind and ride the rails for that stop.
Is one bowl enough for two people to share?
Order your own; splitting rarely works out. The Yongsan kitchen's listing specifies a 40-day-old bird, small enough that one diner clears it solo, bones included.

None of these three will win a design award, and that's sort of the point: a bowl of samgyetang is supposed to look like what your grandmother made, not a plating exercise. Start with Goryeo Samgyetang if the history interests you, and save Gangwonjeong for a day you've got room to work around its break.
