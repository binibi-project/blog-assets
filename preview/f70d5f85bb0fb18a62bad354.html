Deoksugung Palace: Seoul's Last ₩1,000 Royal Ticket

Deoksugung's ₩1,000 admission hasn't moved since 2005, and a fee review is due by January 2027. The guard-ceremony times, the City Hall Station exit, and what's still free.

Quick answer
  • ₩1,000 buys the whole palace: one ticket covers Daehanmun, the grounds, and Seokjojeon's history museum inside, but that price won't last much longer.
  • The ceremony runs three times a day: catch the guard change at 11:00, 14:00 or 15:30, Tuesday through Sunday, never on Monday.
  • Get the exit right: take City Hall Station's Line 1 Exit 2, and the ticket booth at Daehanmun is about 105 meters straight ahead.

Half past two on a weekday, and office workers on their lunch break stop mid-stride on the sidewalk outside Daehanmun. Drums start somewhere behind the gate, then a line of guards in gold and blue robes comes through carrying flags taller than they are, and for a few minutes the glass towers of Jung-gu's financial district disappear behind a Joseon-era changing of the guard. It's an odd, good kind of whiplash, and it happens on a schedule you can actually plan around.

Deoksugung rewards a slow look precisely because it's compact: a Western-style stone hall sitting a few steps from a Joseon throne room, a stone-wall lane locals still half-joke about, and a ticket price that has not moved since before most tourists reading this were old enough to travel alone.

👮 Is the guard ceremony worth timing your visit around?

Yes: it's free with admission, runs most days, and you can watch it from right outside the gate. The Deoksugung Guard Changing Ceremony takes place in front of Daehanmun three times daily, at 11:00, 14:00 and 15:30, Tuesday through Sunday, with a separate patrol procession once a day after the first ceremony. A 2025 event listing counts 78 performers taking part, starting once everyone is in place and a drummer in the trumpet corps signals the start.

What is Daehanmun (대한문)?

Deoksugung's main gate, and the stage for the guard ceremony. It was originally called Daeanmun, then renamed Daehanmun on April 25, 1906, becoming the palace's main gate.

The ceremony was suspended for seven months in 2020 during COVID-19 and picked the same three-times-a-day schedule back up once it resumed, which is the schedule still listed for recent event seasons. Monday is the one day you'll find the gate quiet: the palace runs 9:00 to 21:00 with last admission at 20:00 every other day of the week.

💰 What does admission actually cost, and for how long?

₩1,000 (about $0.75) for an adult ticket, a price that has held since 2005 and is now under official review. Deoksugung's ₩1,000 fee matches Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo, and the Joseon royal tombs charge the same too, while Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung both charge ₩3,000. Those rates have held since 2005, according to Herald Economy's report on Korea's heritage-site fees. The government plans to announce a new fee structure in November 2026, to take effect from January 1, 2027, though officials say the exact increase and any discount rules are still being worked out.

That ₩1,000 (about $0.75 at ~₩1,325/$1) looks almost like a rounding error next to other countries' palaces, and that gap is part of the case the government report makes for a review.

A government-commissioned survey of 6,635 people found 50% supported an admission increase versus 19% opposed, and respondents who wanted a change suggested an average fair price of about ₩9,203 for palace tickets, checked 2026-09-25.

On paper the combined ticket looks like the smart buy, but it only pays off if you're actually doing all five sites: one entry each, within six months. If Deoksugung is your only palace stop in Seoul, the single ₩1,000 ticket is simply cheaper.

🚶 How do you actually get there, and what's inside the gate?

Take City Hall Station's Line 1 Exit 2, walk about 105 meters, and Daehanmun's ticket booth is right in front of you. From the booth, it's another 25 meters through the gate to MMCA Deoksugung, a modern-art museum built into the palace's 1938 West Wing. Line 2 riders can use City Hall Station's Exit 12 instead.

What is Seokjojeon (석조전)?

A three-story neoclassical stone building designed in 1898 by British architect John Reginald Harding and completed in 1910 as a Western-style residence for Emperor Gojong, built right beside the palace's traditional wooden halls. It now holds the Daehan Empire Historical Museum, which general palace admission already covers. It's a separate building from MMCA Deoksugung next door, which charges its own ticket.

MMCA Deoksugung keeps its own hours and does not include palace admission: Tue/Thu/Fri/Sun 10:00-18:00, Wed/Sat 10:00-21:00 with free entry after 18:00, closed Mondays and January 1.

Right outside the palace's west wall runs Jeongdong-gil, a quiet, tree-lined lane that locals also call the stone-wall road. Walk it before or after the ceremony rather than rushing it. It's one of downtown Seoul's few genuinely quiet stretches.

What is the Deoksugung stone-wall road?

A quiet lane running alongside the palace's old stone outer wall, long popular with couples on dates. A well-known Seoul legend says couples who walk it together will soon break up, supposedly because a divorce court once stood nearby, in a building that's now the Seoul Museum of Art. Treat the origin story as folklore, not documented history.

If you've already done Gyeongbokgung, Deoksugung still earns the detour: it's a different, shorter visit, and the ₩1,000 ticket makes it an easy add-on rather than a second full afternoon. Deoksugung served as the main palace of the short-lived Korean Empire from 1897 to 1910, which is why a Joseon throne hall and a European stone palace sit inside the same low wall, with modern Seoul's office towers visible over the top of both. Budget an hour for the grounds and Seokjojeon, longer if you build your visit around a ceremony slot.

My take If you're deciding whether a smaller, cheaper palace is worth the detour on a short Seoul trip, in your shoes, yes: nowhere else in the city packs a Joseon gate, a European stone hall and a changing-of-the-guard ceremony into one ₩1,000 ticket. Build the stop around a ceremony time rather than wandering in cold, and don't let the combined ticket talk you into palaces you weren't going to visit anyway.

FAQ
What's Deoksugung's exact address for a map app?
99 Sejong-daero in the Jeongdong district of Jung-gu, postal code 04519, pinpoints the ticket booth on Naver Map or Google Maps.
Does the ₩6,000 combined ticket cover everything inside Deoksugung?
Not entirely. The pass excludes Changdeokgung's Huwon walk and Deoksugung's own museum ticket, both bought separately on arrival even though a standalone Deoksugung ticket already includes the museum.

For a palace this size, the ceremony and the gate fee are the two things worth building a schedule around; everything else inside is a bonus. Prices and hours were checked on 2026-10-26; they change, so double-check before you go.
