Seoraksan's Fall Peak Hits Oct 20: What It Costs to Go

Korea's 2026 foliage forecast puts Seoraksan's peak at October 20. The bus, the free park, the cable car, and one fare hike to plan around.

Quick answer
  • Seoraksan turns first: the Korea Forest Service puts this year's peak at October 20, about three weeks out from a late-September visit.
  • Getting in costs nothing: the park itself, and now even Sinheungsa Temple on the main trail, dropped their entrance fees years ago.
  • The bus is the expense that's about to move: book before October 1 if you can, because intercity fares are rising nationwide that day.

At dawn on a mid-October weekday, the first bus out of Dongseoul Terminal is already half full of hiking poles and empty thermoses. Somewhere past Hongcheon the hills start to catch fire at the edges, orange first, then a deep maple red climbing the ridgeline toward Sokcho. By the time the bus turns off the coastal highway, half the passengers have their phones against the window. This is the three-to-four-week stretch every other fall trip in Korea gets measured against.

If you're deciding whether to book this week or wait, the forecast and the fare calendar both point the same way: go sooner, and lock in the bus before it gets more expensive.

🍁 When exactly does Seoraksan turn this year?

Seoraksan is forecast to peak on October 20, roughly a week to ten days ahead of most of the country's other big color spots. The Korea Forest Service published its 2026 autumn color map on September 22, putting the mountain's peak (defined as the point where at least half its leaves have turned) about 0.8 days later than the five-year average. Color is already moving: the agency's monitoring station at Jombongsan in nearby Inje county logged the season's first turned leaves on September 14.

What does "peak" foliage actually mean?

The Korea Forest Service defines peak (절정) as the day at least half of a mountain's leaves have changed color, not the single most photogenic day. Color keeps building for a week or two beforehand and holds for a few days after, so arriving within a week of the forecast date still gets you a full mountain.

🚌 Getting from Seoul to Seoraksan

Take the intercity bus from Dongseoul Terminal. There still isn't a train that reaches Sokcho. Buses run the 151-km route about 33 times a day, first departure 06:05 and last at 21:00, with the fastest services taking around two hours and ten minutes, though some general-class runs stop more and take closer to three. Three fare tiers cover the same road:

These fares hold only through September. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport is raising the nationwide fare ceiling for both intercity and express buses by 9% starting October 1, 2026. Fares in KRW, USD at ₩1,358.4/$1, checked 2026-09-24.

What's the difference between "standard" and "premium" bus seats?

Korean intercity buses often sell three tiers on the same route: general (일반, three seats across), standard (우등, 2+1 recliners), and premium (프리미엄, wider single recliners). Only the seat and the legroom change. The road, the stops and the arrival point are the same.

From Sokcho's terminal, city bus 7 or 7-1 continues to the park entrance for Gangwon's flat city-bus fare of ₩1,700.

🎫 What a day at the park costs, and is the cable car worth it?

The park and the temple on the main path in are both free, but the cable car isn't, and its senior discount won't help you during foliage season. Seoraksan has charged no entrance fee since January 1, 2007, when Korea abolished admission at national parks nationwide, with only a handful of exceptions that don't include Seoraksan. Sinheungsa Temple, right on the route to Ulsanbawi and the cable car, used to charge its own cultural heritage viewing fee on top of that even after the park itself went free, until May 4, 2023, when the government began covering that fee for visitors instead [source:https://www.srtoday.co.kr/%EC%8B%A0%ED%9D%A5%EC%82%AC-%EB%B0%B1%EB%8B%B4%EC%82%AC%EB%93%B1-%EB%AC%B4%EB%A3%8C-%EC%9E%85%EC%9E%A5-%EC%A0%84%EA%B5%AD-65%EA%B0%9C-%EC%82%AC%EC%B0%B0-%EA%B4%80%EB%9E%8C%EB%A3%8C-%EB%A9%B4/).

What was the "cultural heritage viewing fee"?

For decades, temples holding a state-designated cultural asset, like Sinheungsa's bronze Buddha, charged their own entry fee separately from the park's, even after national park admission went free in 2007. Since May 2023 the government pays that fee on visitors' behalf at about 65 temples nationwide, Sinheungsa included, so walking through no longer costs anything [source:https://www.srtoday.co.kr/%EC%8B%A0%ED%9D%A5%EC%82%AC-%EB%B0%B1%EB%8B%B4%EC%82%AC%EB%93%B1-%EB%AC%B4%EB%A3%8C-%EC%9E%85%EC%9E%A5-%EC%A0%84%EA%B5%AD-65%EA%B0%9C-%EC%82%AC%EC%B0%B0-%EA%B4%80%EB%9E%8C%EB%A3%8C-%EB%A9%B4/).

The Seorak Cable Car up to Gwongeumseong sells round-trip tickets only, no one-way option, since there's no walking route down from the top: adults pay ₩16,000, children ₩12,000, and there's no online booking, only same-day counter sales. Its 65-and-over rate of ₩14,000 doesn't apply from July 18 to August 23 or from September 19 to November 15, 2026. That blackout window swallows almost the entire foliage season, Seoraksan's October 20 peak included, so most visitors this season pay full price no matter their age.

Skip the cable car and the alternative is the 707-step staircase up to Ulsanbawi itself, open to anyone willing to climb it rather than ride, with no fee either way. Weekday mornings before 9 a.m. are the one window locals say is worth the early bus. In Sokcho itself, a bowl of the city's famous sundubu (soft tofu stew) at a place like Chodang runs about ₩14,000.

My take I haven't done this hike myself. My east coast trips the past couple of years have stopped at Gangneung's coffee street, not the mountain behind it. If I were finally doing this, I'd take the earliest 06:05 bus out of Dongseoul, both to beat the cable car line and because the standard-class upgrade barely costs more than general seating for a two-hour ride. I'd also buy the ticket before October 1. A 9% jump on a ₩21,300 fare is real money for a trip I could otherwise fund with one bowl of sundubu in Sokcho.

Check before you go
  • Buy the bus ticket before Oct 1 if you can: intercity and express fares rise 9% nationwide that day.
  • Line up for the cable car in person: there's no online reservation, only same-day walk-up tickets.
  • Don't plan around the senior discount: it doesn't apply from Sep 19 through Nov 15, which covers the whole foliage season.
FAQ
Is Seoraksan free to enter?
Yes. The park has had no entrance fee since 2007, and Sinheungsa Temple on the main trail stopped charging its own fee in 2023.
Do I need to book the cable car in advance?
No, and you can't. Seorak Cable Car only sells same-day tickets at the counter, so budget extra time on weekends.

Three weeks of lead time before an official peak date is enough to book the better bus seat and still catch the color; waiting until the forecast date itself just means a longer line at the cable car counter. Book the early bus out of Dongseoul before October 1, and you'll beat both the fare hike and the crowd.
