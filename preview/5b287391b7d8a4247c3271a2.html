Incheon Airport to Seoul: AREX, Bus or Taxi, by the Won

AREX Express is ₩13,000 and 43 minutes from Terminal 1; the all-stop train is ₩4,750. Real fares for the train, bus and taxi, all sourced.

Quick answer
  • Fastest: the express train, ₩13,000 and a flat 43 minutes from Terminal 1 to Seoul Station, no stops.
  • Cheapest: the all-stop train, ₩4,750 with a T-money card, about an hour, 10 more stops along the way.
  • Before you pick: check which terminal you're landing at and whether you'll arrive before the last train.

You clear customs, the doors slide open, and Incheon's arrival hall hits you with cold air-conditioning and a wall of signs in four languages, all pointing somewhere. Somewhere in that crowd is a train platform, a bus stop and a taxi line, and every one of them will get you into Seoul. The question that actually matters isn't which is "best." It's which one fits the ninety minutes you have left before you'd rather be lying down.

What is AREX?

Airport Railroad Express, the train line connecting Incheon International Airport to Seoul Station. It runs two kinds of trains on the same track: a non-stop Express and a commuter-style All-Stop that calls at every station in between, including Gimpo Airport.

🚄 Is the ₩13,000 express train worth it over the ₩4,750 all-stop?

Yes, if your time is worth more than about ₩8,000 an hour to you. AREX's own fare page prices the Express train at ₩13,000 one way, non-stop, 43 minutes from Terminal 1 to Seoul Station and 51 minutes from Terminal 2. It has assigned seating, so you're not standing over a suitcase for the ride.

The All-Stop train covers the same route for ₩4,750 with a T-money or other transit card (₩4,850 for a single-use ticket, plus a refundable ₩500 deposit) from Terminal 1, and ₩5,350 from Terminal 2. It takes longer because it actually stops: 59 minutes from Terminal 1 and 66 from Terminal 2, sometimes a few minutes more depending on the run. For a solo traveler without much luggage, that extra 16 minutes for a ₩8,250 saving is an easy trade.

Trains run often on both lines, but they don't run all night. On the Express, Seoul Station's first train out toward the airport leaves at 06:10 and the last one leaves at 22:50; coming from the airport, the first is 05:15 and the last is 22:40. Land on a flight that clears customs after 11 p.m. and the train isn't an option at all, express or all-stop.

What's the difference between "직통" and "일반" on a Korean sign?

직통 (jik-tong) means non-stop, and 일반 (il-ban) means regular or all-stop. At Incheon, these are two different ticket counters for the same train line, so read the sign before you queue.

🚌 When does the limousine bus make more sense than the train?

When your hotel isn't near a subway line, or you'd rather sit with your bags than haul them through a station. Route 6009, one of the main limousine lines into Gangnam, charges ₩17,000 for an adult and ₩11,000 for a child, with the first bus leaving Terminal 1 at 05:20 and the last at 22:52. It picks you up curbside, drops you at a marked stop close to the door, and every seat is guaranteed since you buy a specific departure.

The trade-off is traffic. A bus can take well over an hour into Gangnam depending on the hour, longer than either train, because it's sharing the road with everyone else headed the same direction. If you land during evening rush hour, budget extra and don't book anything tight right after.

🚕 Is a taxi ever worth it?

Only for a group splitting the fare, heavy luggage, or a flight that lands after the last train and bus. A regular taxi's meter starts at ₩4,800, the same base whether it's a Seoul, Gyeonggi or Incheon-registered car; a deluxe or nine-seater taxi starts at ₩7,000. Incheon Airport's own taxi guide adds a metropolitan surcharge once the ride crosses outside the taxi's home zone: 20% for a Seoul or Gyeonggi-registered car, 30% for an Incheon-registered one, plus a separate night surcharge of 20-40% between 22:00 and 04:00.

None of that adds up to one clean number, since a meter runs on distance and time, not a flat rate. Several taxi-fare guides put a metered ride to Gangnam somewhere around ₩70,000-100,000, more if traffic is bad. Split four ways, that lands close to what one person pays for the limousine bus alone, which is the only situation where a taxi actually beats the bus on price.

What is an International Taxi?

A licensed airport taxi service (1644-2255) with English-speaking drivers, meant for visitors who don't want to explain an address in Korean. It's an official option at the taxi stand, not a private van company; skip anyone who approaches you inside the terminal offering a ride before you reach the stand.

My take I live in Gangnam, close to where the 6009 bus drops off, so when a friend who doesn't speak Korean is landing I point them at that bus over a taxi they'd have to explain an address to. If the flight gets in early enough for the trains, though, I'd take the all-stop over the express for anything short of a same-day work meeting; ₩8,250 buys a coffee and a rest at Seoul Station instead of racing for a connection.
Go deeper
  • If you have one more hour Skip the express and take the all-stop instead. The extra stops are also useful if your hotel sits along the line rather than right at Seoul Station.
  • What locals argue about Whether the limousine bus is worth ₩17,000 when the all-stop train covers the same distance for a third of the price. Frequent flyers tend to land on the bus anyway, for the seat and the curbside drop.
  • Watch for Your landing time against the last train (22:40-22:50 from the airport) and the last bus (22:52 from T1). Miss both and a taxi becomes the only option, not just the fastest one.
Check before you go
  • Confirm your terminal before choosing a train: Terminal 2 adds 8-15 minutes over Terminal 1 on both AREX lines.
  • Time your landing against 22:40: that's roughly when the last express train leaves the airport, and the all-stop and bus stop close to the same time.
  • Use the marked taxi stands only: Incheon Airport's own guide warns that unlicensed vans solicit riders inside the terminal at inflated prices.
FAQ
Is the AREX express train worth the extra money over the all-stop?
For most travelers, no. The all-stop train saves ₩8,250 for about 16 extra minutes, which is a good trade unless you're on a tight connection.
What's the cheapest way from Incheon to Gangnam specifically?
The AREX all-stop train to a connecting subway line, still under ₩6,000 total with a T-money card, though it means at least one transfer that the direct 6009 bus skips.

For a first trip with a manageable bag, the all-stop train is the one I'd default to, and save the express for the day you're cutting it close. Prices and hours were checked on 2026-09-25; they change, so double-check before you go.
